What to see in Castel San Pietro Romano

Borgo perched on Mount Ginestro above Palestrina, with the Rocca dei Colonna on the ancient acropolis, polygonal walls of the 6th century BC and views of the Sacco valley and Rome; here Luigi Comencini turned in 1953 "Pane," love and fantasy" .

Rocca Colonna

History and architecture · Castles and fortresses · Viewpoint

Built by the Colonna on the top of Mount Ginestro, it was destroyed in 1298, rebuilt and damaged again in 1436-37; with the restoration of Stefano Colonna in 1482 it lost its military function and became a food store of the community. Abandoned in the nineteenth century, it was recovered for public use at the beginning of the twentieth century; Jacopone was held by Todi and Corradino of Swabia. In winter it hosts an artistic nativity scene with life-size characters.

Acropoli e mura poligonali

History and architecture · Archaeology · Free entry · Viewpoint

Mount Ginestro was the seat of the acropolis and the cult of Jupiter Arcano; the belt in polygonal dry work of the VI century B.C. is part of a fortified system of about 4.5 km that encloses also the underlying Palestrina.

Chiesa di San Pietro Apostolo

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches · Free entry

It rises on the area of a Benedictine monastery, was rebuilt by the Barberini in the mid-17th century and assumed the present form with Clement XII around 1730-32. At the entrance there is the statue of St. Peter of Paolo Benaglia (about 1730), a holy waterworks made from an altar and a base of Roman statue, the altarpiece of Giacinto Gimignani of 1633 and, under the altar, the remains of Blessed Margaret Colonna.

Centro storico medievale

History and architecture · Villages · Free entry · Viewpoint

Alleys, cordonades and squares with views of the valley of Sacco and Rome; there you enter from two open Urbian gates in the walls. The village was set in 1953 to "Pane," love and fantasy" by Luigi Comencini, with Vittorio De Sica and Gina Lollobrigida.

Valle delle Cannuccete

Nature and landscape · Parks and nature reserves · Free entry

Natural monument of about twenty hectares just outside the center, with maples, ceres and ancient hornbeams protected for centuries to protect the springs; it preserves the remains of a pre-Roman aqueduct of the sixth century B.C., with letters of the Greek alphabet engraved on the walls of the duct.
