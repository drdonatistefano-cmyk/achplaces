What to see in Baranello

Molise country linked to the architect Giuseppe Barone, who in 1897 donated his collection of art and archaeology to the Municipality, today civic museum, and signed the fountain of Cerere and the enlargement of the church of the Rosary; on Mount Vairano, towards Busso, remain the walls of a city of the Sanniti Pentri.

Museo civico «Giuseppe Barone»

Museums · Old masters

On the second floor of the former Town Hall, renovated by Barone in the late nineteenth century with façade in Florentine style; the collection donated in 1897 is exhibited in two rooms with original cases: Neapolitan paintings (Luca Giordano, Francesco Solimena) and Flemish, an Egyptian canopy vase, over 120 Greek and Italian ceramics, Etruscan bronzes and jewels, porcelain of Capodimonte and Pescolanciano, Chinese, Japanese and Indian objects.

  • Where Via Santa Maria 13, Baranello
  • Entry To be checked
  • Suggested time 1-1.5 hours
  • Official website museodibaranello.it

Sources: museodibaranello.it · visitmolise.eu

Chiesa di San Michele Arcangelo

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches

The parish church was rebuilt after the earthquake of 1805 on a project by Berardino Musenga; Count Giuseppe Zurlo supported the works and donated a rich collection of paintings, much of the country's artistic heritage.

  • Where Baranello
  • Entry To be checked
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes
  • Tourist portal page www.visitmolise.eu

Sources: museodibaranello.it · visitmolise.eu

Chiesa della Beata Vergine del Rosario (San Biagio)

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches

Born as a kind chapel around 1500 on the remains of a church of the thirteenth century, a single nave with baroque stuccoes and oil paintings; in 1890 Giuseppe Barone designed its extension and the bell tower.

  • Where Piazza Santa Maria, Baranello
  • Entry To be checked
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes
  • Tourist portal page www.visitmolise.eu

Sources: museodibaranello.it · visitmolise.eu

Torre e centro storico

History and architecture · Squares and old town · Free entry · Viewpoint

At the highest point of the village the tower dating back to the ninth century, defensive and then part of a castle, with stretches of the ancient city walls around; in the center also the Zurlo palace, rebuilt after the earthquake of 1805 by Count Giuseppe Zurlo and his brother Biase.
