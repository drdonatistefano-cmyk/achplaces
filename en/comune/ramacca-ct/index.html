What to see in Ramacca

Center on the first hills west of the Piana di Catania, 270 meters, founded by the Marquis Ottavio Gravina to welcome those who had lost their home with the earthquake of 1693 and the flows of Etna; on the central square the Mother Church of the Nativity of Mary with the seventeenth-century bell tower, not far away the church of the Immaculate which houses the parish of the patron Saint Joseph (March 19).

Chiesa Madre Natività di Maria SS.

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches

It rises next to the central square of the village, founded in the early eighteenth century with the licentia populandi granted in 1688 by Charles II; the building was born from a warehouse of an ancient farm together with the pre-existing sixteenth-century church of the SS. Crucified. The façade is marked by two large sandstone pilasters, with a tympanum portal and a small rose window; the 17th century square bell tower in carved stone, originally in two orders, was raised with the civic clock. Inside, with a single nave, the ancient main altar with a tabernacle in polychrome marble and, in the niche above, the eighteenth-century wooden statue of the Madonna delle Grazie; altar and modern marble ambos are brothers Giacomo and Lucio Alberghina of Caltagirone.

Chiesa dell'Immacolata (parrocchia di San Giuseppe)

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches

Church with annexed convent, originally dedicated to the Sorrowful Virgin and passed in 1858 to the title of the Immaculate Virgin; from the decree of 8 December 1952 it houses the parish of St. Joseph, patron saint of the country. It has neoclassical façade and a single nave with stuccoes and altars, oil paintings of the Cross, of the Sorrows and of Capuchin saints and wooden statues including a San Luigi Gonzaga by Luigi Santifaller from Ortisei, a Christ in the column, a Sorrowful and a Crucifix. In the apse, renovated in 1977, a wooden statue of the Madonna; altar, ambo and baptismal font in marble are the Alberghina brothers of Caltagirone, the wooden tabernacle inlaid is nineteenth century.
