What to see in Valmontone

Country on a hill of tuff whose name, attested around 1100, indicates a valley dominated by a mountain: the heart is the "pamphilia" city wanted by Camillo Pamphilj, with the Palazzo Doria Pamphilj frescoed by Mattia Preti, Gaspard Dughet and the Burgundy and the Collegiate of Mattia de' Rossi, the only structures of the ancient nucleus survived the bombings of the Second World War, to which is added the Convent of Sant'Angelo on the homonymous hill.

Palazzo Doria Pamphilj e Museo

History and architecture · Palaces and villas · Free entry

In 1651 Camillo Pamphilj, Pope Innocent's nephew X, bought the manor and, demolished in 1652 the Sforza castle, started in 1654 the new palace (completed around 1670) on a project attributed to the Jesuit Benedetto Molli, then followed by Antonio Del Grande: 365 rooms, a facade with 7 windows like the days of the week and another with 12 months. On the noble floor, between 1657 and 1661, Pier Francesco Mola, Gaspard Dughet, Guillaume Courtois called the Burgundian, Francesco Cozza and Mattia Preti frescoed the four rooms of the Elements, the four chamber of the Continents, the Prince's Hall with the walls trompe-l'oeil and two chapels. The museum combines this artistic section with an archaeological one, with finds and plastics from the 4th century BC to the 4th century A.D. emerged from the excavations for the High Speed; free entrance, with route accompanied by the operators (reservation recommended on weekends); the municipality indicates Tuesday-Friday 9-13, Saturday and Sunday 9.30-13 and 16-20.

Collegiata di Santa Maria Maggiore (dell'Assunta)

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches · Free entry

Built between 1685 and 1689 for Prince Giovanni Battista Pamphilj on a project by Mattia de' Rossi, Bernini's favourite pupil, instead of a thirteenth-century Gothic church of which the ancient baptismal font remains; it was consecrated on 27 May 1703. The semicircular and colonnade façade is flanked by two bell towers and surmounted by a dome; the six side chapels preserve works by Agostino Scilla, Andrea Pozzo, Giacinto Brandi, Ciro Ferri and Pietro Locatelli, while the central cap has the altarpiece of the Assunta by Lorenzo Gramiccia. It preserves the wooden processional statue of the patron Saint Louis Gonzaga and the burial of Cardinal Oreste Giorgi (1856-1924).

Convento di Sant'Angelo

Places of worship · Abbeys and monasteries · Free entry

On the hill, perhaps already home to a temple dedicated to Mercury, the Benedictines of Subiaco founded in the eighth century the church and monastery of San Zosimo; in the first half of the thirteenth century the complex passed to the Friars Minor, who placed it under the protection of San Michele Arcangelo, from which the name of the hill. It was the destination of pilgrimages as an Anthony's sanctuary and there is a finger of Saint Anthony of Padua; in the cloister there is a stationary marble cross carved on the back of a sacrificial stone.

  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes

Gnocchi, cacio e sagre di Valmontone

Local life and traditions · Food and wine · Free entry

The local cuisine offers the gnocchi di tritello, a peasant dish with tritello and flour, eggs, tomato, garlic, anchovies, parsley, oil and nuts, and the long and thin gnocchi "a co' de soreca" of the Prenestini Mountains, celebrated in mid-June; in the pastures between Cave, Genazzano and Valmontone the Cacio di Genazzano is born, pecorino with raw milk Presidio Slow Food. At the beginning of June the Wineries are held in Varmondò, with food and music, and at the end of June the Snail Festival in the district of San Giovanni.

  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes
