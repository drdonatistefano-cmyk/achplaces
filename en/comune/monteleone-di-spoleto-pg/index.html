What to see in Monteleone di Spoleto

A mountain village in Valnerina with four medieval gates and three walls, the Clock Tower and the San Francesco complex, under which is displayed the copy of the Etruscan Biga of the 6th century BC found in 1902 at the Colle del Capitano and today at the Metropolitan Museum in New York.

Complesso di San Francesco

Places of worship · Monumental complexes

It rises on a Benedictine oratory of the 12th century, it is dedicated to Santa Maria Assunta but known as San Francesco for the friars who arrived around 1280; the Romanesque-Gothic ogival portal has three orders of tortile columns with reliefs attributed to Lombard workers. Inside two aisles, wooden ceiling painted by Giuseppe Frigerio da Norcia (1760), frescoes from the 14th to the 16th century and a longobard aquasantiera; in the lower church Franciscan frescoes of the early 15th century, in the 18th century lunettes cloister with the life of St. Francis and an antiquarium of Roman and medieval epigraphs and sculptures.

Museo della Biga (copia della Biga di Monteleone)

Museums · Archaeological

In the premises under the complex of San Francesco is exposed the life-size copy, made by the School of Art of Manzù after the Year of the Etruscans (1985), of the parade biga of the middle of the 6th century BC in walnut wood covered with golden bronze with scenes of the life of Achilles. The original, found by chance in 1902 in a mound of the Colle del Capitano, was purchased in 1903 by the Metropolitan Museum of New York; two rooms document discovery, fugitive activity and funeral equipment, with an archaeological map of the territory.

Torre dell'Orologio

History and architecture · Towers · Free entry · Viewpoint

A tower of the 13th century, one of the three gates of the second circle of walls: it marks the entrance to the medieval castle and gives access to St. Francis and the oldest part of the center. From the top, above Piazza del Mercato, the view of the valley opens.

Centro storico, porte e mura, Palazzo Bernabò e San Nicola

History and architecture · Villages · Free entry

The ancient village preserves intact four medieval doors and three concentric rings of walls largely visible. There are the fifteenth-century Palazzo Bernabò, two-storey with central loggia on the roof, and the church of San Nicola, with altarpieces attributed to Ghezzi and Masucci.

  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes
  • Tourist portal page www.italia.it

Ex convento e chiesa di Santa Caterina

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches · Free entry

In 1310 ten Augustinian nuns gathered there to follow the rule of Chiara da Norcia, next to the chapel of Sant'Agnese; damaged by the earthquake of 1703 and reopened in 1715, the church was confiscated with the suppressions of 1866. It has a singular eggplant generated by four braided equilateral triangles.

Necropoli del Colle del Capitano

History and architecture · Archaeology

Large necropolis about 3 km north-east of the village, in use from the Bronze Age to the sixth century BC, with at least 44 graves incinerated; discovered in the early twentieth century, returned in 1902 the Biga from the great princely mound.
