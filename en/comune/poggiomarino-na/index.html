What to see in Poggiomarino

Municipality of the upper Sarno valley, about 10 km from Pompeii, known for the protohistoric village of Longola, river settlement on artificial islets discovered in 2000; in the village remain palaces of the late nineteenth century and the characteristic rural houses with dome called "vesuvian trullos."

Parco archeologico di Longola

History and architecture · Archaeology

Discovered by chance in November 2000 during the work for a sarno purifier, it was a river settlement on artificial islets separated by canals, inhabited by the middle age of the Bronze Age to the sixth century B.C., with huts of perishable materials and workshops that worked metal, bone, glass paste and amber; there were found a landing place and three pirogues. The humid environment has conserved many perishable materials; the site is managed by the Municipality under the Archaeological Park of Pompeii, with educational room, captions, marked routes and cafeteria.

According to the MiC closed from 1 November to 28 February except events and holidays; the Park of Pompeii does not indicate ordinary times and announces only extraordinary openings: check on longola.it or at 081 8658111

Palazzo Carotenuto e Palazzo Giugliano

History and architecture · Palaces and villas

Palazzo Carotenuto, from the late nineteenth century, was built by Don Ettore d'Alessio, who administered the goods of the princes of Marinis: it is a solid three-storey building with the facade divided into two symmetrical parts by a door, leading to the internal stairs and a back courtyard overlooking a large garden. Palazzo Giugliano also has a symmetrical façade, with two semicircular wings that seem to embrace the large square in front.

Trulli vesuviani

Local life and traditions · Hidden corners

These are the typical rural houses of Poggiomarino: cubic plant, often only one room and dome roof, built in compact and waterproof Vesuvius stone. The dome caused the water or lapilli to flow in case of eruption and also served to dry the products of the countryside; the walls, solid and rough on the outside, look like bulwarks of rock.
