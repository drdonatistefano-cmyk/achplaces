What to see in San Bartolomeo in Galdo

Country of the Fortore on a hill of olive groves and orchards overlooking the valley of the river, founded in the fourteenth century by the abbot of the abbey of Santa Maria a Mazzocca: it dominates the bell tower of the Mother Church, which was in fact a cathedral of the bishops of Volturara, next to the seventeenth-century convent of Santa Maria degli Angeli and the palaces of the historic center.

Chiesa Madre di San Bartolomeo Apostolo

Places of worship · Cathedrals · Free entry

Erected in 1330 by the abbot Nicola da Ferrazzano on a chapel of 1277, from 1332 she was a cathedral for the bishops of Volturara; renewed, was consecrated on July 8, 1703 by Cardinal Vincenzo Maria Orsini, then Pope Benedict XIII. It has a Latin cross plan with three naves, dome with yellow and green majolica, bell tower of about 35 meters symbol of the village, late dogotic rosette with the Carafa coat of arms and two portals of the fifteenth century from the abbey of Mazzocca; the bronze door of Valeria Sicily (2009) has 24 tiles on the patron saints. It preserves the reliquary bust of St. Bartholomew of the Neapolitan goldsmith Filippo Del Giudice (1767), the main altar in polychrome marble of the early eighteenth century, a stone tabernacle of 1541 and, in the treasury, silver and a mitria of the seventeenth century.

Chiesa dell'Annunziata e orologio «alla romana»

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches · Free entry

Church with portal of 1498, next to the Cathedral, with a clock \'alla romana' of the seventeenth century with a single hand and numbering at six hours; between the portals of the two churches the old bishop's palace, now a private residence, with a Latin inscription, opens in arch.

closed to cult for damage of the earthquake of 1962 (Common)

Centro storico, Piazza Garibaldi e palazzi

History and architecture · Squares and old town · Free entry · Viewpoint

The historic centre preserves the Renaissance Palazzo Martini, which belonged to the Jesuits and then to Monsignor Gürtler, with frescoed rooms; on Corso Roma the façade of Palazzo Rosa with the dial of a 24-hour mechanical watch; Piazza Garibaldi, formerly Piazza San Vito, was created in 1780-1791 during the episcopate of Gürtler. The village overlooks the Fortore valley, whose waters form the dam of Occhito.
