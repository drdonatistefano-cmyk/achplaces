What to see in Porano

Village on the plateau south of Orvieto with the fourteenth-century church of San Biagio and the historical park of Villa Paolina; in the territory the Tomb of the Hescanas, the only Etruscan sepulchre of Umbria with the original paintings on the walls, and the thirteenth-century castle of Castel Rubello.

Tomba degli Hescanas

History and architecture · Archaeology

Etruscan tomb of the end of the fourth century BC dug into the tuff of a wooded hill on the Montacchione stream, belonged to the aristocratic family of the Hescanas: dromos of about 16 meters, vestibule with two doors, double sloping ceiling and counter with two sarcophagi in pepper. The walls preserve a funeral banquet, a procession and winged spirit figures; it is indicated as the only Etruscan tomb of Umbria with the original paintings.

Villa Paolina e parco

History and architecture · Palaces and villas

18th century villa with avenues and gardens of the 17th and 18th centuries, among the most important historical parks in the region; it houses the Institute of Agrosilviculture of CNR and, in the former lemon house, the PAAO Visitor Centre (CEA) with the faithful replica of the paintings of the Tomb of the Hescanas. The park welcomes horse riding, dog shows and the national juggling rally every year.

Chiesa di San Biagio

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches · Free entry

The fourteenth-century church of the historic centre, already mentioned in the 13th century in the Ratio Decimarum; it contains two frescoes from the fourteenth century of the Orvieto school, a saint's waterworks from 1680 and, in the sacristy, a fifteenth-century cross.

  • Where Centro storico, Porano
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes
  • Tourist portal page www.umbriatourism.it

Sources: umbriatourism.it

Castel Rubello

History and architecture · Castles and fortresses · Viewpoint

13th century castle on a hill at 430 meters, in good condition, formed by two complexes: houses leaning against a church, defended by a rocky bastion and a large square tower, and a residential building with fireplace and frescoes of Lombardelli around a courtyard. Contested in the fourteenth century between Malcorini and Muffati and in the fifteenth century between Ladislao d'Angiò and Orvieto, he passed to the Valenti, the Avveduti and in 1816 to the Society of Jesus.
