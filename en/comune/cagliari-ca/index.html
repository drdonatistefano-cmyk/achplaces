What to see in Cagliari

The Sardinian capital on the seven hills overlooking the Gulf of the Angels: the fortified district of Castello with Pisan towers and bastions, the Roman amphitheatre, the Punic necropolis of Tuvixeddu, the Poetto and the flamingos of Molentargius.

36 places to visit in Cagliari, in the province of Cagliari (Sardinia). Every entry is checked against official sources.

Quartiere di Castello

History and architecture · Squares and old town · Free entry · Viewpoint

"Casteddu," the Pisan-fortified neighborhood on a limestone hill overlooking the sea: narrow streets remained almost unchanged, artisan workshops, churches, historical buildings, towers and bastions with 360 degrees views of the city.

  • Where Castello, Cagliari
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes
  • Tourist portal page www.italia.it

Sources: italia.it

Bastione di Saint Remy

History and architecture · Monuments and memorials · Free entry · Viewpoint

Built between 1899 and 1902 on the Spanish ramparts of the Sperone and the Mint, with the large terrace Umberto I and the highest one of Santa Caterina: you walk up from Piazza Constituzione or elevator, with views of Marina, Stampace, port and Poetto; on Sunday morning market of handicrafts and collecting.

  • Where Piazza Costituzione, Cagliari

  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes
  • Tourist portal page www.italia.it

Sources: italia.it

Torre di San Pancrazio

History and architecture · Towers · Viewpoint

The highest point of Cagliari: 37 meters tower on a terrace 130 meters above sea, built by the Pisans at the beginning of the 14th century and used in the 19th century by Alberto Della Marmora to draw the map of Sardinia.

Anfiteatro romano

History and architecture · Archaeology · Paid entry

Built between the end I and the beginning II century A.D. in a basin at the foot of the hill of Buon Camino, partly dug into the rock: up to ten thousand spectators, a facade over twenty meters high and an underground corridor of about 95 meters towards a cistern; rediscovered in the nineteenth century by the canon Giovanni Spano.

  • Where Via Sant'Ignazio da Laconi, Cagliari
