Castello Ruspoli-Candriano

History and architecture · Castles and fortresses · Viewpoint

In the highest point of the medieval village, built in the Lombard age between the IX and XI century and rebuilt in the Norman-Sveva age; after the earthquake of 1466 he received two round towers with shoes that incorporate the most ancient square towers. The cylindrical tower, built by the Saracene feudal lords, is quoted from 1151. In the seventeenth century the Caracciolos transformed him into a residence; the name comes from the marriage of Laura Caracciolo with Emanuele Ruspoli, a family that held him until 1959, when Terry Margherita Blanc, widow of Camillo Ruspoli, donated him to the municipality. Rebuilt after the 1980 earthquake, it now houses municipal offices, the civic museum, the Pro Loco and the Museo del cinema dell'Associazione Sergio Leone, dedicated to film of the director (1929-1989).

Museo civico «Turella Parva Turris»

Museums · Archaeological

In the rooms of the second level of the castle, connected to the path of the noble palace, he exhibits in five rooms and twelve windows a sample of the about 30,000 excavations carried out in the castle between 1993 and 1997 by Marcello Rotili: painted ceramics and protomaiolica (XIII-XIV century), graffite (XIV-XV), cooking vases, majolica in blue, and six-seventh-century dishes with the coat of arms of the Caracciolo. The route passes through the former hanging garden, which gives access to the defenses and tower.

Visitable by reservation (Pro Loco, tel. 0827 44060 / 0827 44147)

Torre normanna di Girifalco

History and architecture · Towers · Free entry · Viewpoint

Square tower with thick walls, from the 12th century, on the hill of Girifalco in a wood of centuries old cerres, with the characteristics of the first Norman military architecture. Fontana monumentale

Local life and traditions · Hidden corners · Free entry

Hydraulic work of the middle of the nineteenth century in the lower part of the village, near the castle, built to bring to the village the water of the nearby springs; it became the symbol of Torella.
