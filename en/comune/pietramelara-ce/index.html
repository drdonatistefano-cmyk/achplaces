What to see in Pietramelara

Medieval village at the foot of Monte Maggiore, mentioned already in 928 and plundered in March 1496 by Aragonese troops: the town with a radiocentric plan rises between arches and steps to the Lombard tower, while in the village you can visit the church of the Annunziata, the church of San Rocco and the small Museum of Sacred Art.

Borgo medievale e torre

History and architecture · Villages · Free entry · Viewpoint

First mentioned in 928 in an act of the Longobard princes Landolfo and Atenolfo, it has a radiocentric plan with two concentric roads, alleys to steps and medieval houses dug into the rock; the belt had twelve towers, of which few traces remain. You enter from a large arch with two frescoes of Christ and Our Lady and climb up to the tower at the top: 15.40 meters, square plan of 9 meters, walls 2.20 meters thick, damaged in 1496, dovecote in the nineteenth century and lowered in 1965, with the terrace today belvedere. In 1996, a bas-relief recalled the 500 years of reconstruction after the looting.

Chiesa dell'Annunziata

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches

It dates back to the early sixteenth century and is a national monument; it preserves an altar in onyx of Mount Maggiore.

Chiesa arcipretale di San Rocco

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches

It rises on a chapel of San Leonardo documented since 1308; it dates back to the sixteenth century and was entirely rebuilt after the collapse of 1889. It is known for the wooden portal entirely carved by hand.

Museo di arte sacra

Museums · Themed

A small museum between the church and the municipality (former convent of the Augustinians next to the parish church of Sant'Agostino, renovated in the eighteenth century): it exhibits paintings, sacred objects and vestments of the church's treasure.
