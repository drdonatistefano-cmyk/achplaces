What to see in Giove

Medieval village on the Tiber Valley, dominated by the Ducal Palace of Mattei with its 365 windows; in the center the chapel of San Rocco and the 18th century parish church, and the Walk of San Valentino towards the ancient Roman river port.

Palazzo Ducale (Castello di Giove)

History and architecture · Castles and fortresses · Viewpoint

The castle of Jugo is documented since 1191; in 1503, Cesare Borgia had its defenses dismantled and the sixteenth-century palace, initiated by the Farnese, was completed by Mattei, who bought it in 1597 and became dukes by the will of Urban VIII. It has five floors, corner towers, 365 windows and a helical ramp that can be crossed by carriages going up to a loggia on the Tiber Valley; the halls have mythological ceilings attributed to the schools of Domenichino, Veronese and Orazio Alfani.

Cappella di San Rocco

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches

Wanted by the Farnese in the sixteenth century as a former vote against the plague; inside votive paintings attributed to the brothers Lorenzo and Bartolomeo Torresani and a sixteenth-century Crucifixion of Folignate school with Jerusalem in the background. Since 1969 it has been a shrine of the fallen of all wars.

Chiesa parrocchiale di Santa Maria Assunta

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches · Free entry

Rococo church of 1740 with late Baroque façade and two symmetrical bell towers, with a Greek cross plan; it houses an Assumption attributed to Niccolò di Liberatore called the pupil or to his school and the "Madonna del Perugino" commissioned in the seventeenth century by the Perugino Francesco Caffarelli.

Cammino di San Valentino

Nature and landscape · Lakes and rivers · Free entry

A trail of about 8 km opened in 2019 that descends from the old town towards Attigliano and the site of an ancient Roman river port on the Tiber, where up to the nineteenth century stood a church of San Valentino.
