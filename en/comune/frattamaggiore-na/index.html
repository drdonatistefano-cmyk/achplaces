What to see in Frattamaggiore

City of hemp and ropes founded according to tradition in 850 by refugees of Miseno, Frattamaggiore has its heart in the Basilica of San Sossio, a national monument with Romanesque crypt that preserves the relics of Saints Sossio and Severino and the Sansossian Museum of sacred art.

Basilica di San Sossio Levita e Martire

Places of worship · Basilicas · Free entry

The main place of worship of the city, formerly Santa Maria degli Angeli and San Sossio, dates back to the 10th century in Romanesque style and the tradition ties it to the Misenatas who escaped the destruction of Miseno by the Saracens. It preserves the remains of Saint Sossius, patron saint of the city, who arrived in 1807 from the ruins of the church of Miseno, and those of Saint Severino, patron saint of Austria and Bavaria; it has Romanesque crypt, Gothic naves, Renaissance portal and Baroque chapels. It's a national monument.

  • Where Piazza Umberto I 1, Frattamaggiore
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes
  • Tourist portal page www.comune.frattamaggiore.na.it

Sources: comune.frattamaggiore.na.it · napoliforme.comune.napoli.it

Museo sansossiano di arte sacra

Museums · Old masters · Free entry

Established on 26 November 1996 in the Romanesque crypt of the basilica, it exhibits about one hundred goods on 251 sqm: marble fragments of three eighteenth-century altars, tombstones from the fifteenth to the nineteenth century including the slab of a togato knight from the sixteenth century, nineteenth-century riggioles, silver sacred from the seventeenth to the twentieth century, reliquary statues, fragments of sixteenth-century tables saved from the fire of 1945 and the altar candlesticks of Aniello Treglia (1704); on the second floor bell liturgical vestments of the eighteenth-nineteenth century. In 2016, a gallery was added in a building next to the basilica. The municipality indicates free entrance.

discordant times: the MiC card indicates closed every day, NapoliForMe indicates 9-18.30 every day; the municipality does not publish timetables (parrocchia@sansossio.it, tel. 081 8352792)

Santuario dell'Immacolata Concezione

Places of worship · Sanctuaries · Free entry

Built between 1855 and 1866, after the proclamation of the dogma of the Immaculate, on a project by engineer Gregorio Micillo, in place of the oldest church of the Immaculate and the Angel; in 1919 it was added to the Vatican Basilica. It has a large nave with four chapels on each side, frescoed and decorated with stucco by Cozzolino and Serino, altar in polychrome marble and wooden choir; the two-order façade has portal between Doric columns and triangular tympanum.

  • Where Via Roma 7, Frattamaggiore
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes
  • Tourist portal page www.comune.frattamaggiore.na.it

Sources: comune.frattamaggiore.na.it

Chiesa di San Rocco

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches · Free entry

Church of 1910 was created to replace an ancient medieval chapel of Santa Giuliana, the city's compatron, along the path of the aqueduct from Arcopinto to Atella; it is linked to the funaries of Piazza Miseno. The circular plant recalls the Pantheon of Rome and the basilica of St. Francis of Paola in Naples.

  • Where Via Don Minzoni 66, Frattamaggiore
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes
  • Tourist portal page www.comune.frattamaggiore.na.it

Sources: comune.frattamaggiore.na.it

Centro storico

History and architecture · Squares and old town · Free entry

The ancient center preserves palaces of the Aragonese and Spanish era with portals in piperno and travertine and baroque masks; among the ancient places the Municipality remembers the castle of via Castello, today via Genoino, and the district of via Miseno, linked to the tradition of the funnels.

  • Where Piazza Umberto I, Frattamaggiore
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes
  • Tourist portal page www.comune.frattamaggiore.na.it

Sources: comune.frattamaggiore.na.it
