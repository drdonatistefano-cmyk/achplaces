What to see in Santo Stefano di Sessanio

Medieval village in white limestone in the Gran Sasso and Monti della Laga National Park, marked by the Medici's domination in the sixteenth century: the Medici Tower rebuilt after the earthquake in 2009, the door with the Florentine coat of arms, the churches of Santo Stefano Protomartire and Santa Maria in Ruvo and, just outside, the Madonna del Lago.

Centro storico e Porta medicea

History and architecture · Villages · Free entry · Viewpoint

The village is entirely made of white limestone, without real walls: a continuous ring of houses with a few small windows was a dwelling and a defence. Straight and winding streets go up to the tower, through arcades, arched portals, stone windows, mullioned windows and Medici balconies; the House of the Captain is of the fifteenth century. The southeast gate keeps the Medici coat of arms.

Torre medicea

History and architecture · Towers · Viewpoint

Cylinder watchtower of the fourteenth century, crowned by blackbirds on thick catchels; the name comes from the coat of arms of the Medici, lords of the village in the sixteenth century. It collapsed almost entirely with the earthquake of 2009, it was rebuilt with the original material recovered (restored completed in 2021) and inside the walls hosts an exhibition. From the top you can see the valleys of the Tirino and the Aterno up to the Sirente and Maiella.

Chiesa di Santo Stefano Protomartire

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches

Built between the fourteenth and fifteenth centuries, it has only one nave in five spans, an unusual presbytery open on chapels and a semicircular apse. The patron saints Stefano protomartyre and Saint Isidoro are celebrated on 2-3 August.
