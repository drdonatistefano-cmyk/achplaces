What to see in Trinitapoli

Trinitapoli is just outside the village the Ipogei Archaeological Park, one of the most important necropolis of the Bronze Age in Italy, and a civic museum with bronzes, avori and the Trinitapoli Table. The historic centre is made of white houses, alleys and churches such as the Mother Church of St. Stephen.

Parco archeologico degli Ipogei

History and architecture · Archaeology

Sanctuary of the Bronze Age of about 7 hectares with 15 hypogeans, where more than 3000 years ago they practiced rituals of fertility, death and rebirth; later the rooms were re-used as tombs of the warrior elite. The Ipogeum of the Bronzes (discovered in 1987) and that of the Avori (2001) can be reached from a ramp and a corridor that leads to a room with a dome vault. There are about 200 burials: jewels in the female tombs, swords, daggers and arrowheads in the male ones, in addition to the burial called "Lady of the Ambres." Guided tours, including evenings, and experimental archaeology workshops are provided.

Museo civico

Museums · Archaeological

It preserves the Latin epigraph of the Table of Trinitapoli, bronzes, avori, remains of ritual sacrifices and finds of underwater archaeology. The Superintendence reports at the park an archaeological museum of the Ipogei with finds of more than 30 years of excavations.

Centro storico e chiese

History and architecture · Squares and old town · Free entry

The historic centre is made of white houses and a dense network of alleys. Among the buildings of interest are the Sanctuary of Our Lady of Loreto, the Church of St. Joseph, the Mother Church dedicated to the patron Saint Stephen and the church of St. Anne, which preserves traces of the Knights of Malta.
