What to see in Porticoes

City between the Vesuvius and the Gulf of Naples dominated by the Reggia commissioned by Carlo di Borbone in 1738, today home of the Department of Agriculture of the University Federico II, with the large park, the botanical garden and the museum center MUSA.

Reggia di Portici

History and architecture · Palaces and villas

Built from 1738 to 1742 at the behest of Carlo di Borbone as a summer residence, attracted by the excavations of Ercolano, on a project by Giovanni Antonio Medrano and Antonio Canevari, with interventions by Vanvitelli and Fuga; the main staircase was frescoed between 1743 and 1746. Sold in 1871 to the Province of Naples, since 1872 it hosts the Scuola Superiore di Agricoltura, today the Department of Agriculture of Federico II.

Parco della Reggia di Portici

Nature and landscape · City parks

The garden, designed by Francesco Geri, includes the Lower Bosco and the Upper Bosco, with the Boschetto, the Pallone Game, the Castle with observatory tower, the Real Pagliaia and the Vaccheria; in 1814-1815 an English garden with artificial pond was created. The Gallopatoio, a rare covered riding stable, was restored in 2021.

Centro museale MUSA e Orto botanico

Museums · Science and university

The museum center Museums of Agricultural Sciences, established in 2011 in the Reggia, brings together the Botanical Garden, founded in 1872 and among the oldest in Italy, the Herculanense Museum, reopened in a multimedia way in 2009, and other scientific museums.
