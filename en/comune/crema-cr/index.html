What to see in Crema

Città del Cremasco with a historic centre enclosed by the Venetian walls (1488-1508): the heart is Piazza Duomo, with the Gothic-Lombard cathedral, the Torrazzo and the Renaissance palaces; outside the walls stands the basilica of Santa Maria della Croce, masterpiece of the Lombard Renaissance, and in the former convent of Sant'Agostino the Civic Museum is located.

Duomo di Crema

Places of worship · Cathedrals

Dedicated to Santa Maria Assunta, the Gothic-Lombard cathedral with Cistercian influences rises in place of the one destroyed by Barbarossa in 1160 and was built between 1185 and 1341; in the eighteenth century it was altered in Baroque style, and the façade, slender and closed by a gallery, was restored between 1913 and 1916. The portal has a bezel perhaps of the previous cathedral and statues of the Madonna with Child and two saints; the interior with three naves of five spans has a marble rosette with 16 petals, works attributed to Vincenzo Cremasco and, as a true heart, a wooden crucifix of the thirteenth century. The crypt preserves ancient vestiges, Gothic sculptures and tunnels.

Campanile del Duomo

History and architecture · Towers · Viewpoint

Built between the end of the 13th and the beginning of the 14th century above the apse of the southern nave, of Romanesque phase, it was born with military functions and then served to call the faithful and citizens; it is 59,66 meters high and the mechanical clock of Domenico Crespi (1751) was restored and put into operation in 2023. From the top you can enjoy the view of Cream and surroundings; ascend on Saturday, Sunday and holidays 10-12.30 and 14-19, closed from 24 to 26 December.

Piazza Duomo e palazzi civici

History and architecture · Squares and old town · Free entry

Heart of the city, with the cathedral, Renaissance buildings and shop houses; the southern porch is 1474. On the side of the Palazzo Pretorio a municipal palace is already attested in 1211; Torrazzo, Palazzo Comunale and Palazzo Pretorio were rebuilt between 1525 and 1555, while the Palazzo della Notaria (1548-49) was donated to the future diocese as a bishop's residence.

Torrazzo

History and architecture · Monuments and memorials · Free entry

Despite its name it is not a tower but a monumental door between Piazza Duomo and Via XX Settembre, built in the early sixteenth century Lombardy instead of a more modest entrance of the citadel. On the side of the square has the statues of the Saints Pantaleone and Vittoriano and the coat of arms of Crema; towards Via XX Settembre the Lion of San Marco of 1525, scarred by the French in 1797; the clock is of 1555 and the bell of the lantern announced from 1594 the meetings of the Council. In 2016 there was a scene of "Call me by your name" .

Basilica di Santa Maria della Croce

Places of worship · Sanctuaries

He rose after the Marian apparition to Catherine of the Uberts on April 3, 1490: the first stone was laid on August 6, 1490 and the works were closed in 1500, on a project by Giovanni Battagio, pupil of Bramante; in 1630 it was used as a lazzaretto. The central plan in terracotta has three overlapping galleries and chapels towards the four cardinal points; the frescoes, Renaissance at the bottom and Baroque at the top, with the Triumph of the Cross in the eight-sliced dome, are the main artists of Crema and Cremona, including Benedetto Diana, Antonio and Bernardino Campi, Carlo Urbino and Giacomo Parravicino. Open every day from 7 to 19.

Museo civico di Crema e del Cremasco

Museums · City history · Free entry

In the Renaissance monastery of Sant'Agostino (1439-66), the Cremasco recounts from prehistoric times to today with archaeological finds and documents, the reconstruction of rural life of the late nineteenth century and the industrial past; it has a painting gallery from the seventeenth century to today, a large collection of works by the architect and stage designer Luigi Manini and one of the most important Italian collections of pirogues, found in Adda, Oglio and Po. In the ancient refectory, frescoes of the Crucifixion and the Last Supper by Giovan Pietro da Cemmo.

Museo d'arte organaria

Museums · Themed · Free entry

Two rooms of the Civic Museum dedicated to the Cremasca organarian tradition, which in the first half of the twentieth century reached international fame with the shops Lingiardi, Franceschini, Inzoli and Tamburini, exporting to Great Britain, America and Japan. The first room has instruments, rods and protagonists, with a section on Pacific Inzoli and a multimedia instrument similar to a double keyboard organ; the second is a laboratory on the construction phases, from the rods to the finished instrument.

Mura venete e porte

History and architecture · Castles and fortresses · Free entry

After the medieval walls, rebuilt from 1185 and completed in 1199 with 21 towers, four towers and the gates of Ombriano, Pianengo, Serio and Ripalta, Venice surrounded the city with new walls between 1488 and 1508, of which remain significant vestiges around the historic center. Porta Ombriano and Porta Serio were rebuilt with ornamental function between 1804 and 1807.

Alberi monumentali di Porta Serio e del centro

Nature and landscape · City parks · Free entry

In the public park in front of Porta Serio, east of the historic centre, there are raised bald cypresses (Taxodium distichum) almost 30 meters tall, indicated as the most fascinating monumental trees of Cremasco; in Piazzale delle Rimembranze two symmetrical ginkgo bilobas on the sides of the fountain accompany towards the center, and between the cobbled streets a cedar of Lebanon pops up for almost two centuries beyond the wall of Palazzo Premoli.
