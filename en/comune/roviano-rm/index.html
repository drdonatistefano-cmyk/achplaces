What to see in Roviano

Medieval village of the Valle dell'Aniene, on a hill of about 500 meters between the Lucretile Mountains and the Simbruini, about 55 km from Rome; in the Castello Brancaccio, born around the Mille, is located the Museum of Rural Civilization Valle dell'Aniene, among the first demoetnoanthropological museums of Lazio, and in the territory remain polygonal walls, the Roman aqueduct and traces of the Via Valeria.

Castello Brancaccio

History and architecture · Castles and fortresses

Built around the year 1000, repeatedly pillaged and destroyed, it was expanded and rebuilt by Colonna, Barberini Colonna di Sciarra, Massimo and Brancaccio. In the ancient chapel there is the cycle of Renaissance frescoes of ...Giuditta and Holoferne.Today it houses the Museum of Rural Civilization.

  • Entry To be checked
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes

Museo della Civiltà contadina Valle dell'Aniene

Museums · Themed

In the Castello Brancaccio, it was the first demoethnoanthropological museum of Lazio: about two thousand objects, largely donated by the inhabitants, on pastoralism, agriculture, school, popular religiosity and domestic life, with reconstructed environments, photographs, letters and toys; of particular interest the part on religious and festive traditions. A recently opened archaeological section exhibits remains of Roman aqueducts that carried the Aniene water to Rome and finds of excavations on the Sublacense street. It is part of the Demos system and Medaniene network.

  • Suggested time 1-1.5 hours

Porta Scaramuccia e borgo medievale

History and architecture · Squares and old town · Free entry

The Gothic gate that closes the old town was demolished by the Municipality in 1878 and rebuilt by Camillo Massimo in 1880. The name of the village derives from the Roman gens of the Rubri, settled in the area after the submission of the Equi in 304 BC.

Acquedotto romano e via Valeria

History and architecture · Archaeology

In the territory passes one of the main aqueducts of the area, dated to 144-130 BC and attributed to the Pretore Quinto Marcio Re; there are also traces of the Via Valeria Vetus, which climbed for Cineto Romano, Roviano and Riofreddo.
