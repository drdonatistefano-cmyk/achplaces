What to see in Amandola

Borgo del Parco nazionale dei Monti Sibillini with the Podestà Tower on the Alta square, the fourteenth-century churches of Sant'Agostino and San Francesco with the Anthropogeographic Museum in the cloister, and in the surrounding area the Romanesque abbeys of Santi Ruffino and Vitale and Santi Vincenzo and Anastasio.

8 places to visit in Amandola, in the province of Fermo (Marche). Every entry is checked against official sources.

Museo antropogeografico e Chiostro di San Francesco

Museums · Themed · Paid entry

Visitor centre of the Park on the first floor of the cloister of San Francesco, rebuilt in the early seventeenth century with frescoed lunettes in 1635: presents the Sibillini Mountains with paths for images and texts on environments, flora, fauna, legends, art, history and popular culture, with attention to how man shaped the landscape. Reduced €3 for children 7-16 years and groups, free of charge up to 6 years.

the MiC card (updated to January 2021) indicates closed every day; the Park warns that after the earthquake 2016 some areas are forbidden

  • Where Largo Leopardi, Amandola
  • Entry €3
  • Suggested time 1-1.5 hours
  • Tourist portal page www.italia.it

Sources: cultura.gov.it · sibillini.net · italia.it

Abbazia dei Santi Ruffino e Vitale

Places of worship · Abbeys and monasteries

Romanesque complex with raised and crypt dating from the 11th to the 12th century, bell tower of the late fourteenth century, frescoes of the 14th and 15th century in the presbytery and probably remains of the 10th century in the underground chapel.

  • Where Amandola
  • Entry To be checked
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes
  • Tourist portal page www.italia.it

Sources: sibillini.net · italia.it

Abbazia dei Santi Vincenzo e Anastasio

Places of worship · Abbeys and monasteries · Viewpoint

Benedictine abbey about 850 meters at the foot of the Amandola mountain, 5 km from the village: documented from 1044, deeply modified between 1295 and the fifteenth century, with bell tower rebuilt in 1461. The original structure remains the right wing, some arches of the cloister and fragments of the apse; single nave interior with trussed ceiling and single nave crypt on a single central pillar. For the timetables, contact the Municipality.

  • Where Monte Amandola, Amandola
  • Entry To be checked
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes
  • Tourist portal page www.italia.it

Sources: eventi.turismo.marche.it · sibillini.net · italia.it

Chiesa di Sant'Agostino (Santuario del Beato Antonio)

Places of worship · Sanctuaries · Free entry

Erected in the fifteenth century by the prior Antonio Migliorati, with eighteenth-century façade and Gothic portal; the bell tower was completed in 1464 by Pietro Lombardo. Frescoes from the beginning of the twentieth century by F. Ferranti; behind the high altar the urn with the remains of Blessed Antonio.

  • Entry Free

Chiesa di San Francesco

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches · Free entry

Semi-Gothic church consecrated in 1352, with portal of 1429; in the center of the apse a wooden Christ of the late thirteenth century and in the chapel of the Annunciation Umbrian-Marche frescoes of the second half of the fifteenth century.

Piazza Umberto I, Torrione del Podestà e Porta San Giacomo

History and architecture · Squares and old town · Free entry · Viewpoint

The Piazza Alta, an ancient plateau communis, was the center of the social and religious life of the village and is dominated by the Torrione del Podestà of the fifteenth century; in the village also the fifteenth-century house with dovecote, the Porta San Giacomo with Ghibellines merlons and two coats of arms of the fifteenth century and the viewpoint on the east side of the Sibillini.

Chiesa di Santa Maria della Pace (ex monastero delle Clarisse)

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches · Free entry

Monastery built between 1506 and 1560, with cloister with two loggias finished in 1560 and refectory of 1549; portal and oculus were carved in local stone in 1539 by Francesco da Milano.
