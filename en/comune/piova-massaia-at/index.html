What to see in Piovà Massaia

The village of mint is named after Cardinal Guglielmo Massaja, a missionary in Ethiopia; it houses the parish church of Saints Peter and George attributed to Benedict Alfieri, the remains of the church with double apse of San Martino di Castelvero and, in the town hall, a didactic workshop dedicated to Monferrato and Cardinal.

Chiesa parrocchiale dei Santi Pietro e Giorgio

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches

Attributed to Benedetto Alfieri, it was built between 1749 and 1774 on the initiative of Count Francesco Antonio Ricci, with a baroque plan with an elongated Greek cross and an octagonal tiburian dome; the bell tower (1779-1781) has five bells merged by De Giorgis in 1781. Inside there are three marble altars, twelve paintings with the Apostles of Pietro Montaldo (one dated 1824), the altarpiece of Sebastiano Taricco's Assumption and wooden stands perhaps from Sant'Andrea di Chieri. Open on Saturday 15-18 and Sunday 9.30-12.30, other days by reservation (tel. 0131 996603).

Laboratorio Didattico Culturale per il Territorio del Monferrato

Museums · City history · Free entry

Set up in the halls of the municipal palace, he tells the Monferrato through ten immersive stations led by the figure of Cardinal Massaja and panels with QR code on his life until the African years. A second room presents a path between Romanesque and Baroque Alfierian churches, the music of Giovanni Battista Polledro and Monferrato figures such as Giuseppe Govone, Umberto Cagni, Giacomo Bove, Cosma Manera and Giovanni Pastrone. Open for parties and events and always by reservation (tel. 0131 996603).

  • Where Palazzo comunale, Piazza Marconi 1, Piovà Massaia
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 1-1.5 hours
  • Tourist portal page www.comune.piovamassaia.at.it

Sources: comune.piovamassaia.at.it

Chiesa di San Martino di Castelvero

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches

At the entrance of the village are the walls of the church of San Martino di Castelvero, marked by the Romanesque itinerary of the ATL as a "rarissima," a double apse.
