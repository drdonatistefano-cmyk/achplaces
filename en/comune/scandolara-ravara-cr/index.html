What to see in Scandolara Ravara

The municipality is known for the hamlet of Castelponzone, an ancient walled village linked to the Ponzone and the tradition of hemp rope, with sixteenth-century porticos, the church of Saints Faustino and Giovita, the Old Church and the Museum of Cordai.

Borgo di Castelponzone

History and architecture · Villages · Free entry

Ancient walled village with a Renaissance character, divided into regular blocks: the walls have been flattened and the moat of the basement castle, and the ring road follows the path; of the two doors only the southern one remains. Along the main street there are sixteenth-century arcades with arches of every shape, once home to shops, artisans and taverns, on an original cobbled restored; there is also the eighteenth-century residence known as the Villa, with portico with Doric columns. Around, barns, stables and farmhouses tell the rural world.

  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 1-1.5 hours
  • Tourist portal page www.italia.it

Museo dei Cordai

Museums · Themed

It tells the tradition of hemp rope, practiced in the village for at least two centuries and by most of the inhabitants: tools and machinery of different eras, strings and objects in rope, historical photographs of ropemakers at work and videos of the last generations. Open from March to October on Sundays and holidays, 15-19; closed from 15 July to 15 August.

  • Tourist portal page www.italia.it

Chiesa dei Santi Faustino e Giovita

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches · Free entry

18th century parish church dedicated to the saints Faustino and Giovita; it houses the canvas of Saint Lucia del Genovesino (Luigi Miradori), painter of the seventeenth century.

  • Where Via Nuova 9-19, Castelponzone, Scandolara Ravara
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes
  • Tourist portal page www.italia.it

Sources: turismocremona.it · borghipiubelliditalia.it · in-lombardia.it · italia.it

Chiesa Vecchia (Madonna Regina della Pace)

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches

Dedicated to Our Lady Queen of Peace, it is an example of the passage between late Romanesque and Lombard Gothic; it has a beautiful façade, the bell tower, frescoes and a wooden crucifix.
