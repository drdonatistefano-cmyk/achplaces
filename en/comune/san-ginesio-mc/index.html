What to see in San Ginesio

The village is walled in the Sibillini Mountains National Park, called "Balcony of the Sibillini" for the view that goes from the Gran Sasso to the Conero: the Collegiata with the frescoed crypt by Lorenzo Salimbeni, the Pellegrini Hospital and the Pinacoteca "Scipione Gentili," in a center marked by the earthquake of 2016.

Chiesa Collegiata

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches

The portal in travertine with concentric arches with the small figure of San Ginesio is of the 11th century; the upper part of the terracotta façade was built in 1421 by a German master. Inside the wooden Crucifix reported by the exiles in 1450, works by Federico Zuccari, Pomarancio and Simone De Magistris and the crypt frescoed by Lorenzo Salimbeni in 1406.

damaged by the earthquake 2016 with collapses located in the vaults, safety and restoration in design (MIC USS Sisma, January 2026)

  • Where Piazza Gentili, San Ginesio
  • Entry To be checked
  • Suggested time 1-1.5 hours
  • Tourist portal page www.italia.it

Sources: borghipiubelliditalia.it · uss-sisma2016.cultura.gov.it · italia.it

Museo civico e Pinacoteca "Scipione Gentili"

Museums · Old masters · Paid entry

In the former fourteenth-century church of San Sebastiano, it contains paintings from the fifteenth to the eighteenth century: two tables by Stefano Folchetti, the Pietà by Simone De Magistris (1584), a Madonna with Child and Saints by Vincenzo Pagani and works by Nicolò da Siena.

  • Where Via Raffaele Merelli, San Ginesio
  • Entry €2,50
  • Suggested time 1-1.5 hours
  • Tourist portal page www.italia.it

Sources: cultura.gov.it · italia.it

Piazza Gentili

History and architecture · Squares and old town · Free entry

Main square with the Collegiate Church, the Romanesque Civic Tower with its 17th-century onion spire and the Teatro "Giacomo Leopardi."

Ospedale dei Pellegrini

History and architecture · Palaces and villas · Free entry

From the 13th century, it welcomed pilgrims on foot to Loreto or Rome; restored in 1456-57 with the addition of the loggia, it preserves the Romanesque façade and some porticoes.

  • Where San Ginesio
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes
  • Tourist portal page www.italia.it

Eremo di San Liberato

Places of worship · Sanctuaries · Free entry · Viewpoint

Ancient romoratory then sanctuary where he lived in prayer St. Liberate (born between 1215 and 1218); in 1421 the church was partly rebuilt with a convent next door. The Friars Minor and a little above the Prati di San Liberato, suitable for picnics, look at the Adriatic.
