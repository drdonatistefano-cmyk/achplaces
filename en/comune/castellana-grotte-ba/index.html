Cities at the gates of the Valle d'Itria, famous for the Caves of Castellana, karst system of more than 3 km discovered by Franco Anelli in 1938, with the White Grotto and the Speleological Museum dedicated to the discoverer.

Grotte di Castellana

Nature and landscape · Caves and geosites

On January 23, 1938, the speleologist Franco Anelli looked out over the Grave, the 50-metre-wide and 60-metre-deep access hole, and descended there two days later with Vito Matarrese, who in 1939 discovered the White Grotto, considered one of the most candid in the world for the purity of calcium carbonate. The complex extends for 3,348 meters up to 122 meters of depth, with a temperature of about 16,5 °C; between the caves the Wolf, the Monuments, the Corridor of the Serpent, the Little Paradise and the Lake of Cristallo. Two guided itineraries: partial of about 1 km (50 minutes) and complete of about 3 km (about 100 minutes) to the White Grotto.

Museo speleologico Franco Anelli

Museums · Science and university

Opened on 23 January 2000 in the local limestone building designed by Pietro Favia, it is named after Franco Anelli (1899-1977), discoverer and spreader of the caves. It exhibits fossils, minerals, concretions and historical evidences, with the 3D reconstruction of three caves; it offers guided tours, educational workshops and speleotouristic visits in the side branches of the caves. You visit after the cave tour.
