What to see in Bucchianico

Ancient medieval center on a hill between Majella and Adriatic, where on May 25, 1550 St. Camillo de Lellis, founder of the Camillians, was born: the birthplace and the shrine-convento he wanted are the main destinations, together with the church of Sant'Urbano, linked to the Festa dei Bandersi, the palaces of the center and the calanchi of the Ripe dello Spagnolo.

Casa natale di San Camillo de Lellis

Museums · House museums

Paternal house of Captain Giovanni de Lellis and Camilla de Compellis: Camillo was born there on May 25, 1550 in the barn on the ground floor, partly transformed into a baroque hat born in 1712 and dedicated to the saint in 1897; the environment next to it preserves the original form, as evidence of the poverty of the places. On the first floor, for an external staircase, a room with everyday objects reconstructs the age of the saint. Open every day 7.30-19 (in summer until 19.30), without reservation.

Santuario e convento di San Camillo de Lellis

Places of worship · Sanctuaries

In 1604 Camillo de Lellis, at the invitation of the University, decided to establish here a convent of his Order, completed in 1615 by seven masons from Rome, probably on a project by Francesco and Orazio Torriani; it is brick with stone cantonals. Came to the Commune in 1809, he returned to the Camillians in 1819. The shrine preserves a polychrome and golden wooden bust of the saint. Touring hours: 7.30-12.30 and 15.30-19 (daylight saving time until 7.45).

Chiesa di Sant'Urbano

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches

It was born from the Benedictine monastery of Santa Maria Maggiore built in 1034 by the abbot of San Liberatore in Maiella and built by Saint Aldemario da Capua, in a dominant position towards the north-east; the relics of Aldemario are venerated in the medieval crypt. Tradition has it that in the 14th century a dream of St Urban has solved without blood a siege of the Teatini: from here the Feast of the Bandersi, in May, re-enactment that lasts for about seven centuries.

Centro storico, palazzo marchesale e porta della Castellara

History and architecture · Squares and old town · Free entry · Viewpoint

The village preserves traces of the walls with towers of the 13th-XIV century, which had six doors: in the district Castellara remains the only ancient door, with sharp arch. The marquisal palace, probably from 1543-1584, closes the third floor of Mezzo and is the backdrop to the square; in Piazza Platea Magna the town hall of late XII-early XIII century houses on the first floor the church of San Michele Arcangelo, with the Ridotto placed on the back. In the eighteenth century, the bourgeois families, including the de Lellis, built palaces by Lombard masters, with cellars turned into exposed bricks once oil mills and warehouses. From the viewpoints you can see the sea, the Majella, the Gran Sasso and the Pescara valley.

Calanchi delle Ripe dello Spagnolo

Nature and landscape · Landscapes · Free entry · Viewpoint

Calanchi recognized as a good of community interest, which extend for about 3,5 km in the valley of the Alento with walls that in some places exceed 100 meters; in the territory flow streams like the Rio Seripenna, the Focaro and the Valige and survive houses of raw earth.

Cantinarte Museo dell'Olio

Local life and traditions · Food and wine

An ancient olive oil mill transformed into an oil museum, with guided tours and tastings; extra virgin olive oil, Montepulciano d'Abruzzo and saffron are sold there. Opening on request, for groups. Bucchianico is known for the oil produced by several mills.
