What to see in Castelvenere

Medieval village of Sannio with towers and cellars dug into the gray tuff, considered the most lively municipality of the South and European Wine City 2019; in Palazzo Barone the museum of peasant civilization and St. Barbato, born here in 602.

Borgo medievale, cantine di tufo e Torre angioina

History and architecture · Villages · Free entry · Viewpoint

The old town preserves the medieval plant and numerous cavities dug in Renaissance age to extract the gray tuff, then transformed into cellars; the towers and cellars date back to the XIV-XV centuries. At the edge of the village stands the Angevin Tower, with a terrace.

  • Where Piazza San Barbato, Castelvenere
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 1-1.5 hours
  • Tourist portal page www.touringclub.it

Sources: touringclub.it · cultura.gov.it

Museo della civiltà e delle tradizioni contadine

Museums · Themed

Anthropological demoetanological museum in Palazzo Barone, also spread over the territory to the Roman villa of the district of San Tommaso. Four sections: rural life and wine tradition with tools and tuff cellars; photographs of community life; Saint Barbato, born in Castelvenere in 602 and then bishop of Benevento; archaeology and prehistory with Sannito-Roman epigraphs, cippi and remains of the palafitta.

  • Where Via Mulino Borgo Medievale 1, Castelvenere
  • Entry To be checked
  • Suggested time 1-1.5 hours
  • Tourist portal page www.touringclub.it

Sources: cultura.gov.it · touringclub.it

Vigneti e Festa del vino

Local life and traditions · Food and wine

Castelvenere is considered the most lively municipality in the South, with Falanghina, Aglianico and Barbera del Sannio (Camaiola); in 2019 it was European Wine City. The underground cellars in the historic centre host the Wine Festival on the last weekend of August.
