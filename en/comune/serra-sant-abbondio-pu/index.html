What to see in Serra Sant'Abbondio

Borgo dell'alta valle del Cesano at the foot of Monte Catria, founded in the thirteenth century by the free Municipality of Gubbio as a garrison on the road between Umbria and the Adriatic. In its territory stands the Camaldolese monastery of Santa Croce di Fonte Avellana, cited by Dante in Paradise, and the early Christian crypt of San Biagio.

Monastero di Santa Croce di Fonte Avellana

Places of worship · Abbeys and monasteries

Camaldolese hermits founded around 980 by a group of hermits on the slopes of Mount Catria, about 700 m above sea level, linked to the spirituality of St. Romualdo; St. Pier Damiani guided its development. Dante quotes him in the 21st canto of Paradise and perhaps stayed there after 1310. You can visit cloisters, Romanesque church, historical library, scriptorium and ancient cells of monks; it houses retreats, concerts and guided tours. Times indicated: every day 10-12 and 15-17:30.

Centro storico e porte medievali

History and architecture · Squares and old town · Free entry

The village was founded in the thirteenth century by the free Municipality of Gubbio as a defensive point on the road between Umbria and Adriatic. The medieval plant retains two of the four original doors, the Santa Porta with the tower and the thirteenth-century Porta di Macione, and two streets parallel to the sides of the central street. Of the fortress built in 1481 by Federico da Montefeltro on a project by Francesco di Giorgio Martini, then destroyed by order of Duke Guidobaldo, nothing remains.

Cripta paleocristiana di San Biagio

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches

Crypt of the IV-V century, just outside the village on the left bank of the Cesano, built with materials of Roman era coming from a pagan temple.

Boschi del Monte Catria

Nature and landscape · Mountains · Viewpoint

Monte Catria (1,701 m) overlooks the hermitage of Fonte Avellana, surrounded by holm oaks, oaks and hazelnuts; the monastery is the starting point for walks in the woods of Catria between beech and chestnut trees, on panoramic paths.
