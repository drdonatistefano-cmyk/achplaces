What to see in Villanovafranca

The village of the eastern Marmilla, which grew up around the parish church of San Lorenzo, known for the nuraghe Su Mulinu on the valley of Rio Mannu, where a Nuragic altar of the Iron Age was found in the form of nuraghes, now rebuilt in the civic museum.

Nuraghe Su Mulinu

History and architecture · Archaeology · Viewpoint

Complex in marble blocks on a small relief above the valley of the Rio Mannu, built from the 16th-XV century B.C. and expanded in the 14th with a trilobed fortification and a four-tower, then five-tower wall; inhabited until the orientalizing age and re-used in Punic, Roman and Altomedieval era. In the central room, a place of worship from the 14th century BC, was found an altar in sandstone in the shape of nuraghe of the early Iron Age, with a tank for liquids, helmets of swords and a sculpted moon sickle. Excavations started in 1983 by Giovanni Ugas. Opening hours: October-March 8.45-13 and 14.30-17.05, April-September 10.15-13 and 15-19.05; tickets at the museum.

Civico Museo Archeologico Su Mulinu

Museums · Archaeological

In the former Granatic Mountain of the mid-nineteenth century, exhibits ceramic, metal, vitreous and lithic finds of the territory from the fourth millennium BC to the third century AD, especially from the excavations of Su Mulinu, and illustrates the evolution of nuragic architecture. The strong piece is the life-size cast of the nuragic altar of the IX-VIII century B.C. in a scenic reconstruction; there is a section for the blind with captions in Braille and finds to touch. Admission fee, free for residents and children up to 6 years.
