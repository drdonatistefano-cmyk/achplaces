What to see in Grammichele

The city was founded after the earthquake of 1693, which destroyed the nearby village of Occhiola: Prince Carlo Maria Carafa and Brother Michele da Ferla drew it hexagon around a large central square, with the Mother Church of San Michele and the Town Hall; the ruins of Occhiola are today an archaeological park.

Piazza esagonale

History and architecture · Squares and old town · Free entry

Large hexagonal square, studied in the faculties of architecture, from which start the radial streets that reach six peripheral squares; the project was traced by the prince of Butera with brother Michele da Ferla and the first stone laid on 18 April 1693. At the centre is a bronze by Turkish artist Murat Cura, part of a sundial, with a man kneeling wrapped in circles reminiscent of an armillary sphere; the bronze statue of the prince of Butera by Paolo Guarrera has one foot on Occhiola and the other towards the new city.

  • Where Piazza centrale, Grammichele
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes
  • Tourist portal page www.italia.it

Chiesa Madre di San Michele Arcangelo

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches

Scenographic church on the square, funded by the heirs of the Prince of Butera and completed only at the end of the nineteenth century, with a monumental facade with overlapping orders and typical crowning of the Sicilian baroque.

  • Where Piazza centrale, Grammichele
  • Entry To be checked
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes
  • Tourist portal page www.italia.it

Sources: italia.it · visitsicily.info

Palazzo Comunale e Museo civico archeologico

Museums · Archaeological

The palace designed by Carlo Sada in 1896 overlooks the square and preserves the slate slab on which the original plan of the city was engraved; it houses a small archaeological museum with the findings of recent finds, while most of those of historical research is at the Archaeological Museum of Syracuse.

  • Where Piazza centrale, Grammichele
  • Entry To be checked
  • Suggested time 1-1.5 hours
  • Tourist portal page www.italia.it

Sources: visitsicily.info · italia.it · turismo.cittametropolitana.ct.it

Parco archeologico comunale di Occhiolà

History and architecture · Archaeology

The visitable ruins of the medieval village destroyed by the earthquake of 1693, with the castle and part of the urban fabric, and traces of the previous settlements: Paolo Orsi identified the Hellenized Sicula town of Echetla.
