What to see in Legnago

Città sull'Adige, piazzaforte veneziana redesigned by Michele Sanmicheli since 1525 and then fortress of the Austrian Quadrilatero; it remains the fifteenth-century torrione, the marching lions and sections of walls, while the house-museum of the Fondazione Fioroni and the Archaeological Environmental Center tell Risorgimento and prehistory of the plain. Here Antonio Salieri was born in 1750.

Torrione di Legnago

History and architecture · Towers · Free entry

The most evocative monument of the city and symbol of the local military architecture: the first fortifications on the two banks of the Adige go back to the 10th century, in defence of the river port; in 1475 the Venetians rebuilt the fortress with walls, moat and four round corner towers, one of which reached us. Visitable from the outside.

  • Where Piazza della Libertà 14, Legnago
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes
  • Tourist portal page www.visitverona.it

Sources: visitverona.it

Museo della Fondazione Fioroni

Museums · House museums

House-museum in the Floroni-Accordi palace, built on the new avenues after the demolition of the walls: the collection of Risorgimento relics collected by the Fioroni family from the last decades of the nineteenth century was exhibited in the early twenties of the twentieth century in rooms furnished with furniture, tapestries and chandeliers of epoch. Eight rooms retracing the Unity from the Napoleonic epic to the myth of Garibaldi; the ground floor tells the Medieval and Renaissance Legnago with weapons and ceramics, and in the courtyard there are the lapidary, one of the marching lions and the portal of the birthplace of Salieri. Open Tuesday, Thursday and Friday 9-12, Wednesday and Sunday 15-19.

  • Where Via Giacomo Matteotti 39, Legnago
  • Entry To be checked
  • Suggested time 1-1.5 hours
  • Tourist portal page www.visitverona.it

Sources: cultura.gov.it · visitverona.it

Centro Ambientale Archeologico - Museo civico Pianura di Legnago

Museums · Archaeological

In an Austrian military building of the early nineteenth century, already barracks of French cavalry and then military hospital, restored in 1999: exhibits the finds of the Verona plain collected in the last thirty years, about 5,000 years of history from the ancient Neolithic (V millennium BC) to the late Iron Age (II-I century BC). Workshops for families and schools; since 2010 it is part of the Legnago Museums network managed by Fondazione Fioroni.

Area archeologica delle ex Cantine Petternella

History and architecture · Castles and fortresses

Remains of the hexagonal fortress designed in the sixteenth century by Michele Sanmicheli, with thick walls, terrapies, moat and esplanade; the works began in 1525 and lasted about seventy years. After the Unity, the walls were largely demolished from 1887 onwards: only a few stretches remain here and along Via Leopardi.

Visitable only for extraordinary openings

  • Where Via Argine 18, Legnago
  • Entry To be checked
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes
  • Tourist portal page www.visitverona.it

Sources: visitverona.it

Leoni di Legnago

History and architecture · Monuments and memorials · Free entry

Five winged lions in white marble, symbol of the Serenissima, which adorned the gates and walls of the fortress rebuilt at the beginning of the sixteenth century; today one is walled in the ramp of the bridge on the side of Porto, others are at the gymnasium of the Cotta High School and in the gardens of Via XX Settembre, and one in the courtyard of the Fondazione Fioroni. Always visitable.

  • Where Via Lungadige Scrami, Legnago
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes
  • Tourist portal page www.visitverona.it

Sources: visitverona.it

Centro storico e borghi medievali

History and architecture · Squares and old town · Free entry

Between the parish church of San Martino and the fortress opens the maze of alleys of the 'borghi', the most prestigious neighborhoods of the ancient Legnago and still today the most characteristic part; in front of the fortress, Piazza della Libertà hosted in the fifteenth century the weekly market, while Piazza San Martino was the area of shops and empori, with the monument to the fallen Rationalist of 1937. The old city was destroyed by the bombings of World War II for about 70%.

  • Where Piazza della Libertà, Legnago
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes
  • Tourist portal page www.visitverona.it

Sources: visitverona.it

Casa natale di Antonio Salieri

History and architecture · Monuments and memorials · Free entry

Here was born on 18 August 1750 the composer Antonio Salieri, seventh son of the merchant Antonio and Anna Maria Scachi; the building, very renovated, is visible from the outside with a commemorative plaque, while the original portal is in the courtyard of Palazzo Fioroni. Salieri left Legnago in 1766 for Venice and then Vienna, and returned only once, in 1807.

  • Where Via Disciplina, Legnago
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes
  • Tourist portal page www.visitverona.it

Sources: visitverona.it

Teatro Salieri

History and architecture · Historic theatres

Designed in 1911 by the Anonymous Theatre Society, with the works interrupted by the First World War, opened in 1933 and in 1934 it was named after Antonio Salieri; it replaced the ancient theatre of the fortress, abandoned in 1903 and destroyed by the bombs of the Second World War.

  • Where Via XX Settembre, Legnago
  • Entry To be checked
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes
  • Tourist portal page www.visitverona.it

Sources: visitverona.it

Viale dei Tigli e la Legnago Liberty

History and architecture · Palaces and villas · Free entry

After the demolition of the walls, since 1887, new blocks and avenues arose: Piazza Garibaldi was prolonged during the Vittoria and in 1925 was completed Viale dei Tigli, with Liberty style villas, the former National Opera Balilla and public baths of 1924; the former racetrack of 1895 became an urban park with the monument to Vittorio Emanuele II.

  • Where Viale dei Tigli, Legnago
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes
  • Tourist portal page www.visitverona.it

Sources: visitverona.it
