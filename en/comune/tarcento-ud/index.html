What to see in Tarcento

Tarcento, between mountains and plains along the river Torre, is known for its liberty villas and the remains of ancient castles; in the 17th-17th century Palazzo Frangipane is home to the Natural Archaeological Museum, with one of the most important European collections of trilobites, while on the hill of Coja dominates the neo-Gothic Villa Moretti with its park.

Museo archeologico naturalistico di Tarcento

Museums · Themed

It occupies some rooms of Palazzo Frangipane, an ancient noble building of municipal property with construction phases of the XVI-XVIII centuries. On the ground floor local fauna, Mediterranean shells and crabs of the Tuscan Archipelago; in the large octagonal hall of the upper floor one of the most important European collections of trilobites, with ammonites and remains of marine and terrestrial reptiles (plesiosaur, mosasaur, dinosaur) ordered by Cambrian to Oligocene, as well as minerals and a showcase of experimental archaeology. The archaeological and palaeontological finds are not currently exposed. Open on Sundays and holidays (times indicated in a different way from the card), other days on request.

Villa Moretti e il suo parco

History and architecture · Palaces and villas · Free entry · Viewpoint

Designed by the Turin architect Vandone for the industrial Luigi Moretti (project 1897-1900) and built in 1904 as a neo-Gothic castle with towers and battlements, half-cost on the Coja hill, dominates the town. Magazine in 1919-1922 by the Trieste Ruggero and Arduino Berlam in a liberty key and modernized in 1952, was severely damaged by the earthquake of 1976 and then restored and returned to public use. The large park, born from an Italian garden, is today a public park.

the villa is closed to the public and opens only at events

Chiesa di San Pietro apostolo

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches

Of Romanesque origin, it was rebuilt in the 15th century (the Gothic portal of the facade dates back to 1425) and then enlarged: the lateral aisles were extended east to the mid-19th century and the presbytery enlarged at the beginning of the 20th century.
