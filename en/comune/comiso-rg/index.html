Museo civico di storia naturale

Museums · Science and university · Paid entry

Founded in 1991, it has 1,500 m2 of exposure and more than 30,000 fossils and zoological finds; the collection of cetaceans, with about fifty beached specimens, is indicated as the most important in South Italy. In the paleontological section a skeleton of Psittacosaurus, a Brazilian pterosaur of four meters of wingspan and elephants dwarf Palaeoloxodon falconeri found in the territory of Comiso; in the zoological one sharks, moonfish, a clairvoyant of Madagascar, a giant turtle of Aldabra and about 1,600 birds.

  • Where Via degli Studi 9, Comiso
  • Entry €6, reduced €3,50 (residents €4)
  • Suggested time 1-1.5 hours
  • Tourist portal page www.visitsicily.info

Sources: comune.comiso.rg.it · visitsicily.info

Piazza Fonte Diana e Palazzo Iacono-Ciarcià

History and architecture · Squares and old town · Free entry

The heart of the city, once Piazza della Fontana, rises on the source where according to tradition the goddess Diana drank and bathed; the current structure is from the late nineteenth-early twentieth century, with the Town Hall of 1887, Palazzo Iacono (1894) and Palazzo Criscione (1931-1932) of engineer Santoro Cecolo. The oldest building is Palazzo Iacono-Ciarcia, with the late Baroque loggia almost certainly by Rosario Gagliardi (1735-1740); a few meters, at the entrance of Via Calogero, the remains of a Roman thermal complex enlarged in the Byzantine age.

  • Where Piazza Fonte Diana, Comiso
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes
  • Tourist portal page www.visitsicily.info

Sources: comune.comiso.rg.it · visitsicily.info

Chiesa Madre di Santa Maria delle Stelle

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches · Free entry · Viewpoint

Born in the 15th century as Santa Maria del Mulino, destroyed by the earthquake of 1693 and reopened in 1699; the neo-Gothic dome was completed in 1894 and the bell tower in 1936. Of the original building remain the pillars and the arch with sharp sixth of the central nave; inside a wooden ceiling attributed to the Barbalonga of Messina, stuccoes and sepulchral monuments. Teatro Naselli

History and architecture · Historic theatres

The 19th-century theatre, commonly dated to 1841, named after the Naselli family who built Comiso in the 16th century in County; the façade of Doric-Siculus portico in white stone of Comiso was completed in 1859 by engineer Sortino. Rebuilt in the late seventies saving only the facade, it was reopened on November 25, 2000 after the restoration of Salvatore Catalano and Salvino Palumbo and has 264 seats.
