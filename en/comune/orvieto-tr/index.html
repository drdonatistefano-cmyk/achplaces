What to see in Orvieto

City on the tuff cliff between Umbria and Tuscia: the Cathedral with the Chapel of San Brizio di Beato Angelico and Luca Signorelli, the Well of San Patrizio and an underground city of Etruscan caves and tunnels.

11 places to visit in Orvieto, in the province of Terni (Umbria). Every entry is checked against official sources.

Open Orvieto in the guide with map and filters

Duomo di Orvieto e Cappella di San Brizio

Places of worship · Cathedrals · Viewpoint

A symbol of the city, a jewel of Romanesque-Gothic architecture with its façade illuminated by golden mosaics; in the Chapel of San Brizio one of the greatest painting cycles of the Renaissance, begun by Beato Angelico and completed by Luca Signorelli between 1499 and 1504.

  • Where Piazza del Duomo, Orvieto
  • Entry To be checked
  • Suggested time 2 hours or more
  • Tourist portal page www.italia.it
  • Tickets Tickets on Tiqets

Sources: italia.it

Pozzo di San Patrizio

History and architecture · Monuments and memorials · Paid entry · Viewpoint

Wanted by Pope Clement VII in the Renaissance to ensure water in case of siege: 54 meters deep, you walk with a double helical staircase of 248 steps.

  • Where Orvieto
  • Entry paid
  • Suggested time 1-1.5 hours
  • Tourist portal page www.italia.it

Sources: italia.it

Museo Archeologico Nazionale di Orvieto

Museums · Archaeological · Paid entry

Inaugurated in 1982 on the ground floor of the medieval Palazzo Papale di Martino IV, the Etruscan Orvieto recounts with the furnishings of the necropolis of Crocifisso del Tufo, Cannicella, Poggio Ginestra and Porano; there are rebuilt the Tombs Golini of the IV century BC, with scenes of banquet and funeral rites, and a room dedicated to the Federal sanctuary of the Etruscans.

  • Where Piazza del Duomo, Orvieto
  • Entry paid
  • Suggested time 1-1.5 hours
  • Tourist portal page www.italia.it

Sources: umbriatourism.it · italia.it

Necropoli etrusca di Crocifisso del Tufo

History and architecture · Archaeology · Paid entry

Used from the 8th to the 3rd century B.C., with the maximum expansion between the 6th and 5th centuries: tombs \"a die" lined up in blocks along orthogonal roads, each of a family, whose name is engraved on the architrave.

Since September 30, 2025 partially closed for works; the Antiquarium remains open

  • Where Orvieto
  • Entry paid
  • Suggested time 1-1.5 hours
  • Tourist portal page www.italia.it

Sources: cultura.gov.it · italia.it
