What to see in Castel Focognano

Borgo del Casentino with the remains of one of the oldest castles in the valley: the polygonal tower houses the documentation centre on rural culture, next to the palace with a loggia of the early fifteenth century; in the territory the Romanesque parish church of Sant'Antonino in Socana with the altar of an Etruscan temple of the fifth century BC and the church of Sante Flora and Lucilla a Carda.

Borgo di Castel Focognano, torre e palazzo podestarile

History and architecture · Squares and old town · Free entry

The fortifications of one of the oldest castles of the Casentino, dating back to the 11th century, destroyed by Bishop Tarlati in 1322: the small round tower with octagonal base survives, recently restored, together with the ruins of the 14th century military camp. The palazzo podestarile, now canonical, has an elegant loggia of the first fifteenth century and stone coats of arms on the façade; near the center the eighteenth-century church of San Giovanni Evangelista and the historic oven of the village.

Centro di documentazione sulla cultura rurale del Casentino

Museums · Themed

In the watchtower of the castle, it is part of the Ecomuseum of Casentino and collects objects of the life and work of the local population in three sections: work in the woods and fields (grain cycle and tree cutting), pasteurization and transhumance (milk processing, shearing, moving flocks) and domestic processing of wool, from carding to weaving.

Temporarily closed according to Visit Tuscany; check with the Casentino Ecomuseum

Pieve di Sant'Antonino a Socana e area sacra etrusca

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches

The restorations of the sixties and seventies revealed behind the church an Etruscan temple of the fifth century BC dedicated to Tinia and Menerva: the altar remains visible, almost five meters long and reached by a staircase, used mainly between the V and the 1st century BC; the finds (antifixes at the head of Minerva, ceramics, worked blocks) are in the museums of Bibbiena and Arezzo. Between the 8th and 9th centuries there was a first Christian church; the present Romanesque parish church of the 12th century has three naves divided by decorated pillars, a splendid apse and a cylindrical bell tower.

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches

In the hamlet of Carda, it houses a polyptych by Mariotto di Cristofano dated 1425 and a canvas by Pontenani.
