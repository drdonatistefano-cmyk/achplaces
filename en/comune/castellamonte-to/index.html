What to see in Castellamonte

Città della ceramica del Canavese, where the processing of the red earth developed at the end of the eighteenth century with the first stoves "Franklin": the Unfinished Round by Alessandro Antonelli, the neo-Gothic parish, the ceramic museum of Palazzo Botton, the Stove of Ugo Nespolo and the remains of the castle on the hill.

Museo della Ceramica – Palazzo Botton

Museums · Themed · Free entry

In the restored rooms of Palazzo Botton, rebuilt in the eighteenth century and seat of the Municipality from 1854 to 1990; born as the Civic Collection of Terra Rossa, it is open to the public since 1993. The historical section exhibits fragments of friezes, capitals, dishes, wings for fireplaces .Franklin and pieces of stoves from the 17th to the 19th century; the modern works donated by the artists of the Ceramics Exhibition, as well as stoves and crockery of local craftsmen and works of the students of the Art Institute.

Open only during scheduled temporary exhibitions; information at the Cultural Office 0124 5187616

  • Where Piazza Marconi 1 (accesso da via Conti San Martino), Castellamonte
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes
  • Tourist portal page turismotorino.org

Rotonda Antonelliana

History and architecture · Monuments and memorials · Free entry

What remains of the grandiose parish designed by Alessandro Antonelli, the author of the Mole di Torino, which had to be almost as big as St. Peter: the works, begun in 1842, stopped in 1846 due to lack of funds. The circular perimeter walls in stones of the Orco and Piova streams are still alternated with red bricks, which in summer frame the Ceramics Exhibition.

  • Where Piazza Martiri della Libertà 9, Castellamonte
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes
  • Tourist portal page turismotorino.org

Sources: comune.castellamonte.to.it · turismotorino.org

Parrocchiale dei Santi Pietro e Paolo

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches · Free entry

Built between 1871 and 1875 in neo-Gothic style by the Turinese Luigi Formento, in the space destined to the presbytery of the abandoned Antonellian project. Inside the main baroque altar coming from another church in the village, windows of the 1930s, two large paintings by Elio Torrieri and a golden wooden statue of Our Lady of 1792; in the tympanum of the facade of Christ and the Saints Peter and Paul by Renzo Igne.

  • Where Piazza Martiri della Libertà 9, Castellamonte
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes
  • Tourist portal page turismotorino.org

Sources: comune.castellamonte.to.it · turismotorino.org

Castello di Castellamonte

History and architecture · Castles and fortresses · Viewpoint

The medieval castle was largely destroyed during the Tuchini revolt (1380-1393), of which two stone doors remain; it was renovated in the first half of the seventeenth century by the Counts Cognengo and expanded at the end of the nineteenth century by Formento with a brick wing and Ghibelline blackbirds. Above the entrance restored frescoes of the fifteenth and sixteenth centuries; from the square with terracotta balustrade wide panorama of the Canavese and the valleys Orco and Soana. Walk in 10-15 minutes along the cobbled via Conti di San Martino.

La Stufa di Ugo Nespolo

History and architecture · Monuments and memorials · Free entry

Monument in reinforced concrete covered with ceramic tiles designed by Ugo Nespolo and made by ceramists with the students of the Institute of Art Felice Fcio; started in 2002 and completed in 2008 thanks to a donation of a local company.

  • Where Piazza della Repubblica 12, Castellamonte
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes
  • Tourist portal page turismotorino.org

Sources: comune.castellamonte.to.it · turismotorino.org

Centro storico: torre campanaria e Palazzo dei Conti

History and architecture · Squares and old town · Free entry

In the main square the 12th century Romanesque bell tower, elevated in Baroque style in 1762 for the bell tower. On Piazza Marconi, the sixteenth-century Palazzo dei Conti di Castellamonte, with arches and loggias, passed to San Martino Castelnuovo in 1611: the most historically valuable palace in the city.

  • Where Piazza Marconi, Castellamonte
  • Entry Free
