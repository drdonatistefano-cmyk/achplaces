What to see in Cavriana

Borgo delle Colline Moreniche dell'Alto Mantovano, 170 metres on a hill dominated by the tower of the ancient Gonzaga castle, overlooking the Garda hills; at the foot of the fortress Villa Mirra houses the Museo archeologico dell'Alto Mantovano, and outside the village stands the Romanesque church of Santa Maria.

Museo archeologico dell'Alto Mantovano

Museums · Archaeological · Free entry

Founded in 1966 by the Archaeological Group "Cavriana" inaugurated in 1969 in the medieval tower next to the parish church and transferred in 1983 to Villa Mirra, it retraces about 7,000 years of history of the Upper Mantovano in eight rooms: four prehistoric, two Romanization, one for the most recent and the last periods, where Napoleon III stayed during the battle of Solferino, of Napoleonic history; among the finds, ancient enigmatic tablets. There are also a didactic module and Stables for exhibitions; audioguides in five languages. Open Tuesday-Friday 9-12, Saturday, Sunday and holidays 9-12 and 15-18.

  • Where Villa Mirra, piazza Castello 5, Cavriana
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 1-1.5 hours
  • Tourist portal page www.turismo.mantova.it

Villa Mirra e il suo parco

History and architecture · Palaces and villas · Free entry · Viewpoint

Villa at the foot of the fort built in 1812, in the Austrian age, instead of the Gonzaga palace (tourist sources date it differently); the park is a small botanical garden with cedars of Lebanon, alders and cypresses.

  • Where Piazza Castello, Cavriana
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes
  • Tourist portal page www.turismo.mantova.it

Sources: lombardiabeniculturali.it · turismo.mantova.it

Centro storico e torre del castello

History and architecture · Squares and old town · Free entry · Viewpoint

Borgo collected, surrounded by stone walls, documented in 1045 by an act of Henry III in the defensive line north of the Mantua state. The castle, partly destroyed in 1263 in the struggles between Riva and Bonacolsi, was enlarged by Gonzaga, who built a palace where Giovanni da Padova worked from 1450, stayed Mantegna and Isabella d'Este, then abandoned after 1630. The tower remains, with a bell tower with arabish openings, which acts as a bell tower to the eighteenth-century parish church of the Assumption and of San Biagio di Giovanni Maria Borsotto, rebuilt in 1716; next to it, a gothic arched androne marked the ancient access to the village. The name derives from the pastoral tradition ("Capriana") recalled by the Palio della Capra d'Oro on the second Sunday of July.

  • Where Piazza Castello, Cavriana
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 1-1.5 hours
  • Tourist portal page www.turismo.mantova.it

Sources: lombardiabeniculturali.it · turismo.mantova.it

Pieve romanica di Santa Maria

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches · Free entry

Romanesque church of the 11th century dedicated to Santa Maria Assunta, outside the village, which preserves traces of ancient frescoes and a fifteenth-century Gothic sculpture of the Madonna della Misericordia; it is the final destination of the first Mantua stage of the Via Carolingia da Castiglione delle Stiviere.

  • Where Fuori dal centro, Cavriana
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes
  • Tourist portal page www.turismo.mantova.it

Sources: lombardiabeniculturali.it · turismo.mantova.it
