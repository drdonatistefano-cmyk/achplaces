What to see in Lugnano in Teverina

Village among hills, woods, olive groves and vineyards above the Tiber Valley, with the Romanesque Collegiate Church of Santa Maria Assunta, Palazzo Pennone and the convent of San Francesco; just outside, the Roman villa of Poggio Gramignano with the children's necropolis, whose findings are in the civic archaeological museum.

Collegiata di Santa Maria Assunta

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches

Romanesque church built between the 11th and 12th centuries, according to tradition on a church desired by the Longobard king Desire, documented from 1230 and collegiate from the 16th century. Façade with four columns porch, rosette and symbols of the Evangelists; inside three naves with carved capitals, presbytery raised with ciborium and schola cantorum, fourteenth-century floor with cosmatesque and crypt imitation with a sixteenth-century crucifix in alabaster venerated as miraculous. It preserves a triptych by Nicolò Alunno, the Decollation of the Baptist by Livio Agresti (1571) and a Tyrolean organ Werle of 1756.

Palazzo Pennone

History and architecture · Palaces and villas

Also known as Palazzo Farnese-Ridolfi, by the cardinal governors of Lugnano, and "Pennone" because it stands like the pin of a ship: built around 1650, three-storey, with angulars, balustrades and rustic portals in travertine, it is curiously cut into two by the homonymous gallery. It was the residence of the pontifical governor, then barn, mill and bachiculture; restored, it houses the Town Hall.

Chiesa e convento di San Francesco

Places of worship · Abbeys and monasteries

Built in 1229 where in 1212 St. Francis preached and performed the miracle of the child freed from the wolf: the fresco of the miracle, repainted in the seventeenth century, revealed under a fresco fourteenth century intact. The cloister of 1608 has travertine columns and frescoes on the life of the saint; the baroque bell tower dates back to 1680. In the convent, just outside the old town, Blessed Gonella da Norcia died in 1540.

Villa romana e necropoli dei bambini di Poggio Gramignano

History and architecture · Archaeology · Viewpoint

About 5 km from the village, on a hill on the valley of the Tiber, a rustic villa of the first century BC excavated since 1989 by archaeologist David Soren and the University of Arizona: collapsed in the third century A.D., in the fifth century it was transformed into a cemetery of about 60 children, many laid in amphorae; in 2018 there emerged a little girl of about ten years buried with a stone in her mouth. The municipality has made it an educational archaeological park accessible to all.

Centro storico e porta medievale

History and architecture · Villages · Free entry

Stone alleys and ancient houses between churches and palaces; the medieval gate dates back to the middle of the ninth century with the walls and was restored in the second half of the fifteenth century at the behest of Pope Pius II. In Piazza della Rocca, the former 19th-century factory of Palazzo Pannicelli hosts the multimedia tourist office.
