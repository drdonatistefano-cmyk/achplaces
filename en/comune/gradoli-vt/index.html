What to see in Gradoli

Borgo on a hill of tuff overlooking Lake Bolsena, dominated by the sixteenth-century Palazzo Farnese di Antonio da Sangallo il Giovane, which houses the Museum of Farnesian Costume; in the historic center the Collegiate Church of Santa Maria Maddalena with its museum of sacred art. Land of Aleatico and Grechetto DOC wines and of the "Lunch of Purgatory" on Ash Wednesday.

Palazzo Farnese

History and architecture · Palaces and villas · Free entry · Viewpoint

Commissioned in 1517 by Cardinal Alessandro Farnese, future Pope Paul III, to Antonio da Sangallo the Younger, rises at the highest point of the country on the remains of an ancient castle and was the summer residence of the Farnese. After the fall of Castro (1649) became a convent of the Filippini Fathers; purchased by the Municipality in 1911, it is municipal seat since 1919 and was restored in 1986 and 1997, with the recovery of many frescoes. The rooms have frescoes, terracotta floors and wooden coffered ceilings.

  • Where Piazza Luigi Palombini 2, Gradoli
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 1-1.5 hours
  • Tourist portal page www.visitlazio.com

Museo del costume farnesiano

Museums · Themed

Established in December 1998 on the noble floor and on the top floor of Palazzo Farnese, it exhibits reproductions of clothes and accessories that illustrate fashion in Tuscia between the fifteenth and seventeenth centuries (clothes, jewels, hats, gloves, footwear, bags, fans), inspired by those of the Farnese in the pictorial cycles, with weapons and armor of the era and ceramics from the excavations of the palace. The frescoed rooms, with grotesques and heraldic Farnesian calls, refer to workers close to Baldassarre Peruzzi. MiC timetable: Mon 16-19, Thu and Sat 10-19, Fri 10-18, Sun 10-13, Wednesday closed.

Collegiata e Museo della chiesa di Santa Maria Maddalena

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches · Free entry

It was mentioned as early as 1296, it was reconsecrated in 1447 and declared "insigne" by Paul III in 1535; after a fire it was rebuilt and consecrated in 1705, with a baroque façade joined to the bell tower and apse decorated in the early eighteenth century by Francesco Alippi and Luca Rubini. Inside a pulpit in unfinished walnut by Matteo Siler (dead in 1678). The museum of sacred art, opened in 1979 in the sacristy, preserves a cycle of Renaissance frescoes of the Passion, fifteen eighteenth-century tables, vestments, ex-vote and a liturgical set donated by Cardinal Ferrata.

  • Where Via Cavour 8, Gradoli
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 1-1.5 hours

Centro storico

History and architecture · Villages · Free entry · Viewpoint

From Piazza Vittorio Emanuele, with the fusiform fountain decorated with masks, the arch \"by Ciuchini" introduces to the village: noble palaces including the house of Horatio Romano, accountant of the Farnese, the church of San Michele Arcangelo (probably already existing in 1118), the remains of San Giovanni Decollato, Palazzo Galeotti with a medieval cistern, the church of San Filippo Neri (consecrated in 1718) on the ground floor of Palazzo Farnese and San Rocco, erected by survivors of the plague.

  • Where Piazza Vittorio Emanuele, Gradoli
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 1-1.5 hours

Chiesa di San Magno

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches · Viewpoint

A fifteenth-century church on the shores of Lake Bolsena, known for the ... "forgiveness" of St. Magnus, the plenary indulgence that is celebrated every year on 19 August.

Chiesa e romitorio di San Vittore

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches · Viewpoint

It rises on a hill with a small hermitage, reached through the arch of San Vittore; according to tradition it was erected by vote in the place where the Saracens were stopped by the fog, saving the country. Every May 14 a procession brings the relics of the saint from the village to the church for the sung mass.
