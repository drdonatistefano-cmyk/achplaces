What to see in Lettere

Borgo sui Monti Lattari dominated by the castle that the Duchy of Amalfi built in the 10th century to guard the northern borders, today site of the archaeological park of Pompeii with views of the Gulf of Naples, the valley of Sarno and Vesuvius; under the fortress stands the sixteenth-century Sanctuary of Sant'Anna, former Cathedral.

Castello di Lettere

History and architecture · Castles and fortresses · Paid entry · Viewpoint

Wanted in the 10th century by the Duke of Amalfi to guard the northern borders, part of the network of fortifications that controlled the two slopes of the Lattari; documents of 1030 and 1033 describe it as a fortified village. The trapezoidal castle preserves four towers, the highest was the mast; within the walls the cathedral, bishop's seat since 987, with 12th century bell tower decorated with tarsies, and the Grano Tower. The museum exhibits ceramics, bronzes and animal remains from excavations started in 2007, from the 10th to the 16th century. Free with the ticket of Pompeii of the same day.

Seasonal hours (Nov-Apr only Thursday-Sunday; May-Oct closed on Tuesday); price €2 on the MiC card against €5 on the Park website

Santuario di Sant'Anna (ex Cattedrale)

Places of worship · Sanctuaries

Also known as the former Cathedral of Letters, it stands on a hill of the Lattari Mountains with the fortress behind it and is today the parish of Santa Maria Assunta and San Giovanni Battista. Built at the end of the 16th century, it has a Latin cross plan and a single nave with four chapels on the right and three on the left; the altar of the presbytery is made with the marbles of the ancient balustrade. In the left transept the Coven of the SS. Rosario holds relics and relics; the statue of St. Anne, invoked by pregnant women, is linked to the tradition of the ... dust of the Saint.
