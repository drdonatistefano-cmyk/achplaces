What to see in Trasacco

Country on the southern shore of the ancient lake of Fucino, with the Basilica of Saints Cesidio and Rufino, the Febonio Tower which was the lighthouse for the fishermen of the lake and the sanctuary of the Madonna of Candelecchia.

3 places to visit in Trasacco, in the province of L'Aquila (Abruzzo). Every entry is checked against official sources.

Basilica dei Santi Cesidio e Rufino

Places of worship · Basilicas · Free entry

Basilica of the patrons on the central square: partly covered hut façade with the torso-pyramidal bell tower, Portale degli Meni Romanico-Renaissance on the right side and Portale delle Donne of the 13th century, ancient main entrance. Inside, with four naves, frescoes from the 15th to the 18th century, baptismal font and monument of Saint Catherine, a sarcophagus with symbolic animals such as the main altar, the ambo of the mid 13th century with symbols of the evangelists and the chapel of St. Cesidius with wooden statue on golden altar.

  • Where Piazza Umberto I, Trasacco
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 1-1.5 hours
  • Official website basilicasanticesidioerufino.it

Sources: basilicasanticesidioerufino.it · cultura.gov.it · comune.trasacco.aq.it

Torre Febonio

History and architecture · Towers · Viewpoint

27.50 meters high, square for two thirds and cylindrical at the top, already remembered in 999 in the Chronica Farfense; it was seen on Lake Fucino and night lighthouse for fishermen, and takes its name from the Febonio family that restored it. Damaged by the earthquake of 1915, restored in the seventies and in 2003; an internal wooden staircase leads to the summit with a view of the Fucino.

  • Where Trasacco
  • Entry To be checked
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes
  • Tourist portal page comune.trasacco.aq.it

Santuario della Madonna di Candelecchia

Places of worship · Sanctuaries · Free entry

Born as a simple newsstand to protect a painting considered miraculous, it passed in 1120 to the Chapter of St. Cesidius; of the time remains a wall with a trilobed arched window on the north side. Restored in 1741 and 1747, it was extended to the present dimensions at the beginning of the twentieth century by the initiative of Fr Ercolano Ciofani.

  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes
