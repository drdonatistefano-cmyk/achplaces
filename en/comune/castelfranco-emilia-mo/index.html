What to see in Castelfranco Emilia

Ancient Borgo Franco founded by Bologna on the Via Emilia and home of the legend of the tortellino: the portico center with the archaeological museum Simonini and the churches of Santa Maria Assunta and San Giacomo, the romantic park of Villa Sorra, the towers of the castle of Piumazzo and the collection of vintage cars Righini at the castle of Panzano.

Museo civico archeologico «A.C. Simonini»

Museums · Archaeological

In Palazzo Piella, in the center: ceramics of the Bronze Age (XVII-XII century BC), finds of the Iron Age with an important storeroom of copper loaves used as a means of exchange before the coin, Roman materials with furnishings of a necropolis of the first century BC and a large panel on the centuriation, and a medieval section with ceramics and buckles from the Borgo Franco; there are also preserved remains of houses of the fourteenth century found at the Rocca Magna di Piumazzo.

Chiesa di Santa Maria Assunta

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches · Free entry

The main religious building of the city, along the Via Emilia: interior with Baroque and neoclassical decorations, stuccoes and side altars; preserves the Assumption of Guido Reni and a Madonna with Child by Modena Adeodato Malatesta.

Chiesa di San Giacomo

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches · Free entry

Anciently second parish church of Castelfranco, south of Via Emilia: a single nave church known for acoustics and used for concerts, with the organ of Domenico Traeri of 1743 (restored in 1987), a statue of St. Nicholas of the Scandlari, a painting of the Virgin of Elisabetta Sirani and the Romanesque bell tower with square plan.

Centro storico, portici e monumento al tortellino

History and architecture · Squares and old town · Free entry

Walk under the arcades between Piazza Garibaldi, the urban stretch of the Via Emilia and a stretch of the late-Medieval walls, probably of the late fourteenth century, in bricks tied with mortar and stone. In Piazza Aldo Moro the bronze by Gianni Ferrari tells the legend of the birth of the tortellino, with a giant tortellino of about 50 cm; the Festival of Tortellino is held in September with a reenactment in costume.

Villa Sorra e il suo parco romantico

History and architecture · Palaces and villas

Villa of the late seventeenth century of the Sorra family, probably designed by Giuseppe Antonio Torri with Francesco Maria Angelini, example of Emilian baroque with oval hall at double height, imperial staircase and chapel; since 1972 it belongs to the Municipalities of Castelfranco, Modena, Nonantola and San Cesario. The park, born in French and transformed between 1827 and 1850 in English on the advice of Giovanni De Brignoli, with fake castle ruins and towers, caves, greenhouses and pond, is considered the most important extensive romantic garden in the region.

The villa is closed for restoration; the park is open to the public during the day all year round

Torrazzo e Rocca Magna di Piumazzo

History and architecture · Towers · Free entry

There are two towers of the castle erected by the Bolognese in 1202 as an outpost towards Modena: the Torrazzo, a square tower crenellated on three floors with the doorway to the village, rebuilt in the mid-Fifteenth century and facing Bologna, and the tower of the Rocca Magna at the entrance of the village, on the side of Modena. Under the Torrazzo the monument to the fallen with a bronze by Augusto Murer (1983).

  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes

Santuario della Madonna della Provvidenza e Oratorio di San Colombano

Places of worship · Sanctuaries

In Piumazzo, the sanctuary was born from a terracotta image of the Virgin on an elm to a crucifix: the oratory of 1817 was replaced by the current sanctuary in 1927. The Oratory of San Colombano, considered the oldest place in the village (VII century), was rebuilt by the Boccadiferro in 1559 and preserves a fresco of the Crucifixion recently rediscovered.

Collezione Righini al Castello di Panzano

Local life and traditions · Motors

Private museum of vintage cars founded by Mario Righini, with about 350 vehicles: among them the Auto Avio Costruzioni 815 of 1940, the first car built by Enzo Ferrari before his factory, and the Alfa Romeo 2300 8C belonged to Tazio Nuvolari, as well as Ferrari models, Mercedes-Benz, Isotta Fraschini and Rolls-Royce.
