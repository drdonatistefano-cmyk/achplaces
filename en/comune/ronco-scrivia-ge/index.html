What to see in Ronco Scrivia

Borgo dell'alta Valle Scrivia of probable Roman origin, documented by 1127, walled fief passed by the bishops of Tortona to Malaspina and Spinola; it preserves the sixteenth-century Palazzo Spinola, the castle of Borgo Fornari and the fifteenth-century bridge on Scrivia, with the hamlet of Porale in the Parco dell'Antola.

Castello di Borgo Fornari

History and architecture · Castles and fortresses · Viewpoint

On a hill at 340 m, it dominates the crossroads between the valley road and the one for Voltaggio. Built by Spinola in the 12th century as a small holiday palace of the feudalist and then enlarged with defensive works, it was long considered the main castle of the upper Scrivia Valley; the semicircular tower could be older. Base in stone and upper floors in brick; the best preserved part is the lower body of the garrison on the walled courtyard, excavated in 2005 by the Institute of Experimental Archaeology of Genoa.

History and architecture · Palaces and villas · Free entry

Porale e Parco dell'Antola

Nature and landscape · Parks and nature reserves · Free entry · Viewpoint

A small hamlet that was the seat of a Benedictine monastery, included in the Parco Naturale Regionale dell'Antola, known for its views of Mount Antola (1,600 m) and the landscapes of the Apennines and the Ligurian hinterland.

  • Where Porale, Ronco Scrivia
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 2 hours or more
  • Tourist portal page www.italia.it

Sources: italia.it
