What to see in Gambolò

Borgo della Lomellina dominated by the Litta-Beccaria Castle, documented by the 11th century and transformed into a noble villa by Agostino Litta in 1573, which in the Long Channel hosts the Lomellino Archaeological Museum; not far from the Romanesque parish church of Sant'Eusebio, renovated in Baroque age.

Castello Litta-Beccaria

History and architecture · Castles and fortresses

A fortified nucleus (castrum) built around the Mille, it assumed in the last quarter of the fifteenth century the current quadrilateral plant with angular towers in Visconti-forzesco style; from 1412 the fief was of the Beccaria, and on January 30, 1573 the Marquis Agostino Litta bought it to transform it into a country villa. In 1680 the tower of the drawbridge became the entrance gate, where you can still see the seats of the locks; in the early eighteenth century the Long Channel was started, or Loggia delle Dame, which ends in the Mirabella tower with a view. Donato al Comune dai Robecchi, heirs of Litta, today houses a school, municipal services and the archaeological museum.

Museo Archeologico Lomellino

Museums · Archaeological

On the first floor of the Long Channel of the castle, it exhibits more than 1,200 exhibits in four rooms, from prehistory to the Roman age, with illustrated panels and a large archaeological map of the Lomellina. Open on Sundays; on other days visits by reservation with at least 15 days in advance for schools and groups.

Pieve di Sant'Eusebio

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches

It rises on the top of the upper torrent Terdobbio, a place of worship with Celtic, Roman (are and epigraph) and Christian traces. The Romanesque system was modified in the Baroque age in the seventeenth century; it houses canvases by B. Fields and G.B. Tassinari, the cantoria del Fiammenghino, other paintings of the Pavese school and two side chapels baroque frescoes.
