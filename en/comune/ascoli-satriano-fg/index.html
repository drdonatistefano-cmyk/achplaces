What to see in Ascoli Satriano

Theatre in 279 B.C. of Pirro's victory over the Romans, Ascoli Satriano houses the Trapezophoros in the Museum Pole with the grifos that pierce a deer, returned by the Getty Museum in 2007; beyond the Roman bridge on Carapelle the Archaeological Park of the Dauni opens, while the castle and the cathedral dominate the historic center.

Polo museale (Museo diocesano e Museo civico «Pasquale Rosario»)

Museums · Archaeological · Free entry

Born from the fusion of the Diocesan Museum and the Civic Archaeological Museum, it is housed in the former monastery of Santa Maria del Popolo. The most important pieces are polychrome marbles illegally excavated in the territory in the seventies and returned to Italy in 2007 by the Getty Museum of Los Angeles: the masterpiece is the Trapezophoros (325-300 B.C.), support of a ritual canteen with two grifos that cling a deer, next to an archaic marble Apollo of the first century AD. Free admission; from Tuesday to Sunday 10-12 and 16.30-18.30 (times subject to change).

  • Where Via Santa Maria del Popolo 68, Ascoli Satriano
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 1-1.5 hours
  • Tourist portal page www.touringclub.it

Parco archeologico dei Dauni

History and architecture · Archaeology · Free entry · Viewpoint

Open-air museum on the gentle slopes of the Serpent Hill, beyond the Roman bridge over the Carapelle River: the route crosses the archaeological evidences of the oldest past of the city. Free admission; from Tuesday to Friday and Sunday 8-13.30 and 15-16 (times subject to change).

  • Where Largo M.T. di Lascia 1, Ascoli Satriano
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 1-1.5 hours
  • Tourist portal page www.touringclub.it

Sources: touringclub.it · interregaismart.regione.puglia.it

Cattedrale

Places of worship · Cathedrals

Ascoli has been the bishop's seat since the 11th century: the cathedral, of Romanesque and Gothic origins, was renovated until the Baroque age.

Centro storico

History and architecture · Squares and old town · Free entry

A labyrinth of alleys leads to feudal squares, among ancient houses, shops and palaces; the name "Satriano" was added in 1870 in memory of the nearby Satricum, destroyed during the Sannitic wars.
