What to see in Tivoli

The ancient Tibur on the Aniene Falls, with two UNESCO sites: Villa Adriana, residence of Emperor Hadrian (1999), and Villa d'Este with its 16th century fountain garden (2001); on the acropolis the Roman temples and, in the gorge, the park of Villa Gregoriana.

12 places to visit in Tivoli, in the province of Rome (Lazio). Every entry is checked against official sources.

Villa Adriana

History and architecture · Archaeology · UNESCO World Heritage · Paid entry · Viewpoint

Official residence built by the Emperor Hadrian around 126 AD, plundered during the invasions and restored from the nineteenth century: the Pecile with its bath, the baths, the canopy's nymph, the imperial palaces, the accommodation of servants and praetorians and the Maritime Theatre surrounded by a water canal. UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1999.

  • Where Tivoli
  • Entry paid
  • Suggested time 2 hours or more
  • Tourist portal page villae.cultura.gov.it

Sources: turismoroma.it · villae.cultura.gov.it · whc.unesco.org

Villa d'Este

History and architecture · Palaces and villas · UNESCO World Heritage · Paid entry · Viewpoint

Wanted by Cardinal Ippolito II d'Este, governor of Tivoli in the middle of the sixteenth century: frescoed rooms by Mannerist painters, including the Salone della Fontana by Girolamo Muziano, and the Italian garden with fountains fed only by gravity by Pirro Ligorio, such as the Bicchierone, the ovate, the Dragons, the Cento Fontanelle and the Organ. UNESCO heritage since 2001.

  • Where Tivoli
  • Entry paid
  • Suggested time 2 hours or more
  • Official website villae.cultura.gov.it
  • Tickets Tickets on the official website

Sources: turismoroma.it · villae.cultura.gov.it · midaticket.it

Santuario di Ercole Vincitore

History and architecture · Archaeology · Paid entry · Viewpoint

Great sanctuary of the second century BC dedicated to the patron god of Tibur, 188 for 140 meters on terraces above the Aniene, with a theatre for about 3,600 spectators, a piazza porticata and the temple; the Via Tecta crossed it in a vaulted gallery. It was abandoned in the 6th century.

  • Where Tivoli
  • Entry paid
  • Suggested time 1-1.5 hours
  • Tourist portal page villae.cultura.gov.it

Sources: turismoroma.it · villae.cultura.gov.it

Villa Gregoriana

Nature and landscape · Parks and nature reserves · Paid entry · Viewpoint

Natural park wanted by Pope Gregory XVI around 1834, with natural and artificial waterfalls, caves, terraces and archaeological remains; it is managed by FAI and is visited from March to December in about an hour and a half.

Tempio di Vesta

History and architecture · Archaeology · Free entry · Viewpoint

Small round temple of the first century BC on the acropolis: ten of the eighteen Corinthian columns remain; perhaps dedicated to Vesta or Tiburno, mythical founder of the city.
