What to see in Rolo

Municipality of the lower north-eastern Reggiana, from the fifteenth century to 1776 small autonomous manor of the Sixes; between the eighteenth and twentieth centuries it was known for the inlaid furniture, the 'roleini', documented today by the Museum of Tarsia, while the center is gathered along the portico of Corso Repubblica with the church of San Zenone.

Museo della tarsia

Museums · Themed

Born to document the tradition of the wooden tarsia of the Rolese, bloomed between the eighteenth and twentieth centuries with the round tables called "Rolini" , requested in the seventeenth century throughout the Duchy of Modena and Reggio and then sold abroad. It exhibits ancient carpenter tools, original documents and some furniture; it is also documentation center with library specialized in wood art and restoration and a photographic archive on the inlaid furniture of Rolo. According to the page of the City is visitable by reservation.

Chiesa di San Zenone

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches · Free entry

Church mentioned already in 1070 among the possessions of Bonifacio di Canossa; it preserves late-Doroma traces along the sides, eighteenth-century furnishings in local tarsia and the canvas of the Madonna della Pace, of the school of Rodolfo del Ghirlandaio. Since 1446 the Six had the right to appoint their archpriests.

  • Where Corso Repubblica, Rolo
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes
  • Tourist portal page emiliaromagnaturismo.it

Sources: emiliaromagnaturismo.it · comune.rolo.re.it

Corso Repubblica

History and architecture · Squares and old town · Free entry

The main street and the heart of the village, with two rows of porticoes; there are the Town Hall, home of the Tarsia Museum, and the parish church of San Zenone. In the schools there is a fifteenth-century plaque, coming from the demolished castle, dedicated to the Sexes.

  • Where Corso Repubblica, Rolo
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes
  • Tourist portal page emiliaromagnaturismo.it

Sources: emiliaromagnaturismo.it
