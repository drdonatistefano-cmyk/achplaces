What to see in Soglio

Small town of Astigiano, born in 1416 around the castle of Pelletta, which preserves medieval walls of the 14th-XV century; on the nearby hill stands the church of San Pietro in Vincoli, rebuilt in 1709 and a long pilgrimage sanctuary.

Castello di Soglio

History and architecture · Castles and fortresses

In an elevated position in the center of the village, it probably dates back to the 12th century: in 1252 it passed to the Biandrates, who gave it to Asti, and from 1285 the fief was of the Pelletta; from 1807 the Crova were the only owners until the extinction of the family in 1840. The east and north sides have walls from the 14th to the 15th century with two ogival windows and a padded terracotta frame; renovated in the 18th century, it has an Italian garden in front of the façade and above the entrance the stone coat of arms Pelletta-Asinari. The municipality does not indicate how to visit.

Chiesa di San Pietro in Vincoli

Places of worship · Sanctuaries

Built in 1709 on the site of an older church and a cemetery, it was a sanctuary and a destination for pilgrimages, with walls covered with ex-vote; in 1816 Pius VII granted plenary indulgence for the two feasts of St. Peter. The altar in stucco has dorsal and summit on tortile columns, flanked by the busts of San Grato and Sant'Orsola, patrons of Soglio. The festival is celebrated on the first Sunday of August.
