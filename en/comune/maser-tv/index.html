What to see in Maser

At the foot of the Asolani Hills, Maser is the village of the Villa Barbaro, designed by Andrea Palladio for the brothers Daniele and Marcantonio Barbaro and frescoed by Paolo Veronese, UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1996 with the Palladian villas of Veneto; next stands the Palladian Temple of 1580, and among the hamlets of Coste, Crespignaga and Muliparte there are ancient churches, oratories and the hilly path of the two Fortress.

Villa di Maser (Villa Barbaro)

History and architecture · Palaces and villas · UNESCO World Heritage

Wanted by the brothers Daniele and Marcantonio Barbaro, commissioned between 1554 and 1560 and designed by Andrea Palladio, who inspired himself to the Roman temples for the facade; the central body is decorated by the fresco cycle of Paolo Veronese (1559-1561), while the stuccoes are attributed to Alessandro Vittoria. Behind the building there is the water lilies wanted by Marcantonio Barbaro; the side boats, intended for agricultural activities, end with two dovecotes decorated with sundials. The visit to the frescoes includes the six rooms painted by Veronese; the villa has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1996.

Tempietto Barbaro

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches

A circular church designed by Palladio at the end of his career, around 1580 by Marcantonio Barbaro, who wanted to give Maser a new parish church; it rises at the foot of the villa, oriented to the east, and the frieze shows the date 1580 with the names of the client and the architect. Tradition has it that Palladio died in Maser that year. You can see with the guided tour between Villa and Tempietto (Saturday, Sunday and Holidays, 1 hour and 15 minutes).

not open to the public: only guided tours and special openings upon reservation

Chiesa parrocchiale di San Tommaso a Coste

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches · Free entry

The primitive building probably dates back to the end of the 10th century; it was radically transformed at the end of the 17th century and expanded with the lateral aisles in the eighties of the 19th century. It contains a statue of St. Thomas in golden wood of the fifteenth century, a Deposition attributed to Jacopo Palma the Younger and works by Giuseppe Bernardi called the Torretto.

  • Where Piazza della Pieve, Coste di Maser
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes
  • Tourist portal page www.villadimaser.it

Sources: comune.maser.tv.it · villadimaser.it

Chiesa parrocchiale di San Bartolomeo a Crespignaga

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches · Free entry

Born as a hospice dedicated to St. James at the beginning of the thousand and then became a monastery, it was consecrated on 21 December 1776 in the present eighteenth century; the bell tower was erected at the beginning of the nineteenth century at the expense of the Querini. It has neoclassical frescoes by Giambattista Canal and an oil on the table of the school of Cima da Conegliano, perhaps by Girolamo Santacroce.

  • Where Crespignaga, Maser
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes
  • Tourist portal page www.villadimaser.it

Sources: comune.maser.tv.it · villadimaser.it

Chiesa di San Paolo a Maser

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches · Free entry

High to parish between the Three and the fifteenth century, it has a structure probably sixteenth century, the eighteenth-century appearance and a restoration of 1863; it preserves paintings including a Martyrdom of Saint Catherine of the Veronese school.

Cà Nani, Municipio

History and architecture · Palaces and villas

Villa belonged to the Nani, then to the Trieste and the Fanzago, sold to the Municipality in 1927 and restored in the eighties of the twentieth century; it has an eighteenth-century sundial and the oratory of San Giovanni Battista, documented since 1686. Visit during the opening hours of the municipal offices.

  • Where Piazzale Municipio 1, Maser
  • Entry To be checked
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes

Chiesetta di San Giorgio

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches · Free entry · Viewpoint

Probably built between the 8th and the 10th century, rebuilt around 1536 and in 1906 by the will of the archpriest Antonio Gardin; it is reached on foot from the last stretch of Via San Vettore or from Forcella Mostaccin, along the path of the two Rocks.

  • Where Via San Vettore, Coste di Maser
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes
  • Tourist portal page www.villadimaser.it

Sources: comune.maser.tv.it · villadimaser.it

Sentiero delle due Rocche

Nature and landscape · Hills and trails · Free entry · Viewpoint

It runs along the ridge of the hills from the Rocca di Cornuda to the Rocca di Asolo, in about 5 hours of good walking without any particular difficulty, touching Mount Sùlder, Forcella Mostaccin, the church of San Giorgio, the former quarries of San Vettore and the Fornèt spring. The highest point is Collalto (496 m), behind Villa Barbaro, covered by a larch forest; on clear days the view goes from Grappa to Vette Feltrine, to the Euganean Hills, the lagoon and the sea.

  • Where Partenza dalla trattoria Al Cacciatore, verso Cornuda
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 2 hours or more
  • Tourist portal page www.villadimaser.it

Sources: comune.maser.tv.it · villadimaser.it
