What to see in Albugnano

Country of the Astigian hills, in whose territory rises the canonical of Santa Maria di Vezzolano, considered the most important Romanesque monument of Piedmont; from the Belvedere Motta, the highest point of the village, the view ranges to 360 degrees on the hills, and towards Berzano there is the Romanesque San Pietro di Fenestrella.

Abbazia di Santa Maria di Vezzolano

Places of worship · Abbeys and monasteries

Considered the most important Romanesque monument of Piedmont, it has been documented since 1095 (the foundation by Charlemagne in 773 is only legend) and combines Romanesque and Gothic forms. The façade alternates bricks and sandstone bands with statues of the archangels Michael and Raphael and of Christ; inside the pier of the late 12th century brings the bas-reliefs of the 35 patriarchs and stories of Mary. The cloister, one of the best preserved in Piedmont, has frescoes from the 13th and 15th centuries; since 2015 the complex has been entrusted to the Museum Pole of Piedmont.

Belvedere A. Motta – Il Balcone sul Monferrato

Nature and landscape · Landscapes · Free entry · Viewpoint

It is the highest point in Albugnano, at about 550 meters, with 360 degrees views of the Asti hills and the mountains in the background. Here stood a medieval castle dismantled in 1401, of which minimal traces remain; it houses the Park of the Remembrance with the cippi of the fallen of the world wars.

  • Where Via Regina Margherita, Albugnano
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes
  • Tourist portal page www.italia.it

Sources: comune.albugnano.at.it · italia.it

Chiesa di San Pietro di Fenestrella

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches

Also known as San Pietro al Camposanto, it is the oldest church in the territory, probably before the thousand: born as parish of the village of Fenestrella, it then became a cemetery church. Rectangular hall with semicircular apse, façade with stone and brick bands and frame with woven hanging arches; the side walls were raised in bricks around 1690.
