What to see in Civitavecchia

Port of Rome founded by Traiano as Centumcellae, dominated by the sixteenth-century Fort Michelangelo; a stone's throw away is the National Archaeological Museum in customs of Clement XIII, the historic center around Leandra Square and, on the hills, the Roman Taurine Thermal Baths and the hot tubs of Ficoncella.

Forte Michelangelo

History and architecture · Castles and fortresses · Free entry

Also known as Fortezza Giulia or Rocca Nova, it was wanted by Giulio II, who laid the first stone on 14 December 1508, and completed in 1537 under Paul III, on a structure of imperial age. Bramante worked on the project, then Antonio da Sangallo il Giovane, Michelangelo (since 1535, for a part) and finally Domenico Zanobio (1538-1539), who enlarged the mastium. About 100 × 120 meters, four corner towers of 21 meters in diameter and an octagonal mast over 23 meters high; underwent the Spanish siege of 1527, the attack of Barbarossa in 1544 and the bombings of the Second World War.

You can visit only the inner square, Saturday and Sunday 10-13 and 16-19; guided tours only in a few days (Cost Guard, tel. 0766 19431)

Museo archeologico nazionale di Civitavecchia

Museums · Archaeological · Paid entry

In the eighteenth-century pontifical customs building commissioned by Clement XIII, a state museum since 1970 heir to the Civic Museum of 1918, on four floors: on the ground floor a Roman copy of Athena Parthenos and the Apollo-Helios, considered a great replica of the Colossus of Rhodes; then the territory from prehistory to the Etruscans (Sanctuary of Punta della Vipera and cult of Menerva), Centumcellae traianea with coastal villas and Terme Taurine, Lviv-Cencelle founded in 854 and finds from archaeological seizures. Tactile plastics, bilingual panels and contents in LIS; open 8:30-19:30, closed on Monday; free on the first Sunday of the month.

  • Where Largo Cavour 1, Civitavecchia
  • Entry €3, reduced €2

  • Official website www.italia.it

Terme Taurine o di Traiano

History and architecture · Archaeology · Free entry · Viewpoint

Roman thermal complex on an isolated hill about 5 km from the center, overlooking the Tyrrhenian Sea. Built in the Sillan Age (90-78 BC) on sources already used by the Etruscans, it reached the maximum development with Trajan and was expanded under Hadrian: a republican part in opus reticulatum and an imperial one in brick, with apodyterium, caldarium, frigidarium, tepidarium, library and gym. Rutilio Namaziano described it in 416 A.D.; it was abandoned during the Greek-Gothic war. In the park the botanical garden Horti Traianei; management Pro Loco, open every day 9:30-13.

  • Where Giardino botanico delle Terme Taurine, SP7b, Civitavecchia
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 2 hours or more
  • Official website www.italia.it

Terme della Ficoncella

Local life and traditions · Spas · Paid entry · Viewpoint

Establishment about 4 km from the city, on a limestone hill, which takes its name from a large fig tree; frequented since ancient times as an alternative to the Taurine Thermal Baths. Five natural pools with clear and slightly bitter waters from 30 to 56°C, open all year round from 8 to 20 (until midnight from 12 June to 31 August); tickets only on site.

Piazza Leandra e Chiesa della Stella

History and architecture · Squares and old town · Free entry

Historical heart of the city, with a medieval atmosphere, with an ancient restored fountain; the name is linked to the legend of the sailor Leandro or perhaps to the oleander. The Church of the Stella (or the Virgin of the Graces) is one of the oldest in the city, founded in 1274 by San Bonaventura and 1688 by the Archconfraternity of the Gonfalone, with twentieth century frescoes by Ennio Galice and the statue of the Madonna delle Grazie taken in procession on September 8.

  • Where Piazza Leandra, Civitavecchia
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes
  • Tourist portal page www.italia.it

Affreschi della Stanza di Eliodoro

Local life and traditions · Hidden corners

In an apartment on Piazza Leandra, discovered in 1972 behind a wall during a renovation: tempera paintings that reproduce the Room of Eliodoro di Raffaello and his school in the Vatican (Messa di Bolsena, Liberation of St. Peter, Meeting of Leo Magno with Attila, Hunting of Eliodoro). The origin is discussed: the age of Raphael, the late eighteenth century or the local painter Tommaso Bigatti (1760-1834).

guided tours only by appointment (tel. 0766 28493)

Chiesa dell'Orazione e della Morte

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches · Free entry

Baroque Church of the Confraternity of Death and Prayer, which buried the abandoned dead, built since 1693 (a project attributed to Mattia de Rossi or Carlo Fontana) and survived the bombings. Greek cross plan with elliptical hall, stuccoes from 1699-1703, wooden statues from the seventeenth century, the dome frescoed by Giuseppe Errante (1786-1788), a balustrade of the organ obtained from the prison of St. Peter of the Battle of Lepanto and the neo-Gothic chapel Guglielmi with monument of Giulio Tadolini.

  • Where Via Gabriele D'Annunzio 23, Civitavecchia
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes
  • Tourist portal page www.italia.it

Sources: comune.civitavecchia.rm.it · italia.it

Cattedrale di San Francesco d'Assisi

Places of worship · Cathedrals · Free entry

The seat of the diocese of Civitavecchia-Tarquinia, built in 1610, rebuilt in 1782 for Clement XIV on a project by Francesco Navone and cathedral since 1805; damaged in 1943 and restored in 1950 by Plinio Marconi. Baroque facade with double ionic order with statues of Faith and Justice by Pietro De Laurentiis; inside a Nativity attributed to the school of the Domenichino and the organ Pinchi restored in 2019.

  • Where Piazza Vittorio Emanuele II, Civitavecchia
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes
  • Tourist portal page www.italia.it

Cinta muraria di Urbano VIII e Fontana del Vanvitelli

History and architecture · Monuments and memorials · Free entry

The wall erected in 1630 by Urban VIII north of the Fort, to separate the port from the city, rests on buildings of the Roman port and preserves the fisheries in opus reticulatum. The travertine fountain of Luigi Vanvitelli, commissioned in 1740 by Benedict XIV and completed in 1743, depicts the head of an old faun in a canopy with curved tympanum; the complex has been recently restored.

  • Where Calata della Rocca, Civitavecchia
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes

Porto storico e Porta Livorno

History and architecture · Monuments and memorials · Free entry · Viewpoint

The port was born in 106 A.D. at the behest of Trajan as a port of call to the west, the Centumcellae of the numerous warehouses. The Porta Livorno, an ancient entrance of the port recently brought to light, is flanked by the statue of the samurai Hasekura Tsunenaga, which in November 1615 brought to the pope a proposal for a trade agreement between Japan and Mexico.
