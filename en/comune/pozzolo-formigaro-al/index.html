What to see in Pozzolo Formigaro

Country of the Alexandrian plain near the Scrivia, dominated by the castle born as a fortress around the 11th century and now seat of the Town Hall, with fifteenth-century frescoes by the Boxilio brothers.

Castello di Pozzolo Formigaro

History and architecture · Castles and fortresses

Born as a defensive fortress around the 11th century on an alluvial terrace overlooking the plain, it is now home to the Town Hall. The front of the entrance, of an effortless age, preserves the porta carraia and the housings of the drawbridge; in the fifteenth-century wing there is a wooden Madonna of the fifteenth century and the frescoes of the school of Dodecanese of the brothers Franceschino and Manfredino Boxilio (Madonna with Child, Saint Lucia, Saint Biagio, Saint Francis), torn from the church of San Sebastiano, today Our Lady of the Ghiare. Guided tours on request in Italian, English, German and French.

Chiesa di Santa Maria delle Ghiare

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches

It rises around the year 1000 outside the village as the chapel of the Augustinian Fathers dedicated to St. Michael, it is mentioned for the first time in a bull of Pope Celestine III of 1192 as property of the abbey of Santa Giustina of Sezzadio. In the 13th century it was enlarged and housed the humble nuns (from Santa Maria Vallechiara derives its current name); after their departure it remained for three centuries under the parish of San Martino. At the end of the sixteenth century the Romanesque complex was enlarged to the present size, in the eighteenth century the bell tower was added and in 1893 a restoration opened four lunettes and erected the porch in front with columns of an ancient convent portico. The FAI card mentions frescoes by several painters, some of the school of the brothers Boxilio di Castelnuovo Scrivia: St. Biagio, St. Lucia and St. Francis in the right camp, a Madonna with Child on the left.
