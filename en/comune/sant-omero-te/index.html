What to see in Sant'Omero

The Teramano hill centre mentioned already in 1173 in the catalogue of Norman Barons, with a nucleus gathered inside a ring of ring of ring and overlooking the valley; in its territory stands Santa Maria a Vico, a Romanesque church of the tenth century considered the oldest monument of Abruzzo almost intact.

Chiesa di Santa Maria a Vico

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches

Romanesque church whose origins date back to the 10th century, built where in the Trajan age there was a temple of the lovers of Hercules and the Emperor, as attested by the "Decree of the lovers of Hercules," epigraphed on the limestone walled inside. Three-nave basilica plant with semicircular apse, columns that hold round arches and light that grows from the rose window towards the presbytery; the facade has a rare masonry opus spicatum and transenna in travertine and terracotta with Greek crosses and of St. Andrew. Restored in 1885 and in 1970-71, when under the apse emerged traces of a larger apse considered early Christian; it received the "Meraviglia Italiana" stamp. Open Saturday and Sunday 8-18 secondo italia.it.

Centro storico di Sant'Omero

History and architecture · Squares and old town · Free entry · Viewpoint

The central core is enclosed by a ring of ring road and can be reached by several routes, including a staircase with views of the valley; between the streets there is the small Largo Alvaro de Mendoza, named after the Spanish feudalist to whom in 1644 Sant'Omero and Poggio Morello were donated, and a balcony road closed by a railing overlooks the valley. The village appears in 1173 in the catalogue of Norman barons, as a fief of Gualtieri di Rainaldo.

Borgo di Poggio Morello

History and architecture · Villages · Free entry

Fraction on a hill near the Salinello valley, 184 meters above sea level and about 10 km from the sea, an autonomous municipality until the French decade (1806-1815); in 1612 Muzio de' Muzij listed it among the castles surrounded by walls. There are still sections of the belt with sharp arches and the ancient entrance door, the Palazzo Striglioni, built between the nineteenth and early twentieth centuries with rooms, gate and railings in Art Nouveau style, and twentieth-century religious newsstands; in the church, recovered since 1996 with interior decorations by Renato Coccia, was restored a seventeenth-century statue of Saint Lucia in painted terracotta.

Le pinciare

Local life and traditions · Hidden corners · Free entry

Rural houses in raw earth built with poor materials found on the spot: walls of earth, clay, straw, chaff and pebbles, roof of tiles in brick on a frame of reeds sealed with mud; the name would come from the dialect "li pinge" the tiles. Their period of maximum diffusion was probably the beginning of the nineteenth century, when under the dominion of Murattiano the lands of the convents were distributed to the citizens; some exemplars are still seen walking through the country roads of the territory.
