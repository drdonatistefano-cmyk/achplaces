What to see in Massazza

Small town of Biellese on a spur of the barracks, dominated by the castle (Rocca dei Cavallari), fortified village castled around the year 1000 with high stone tower, viscontea rock and chapel with fourteenth-century frescoes; in the village the seventeenth-century parish church of Santa Maria Assunta with the memories of the Avogadro.

Castello di Massazza (Rocca dei Cavallari)

History and architecture · Castles and fortresses

Settling perched on a bargage spur, probably Celto-ligure and then Roman, became Lombard fortification and castle around the year 1000: defensive village where food and flocks were kept. It preserves Celtic ares with votive cups, a high stone tower, a viscontea fortress with ghibelline merlons and monofores added by the Visconti in the fourteenth century, and the chapel of Saints Antoninus and Anna with Romanesque apse and fourteenth-century frescoes. It was the Guala di Casallone (1200), the Visconti (1300) and the Avogadro (1400-1800); since 1978 it is of the Cavallari family, who restores it as a dwelling and uses it in part for events, exhibitions and fairs.

Parrocchiale di Santa Maria Assunta

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches

Rebuilt in the 17th century on the site of a sixteenth-century church, it has a rectangular plan with two side chapels adorned with eighteenth-century canvases and a large porch facing the facade. A marble tomb with coat of arms recalls the burial of the Avogadro, feudal lords of the place; the organ is of 1814, the work of Giacinto Bruna of Migliano. The bell tower, restored in the early eighteenth century, is made of stone in the lower part and brick with two orders of mullioned windows at the top.
