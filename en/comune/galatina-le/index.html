What to see in Galatina

Galatina lived the greatest splendor between the fourteenth and fifteenth centuries under the Orsini del Balzo, which is due to the Basilica of Santa Caterina d'Alessandria with its cycle of frescoes. In the ancient village, between three doors and eighteenth-century palaces, the Mother Church of Saints Peter and Paul and the Chapel of Saint Paul tied to tarantism; here the pastry was born.

Basilica di Santa Caterina d'Alessandria

Places of worship · Basilicas

Franciscan Basilica in Romanesque-Apulian style built in 1384 by Raimondello Orsini del Balzo, a national monument since 1886. The three aisles are entirely frescoed, almost all in the fifteenth century on the commission of Mary of Enghien: Apocalypse, Seven Sacraments, Genesis, Life of Christ, Stories of the Virgin and, in the presbytery, Stories of Saint Catherine; there is also an octagonal chapel of the fifteenth century. A recent restoration has involved roof, windows, lighting, cloister and bell tower, and in the former refectory there is a museum.

Chiesa Madre dei Santi Pietro e Paolo

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches

Church built in 1335, rebuilt from 1633 and completed in 1770 in Baroque Lecce, with monumental Rococo façade rich in friezes, niches and statues. Inside, frescoes, altars in polychrome marble and paintings, the Captello del Sacramento of the second half of the seventeenth century, the vault frescoed at the end of the nineteenth century with the life of St. Peter and the stone on which, according to tradition, St. Peter rested, brought here in 1670.

Cappella e pozzo di San Paolo

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches

A small single nave chapel in Palazzo Tondi-Vignola, built in 1789 where a well was the destination of those who had been bitten by poisonous animals. Until a few decades ago, at the dawn of June 29, the feast of Saints Peter and Paul, arrived there the "tarantate" to ask for healing and drink the water of the well: a ritual linked to the origins of the pinch. On the altar, after a long restoration, the canvas of Saint Paul by Francesco Saverio Lillo is back. Info: IAT Galatina, tel. +39 0836 569984.

Borgo antico e porte

History and architecture · Squares and old town · Free entry

You enter from three gates: Porta Luce to the west, with the coat of arms and inscription that recalls the elevation to the city in 1793, Porta Nuova to the north, surmounted by the statue of St. Peter, and Porta Cappuccini to the east, restored in 1803. Inside, streets with original basolato, baroque palaces with portals and eighteenth-century balconies, churches and convents from the late fifteenth to the eighteenth century, the Seat of Via Vittorio Emanuele II with the oldest version of the city coat of arms, Palazzo Orsini today seat of the municipality and the lamp called "la pupa" (the pupa). Italia.it recommends the walk especially in the evening; in a pastry shop in the center was born the pastry.

  • Where Via Vittorio Emanuele II, Galatina
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 1-1.5 hours
  • Tourist portal page www.italia.it

Sources: comune.galatina.le.it · interregaismart.regione.puglia.it · italia.it

Museo civico Pietro Cavoti

Museums · City history

Born in the thirties of the twentieth century on the initiative of Francesco Bardoscia and reopened in 1998 in the former convent of the Dominicans, today Palace of Culture. It exhibits artistic, archaeological, epigraphic and documentary material, medieval finds, a vast collection of drawings, notebooks, prints and photographs by Pietro Cavoti and spaces dedicated to the sculptor Gaetano Martinez and the Galatine artists.

Teatro Cavallino Bianco

History and architecture · Historic theatres

Theatre built between 1947 and 1949 by Antonio De Gioia on a former cellar near the station, with hotel and restaurant attached and 1,062 seats; the name comes from a operetta by Ralph Benatzky. Inaugurated on February 3, 1949 with the Rigoletto di Verdi, in 1980 he hosted the Living Theatre. Place: https://www.cavallinobiancogalatina.it, tel. +39 0836 633424.
