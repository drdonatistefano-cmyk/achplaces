What to see in Sant'Ambrogio di Valpolicella

Municipality of Valpolicella known for the red marble called "S." Ambrogio: on the hill of the village of San Giorgio, among vineyards, olive groves and marogne, stands the Longobard-Romanesque parish church with the 8th century ciborium, the cloister, an archaeological area of the Iron Age and the Museum-Antiquarium.

Pieve di San Giorgio di Valpolicella e chiostro

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches

Longobard-Romanesque church of the VII-VIII century, among the oldest places of worship of Veronese still in use, restored at the beginning of the XII century (Romanesque structures of the east side and bell tower), with three naves with two opposite apses. The most precious piece is the Longobard ciborium of the Master Bear and disciples, with inscriptions of the age of Liutprand (712-736); there are also frescoes of the 11th-XIV century, a Gothic tabernacle of 1433, an octagonal baptismal font of the 12th century obtained from a single block of marble and a Resurrection of Palma the Younger. From a small door on the south side you enter the cloister.

Borgo di San Giorgio

History and architecture · Villages · Free entry · Viewpoint

Stone houses gathered around the parish on a hill between vineyards and olive groves, with a wide view; the village is a frame for re-enactments like the traditional Festa delle Fae.

Area archeologica di San Giorgio

History and architecture · Archaeology

Reachable from the cloister, behind the eastern apse of the parish church, it preserves remains of buildings of the Iron Age and a water tank used in metal processing.

Museo-Antiquarium di San Giorgio

Museums · Archaeological

In front of the church and the square, it collects finds from prehistory to the Roman era found in the area and fossils preserved in the layers of sedimentary stone on which the village stands.

Marogne e sentieri di San Giorgio

Nature and landscape · Hills and trails · Free entry · Viewpoint

Terraced hills with the typical maroons of Valpolicella; the "Salute path" connects Gargagnago to San Giorgio and a network of routes leads to Ca' de la Pela, Monte Solane and Caranzano.
