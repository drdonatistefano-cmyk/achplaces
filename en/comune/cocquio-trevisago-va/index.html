What to see in Cocquio-Trevisago

Common at the foot of the Campo dei Fiori, on the border with Gemonio, where the seventeenth-century mill in which was born and worked the painter Innocente Salvini (1889-1979) is from 1983 house-museum and picture gallery; in the hamlet Torre remain the ruins of a military tower probably of the 11th century and above Caldana, at Cerro, a path in the woods exhibits wooden sculptures.

Museo Innocente Salvini - Mulino storico

Museums · House museums

The birthplace of the painter Innocent Salvini (1889-1979), who lived and worked all his life here, is a rare example of industrial archaeology of the seventeenth century with remains of an ancient medieval tower: two wheels and working gears moved by the stream Viganella, millstones, sieves and systems of wheat transport designed and automated by Salvini himself. Since 1983 it is a gallery: three rooms with rotating paintings with warm and bright colors on rural life, family and landscapes of the village, and frescoes en plein air in the courtyard; it houses exhibitions and events. Open from 1 April to 30 October Wednesday, Saturday and Sunday 15-18.

Sentiero delle sculture del Cerro

Nature and landscape · Hills and trails

Open-air museum in the woods, in an area of the Campo dei Fiori Park, with wooden sculptures made from trunks or branches broken by the local sculptor Sergio Terni: at the entrance the Guardiano del Bosco, then a squirrel with the acorn, the Witch of Cerro, the Dea Berta with his duck, a spider, a gnome, a viper, an owl and a dove, with other strains in processing. The route is flat, no longer than 800 meters long, very easy and suitable for children; from Caldana you climb to Cerro with path 17 and you can continue to Orino.

  • Where Via Castagneto 2, 21034 Caldana, Cocquio-Trevisago

  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes
  • Tourist portal page www.in-lombardia.it

Sources: in-lombardia.it

Torre di Trevisago

History and architecture · Towers

Remains of a tower originally over fifteen meters high, probably of the 11th century, which gives the name to the hamlet Tower: military outpost to control people and goods in transit from Seprio to the subalpine localities, signaled the dangers to the other garrisons with fires visible from afar. It was destroyed and rebuilt several times, it was further damaged during the Italian Wars (1494-1559); not far away stood the church of San Giorgio with a small cemetery, last documented in 1758. Reported in the FAI census

the FAI card describes the remains as dilapidated and asks for their safety; visitability not indicated
