What to see in Pietrabruna

Borgo della Valle di San Lorenzo, between woods and olive groves, born around the Romanesque church of San Gregorio and belonged for centuries to Porto Maurizio, which bought it in 1162; it is home to the stroscia, ancient oil cake, and includes the hamlets of Boscomare, with the open-air museum of murals, and Torre Paponi.

Chiesa romanica di San Gregorio Magno

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches

Shortly before the village, among the olive trees, the Romanesque church is dated from the DMO to 1003; according to the Province the first settlement developed around San Gregorio, lower than the present country.

Centro storico, parrocchiale di San Matteo e Oratorio della Santissima Annunziata

History and architecture · Villages · Free entry

The old town has slate portals surmounted by bas-reliefs and inscriptions. The parish church of Saints Matthew and Gregory is in neoclassical forms (the Province dates back to the 12th century); the Oratory of the Santissima Annunziata houses a canvas of the Annunciation dated 1545; in the village there is also the small church of San Rocco.

  • Where Centro storico, Pietrabruna
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes
  • Tourist portal page rivieradeifiori.travel

Museums · Themed

Boscomare, museo a cielo aperto

Local life and traditions · Hidden corners · Free entry

In the hamlet of Boscomare the alleys house the open-air museum of Pietrabruna, with paintings and colorful murals of artists; there are also the parish church of San Bernardo, the Oratory of the Annunziata with a frescoed wall and wooden inlays, and the remains of a 15th century watch tower linked to Turkish-barbarish raids.

  • Entry Free

Torre Paponi, parrocchiale dei Santi Cosma e Damiano

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches

In the hamlet of Torre Paponi, the Baroque parish church of Santi Cosma e Damiano overlooks a small square with a colorful cobblestone; in the hamlet there are also the Oratory of the Annunziata, the Museum of Sacred Art and Confraternities and the Museum of Jar.
