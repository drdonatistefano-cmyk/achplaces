What to see in Vado Ligure

The ancient Vada Sabatia, cited by Strabone and Pliny and Roman road and maritime node, then bishop's and Genoese piazzaforte with the eighteenth-century forts of San Giacomo and Santo Stefano; in the twentieth century lived and worked the sculptor Arturo Martini, whose original plasters are exposed to Villa Groppallo.

Villa Groppallo, Civico Museo «Arturo Martini»

Museums · Modern art · Free entry

Villa built in 1693 by Nicolò Firpo, enlarged around 1725 by Gio Batta Zerbino for Cesare De Mari and passed in the mid-nineteenth century to the Groppallo; purchased by the Municipality in 1973, restored in 1975-1979 and opened in 1982, with new layout of 2015. It exhibits the legacy of Don Cesare Queirolo (Roman marbles, including a panther of the first century A.D., and 434 coins), the works of the Vado Ligure Prize (1951-1963) and, in the main hall, the original plasters of Arturo Martini, who lived in Vado from 1920 to 1934: the figures of the Monument to the Fallen, Free admission, guided tours by reservation.

  • Where Via Aurelia 72, Vado Ligure
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 1-1.5 hours
  • Tourist portal page comune.vado-ligure.sv.it

Monumento ai Caduti di Arturo Martini

History and architecture · Monuments and memorials · Free entry · Viewpoint

It was created in 1923-1924, and was the first major public commission of Arturo Martini, who won the City's competition in front of a jury with Leonardo Bistolfi and Eugenio Baroni. It combines a four-figure pyramid in bronze, The War, The Victory, History and The Sacrifice (Pastor Fido), whose casts are in Villa Groppallo.

  • Where Lungomare Giacomo Matteotti, Vado Ligure
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes
  • Tourist portal page comune.vado-ligure.sv.it

Forte San Giacomo

History and architecture · Castles and fortresses

Built from 1757 to 1759 in place of the old fort of San Lorenzo sul Capo di Vado, started by the architect Antonio Federico Flobert and completed by engineer Decotte, who connected him to the fort of Santo Stefano with external walls and underground passages. It was used by Napoleon's French troops during the Alpine War and the first campaign in Italy (1792-1797).

Sentiero dei forti da Vado verso Noli

Nature and landscape · Hills and trails · Free entry · Viewpoint

Stage 18 of the Liguria Path (10,3 km, 3 hours and 15, 413 m difference in altitude): behind the Cruise terminal of Savona-Go with the red triangle marker, climb to the Fort San Giacomo, then for a steep staircase between the brooms to the ruins of the fort of Santo Stefano and along the walls of the battery of San Sebastiano, continue on the botanical path and in the cork grove towards Mount Sant'Elena.

  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 2 hours or more

Chiesa di San Giovanni Battista

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches · Free entry

The eighteenth-century church with a single nave: it houses the Presentation at the Temple of Domenico Bocciardo (1720), the Baptism of Jesus by Anton Maria Maragliano, the Transit of Saint Joseph of the Genoese school of the eighteenth century, two paintings by Francesco Baracca in the presbytery, the Sacred Heart by Luigi Venzano and a canvas by Giannetto Fieschi from 1965 in the choir.

  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes

Chiesa di San Maurizio a Segno

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches · Free entry

In the hamlet of Segno, it dates back to the 10th century and was rebuilt in 1711 in Baroque forms; it retains an Annunciation attributed to the Cambiaso, the Baptism of Christ of Barabino, eighteenth-century statues of the Madonna del Rosario, St. Anthony of Padua and St. Maurizio and a wooden Crucifix attributed to Maragliano.

Chiesa di Sant'Ermete

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches · Free entry

Small rectangular church with semicircular apse and a nave on the right side; the apse and the walls of the left side, perhaps of the XI-XII century, are the oldest parts of the original Romanesque building, then very transformed.

Chiesa di Nostra Signora della Visitazione (Madonna delle Grazie)

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches · Free entry

It rises where in 1634 an oratory was built at the Madonna delle Grazie, on a more ancient chapel; venerated by fishermen and sailors of Porto Vado, parish since 1952, restored in 1950-1960. It preserves a bell of 1642, an eighteenth-century marble altar from the demolished church of San Filippo Neri of Savona and the wooden group of the Visitation of Giuseppe Runggaldier (1929). Look at the sea and the fishermen's beach.

  • Where Via Aurelia 386, Vado Ligure
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes
  • Tourist portal page comune.vado-ligure.sv.it
