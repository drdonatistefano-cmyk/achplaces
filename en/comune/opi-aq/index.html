What to see in Opi

Mountain village on a rocky spur at 1,250 meters, overlooking the valley of Sangro in the National Park of Abruzzo, Lazio and Molise, with alleys portraited also by Escher, the church of Santa Maria Assunta and the Museum of Chamois; under the village the Val Fondillo, the greenest valley of the Park, in whose reserve area there is the largest of the old beech woods of the Park recognized World Heritage.

Chiesa di Santa Maria Assunta

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches

Between the new center and the ancient nucleus, of medieval origin, repeatedly damaged by earthquakes and rebuilt in the current forms in the seventeenth century; the bell tower, restored in 1556, preserves the Romanesque structure of the twelfth century and a plaque with the inscription "sacerdos cerealis" .

Cappella gentilizia di San Giovanni Battista

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches

On the central square, dedicated to the patron saint: built in the early seventeenth century in Baroque style by the noble Vincenzo Rossi, with wooden decorations, stuccoes and polychrome marbles reappeared with recent restorations.

Museo del camoscio e area faunistica

Museums · Science and university · Viewpoint

Three rooms in a noble palace built by the Bevilacqua family between the late seventeenth and early eighteenth centuries: the first documents the chamois (with two naturalized specimens, panels, footprints and a plastic of the Park), the second documentary project on the Park, the third is dedicated to other protected areas. Outdoors a wildlife area of about 20 hectares with camos in semi-freedom, observed by two belvederi in the upper part of the country. According to the MiC is open every day from 15 June to 15 September, on weekends and holidays in the rest of the year.

Val Fondillo

Nature and landscape · Parks and nature reserves · UNESCO World Heritage · Free entry · Viewpoint

Ice amphitheatre at about 1,000 meters, the greenest valley of the Park, between Monte Amaro (1,862 m) and Monte Dubbio, united by the Serra delle Gravare. Paths lead to the five springs of the torrent Fondillo, to the Briganti Refuge, to the Grotta delle Fate and to the Passaggio dell'Orso; from the Forest Museum the ascent to Monte Amaro takes about two and a half hours. In the reserve area of the valley there is the largest of the old beech woods of the Park inscribed in the UNESCO World Heritage Site; in 1994 and 1996 there was dug a necropolis with more than one hundred concentric tombs. You can go on horseback or donkey, archery and rent mountain bikes.

Museo della Foresta e dell'Uomo (Vecchia Segheria)

Museums · Themed

In the ancient sawmill where the beech of the valley was worked, today the Park's visit centre: it tells the history of the Val Fondillo and the work of woodcutters and mule trackers, with information on trails and services; it hosts cultural events and workshops for children.
