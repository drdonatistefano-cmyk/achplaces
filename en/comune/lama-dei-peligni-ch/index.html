What to see in Lama dei Peligni

Country of the chamois in the Majella National Park, between the river Aventino and the slopes of the Monte Amaro: at the Visit Center there are the Archaeological Naturalistic Museum "Maurizio Locati" , the botanical garden "Michele Tenor" and the fauna area of the chamois; above the village there are the Cavallone Cave, between the highest visitable caves in Europe, and the Cave of Sant'Angelo.

Museo naturalistico archeologico «Maurizio Locati» e giardino botanico

Museums · Science and university

Museo-Laboratorio del Centro visita del Parco, dedicated to the Milanese zoologist who re-introduced suede here: two large halls with panels, dioramas, multimedia and finds. At the centre is the Abruzzo suede; showcases on traditional industries (wool, pasta) and the replica of a Majella cave with rock paintings; the archaeological section dedicated to F. Verlegia goes from prehistory to the Middle Ages, with Roman funeral epigraphs of imperial age and the cast of the Majella man, over 7,000 years old, from the Neolithic village of Fonterossi. Next to it, the botanical garden "Michele Tenor" with about 500 species, many endemic.

Area faunistica del camoscio

Nature and landscape · Parks and nature reserves · Viewpoint

Opened in 1990 for the re-introduction of the Apennine suede on Majella, with educational, scientific and conservation purposes: about five hectares divided into five fences with rocky walls, woods and clearings, and a veterinary center. You can see the chamois from close up; the guided tours are booked at the Visitor Center.

Grotta del Cavallone

Nature and landscape · Caves and geosites

karst cave over 2 km long, with 15 meters high entrance at 1,475 meters above sea level: among the highest visitable caves in Europe, explored since 1704 and open to tourists since 1893; 1,360 meters, with salt such as the enchanted Forest, the Hall of the Elephants, the lakes of Splendore and Ornella and the Pantheon. In 1904 Francesco Paolo Michetti took it as a model for the scene of the second act of the "Daughter of Iorio" by D'Annunzio. It is one of the geosites of the UNESCO Geopark of Majella; climbing with the Colle Rotondo-Cavallone cable car, reopened in 2022, or on foot in about two hours; guided tours in the summer months.

Grotta di Sant'Angelo

Places of worship · Sanctuaries · Free entry · Viewpoint

It opens at 985 meters above a deep ravine of Majella and is for centuries a place of devotion to the archangel, with remains of a Byzantine altar of the eighth century.

Chiesa dei Santi Nicola e Clemente e piazza Umberto I

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches · Viewpoint

Overlooking the panoramic Piazza Umberto I, built in 1589 according to the date on the bell tower: it is one of the few Renaissance churches of Abruzzo with a basilica plan, with a six-arched porch and portal and rose window decorated with angel heads. Inside a wooden pulpit with statues of 17th century saints attributed to Bartholomew Balcony and, in the sacristy, a silver Madonna of Nicola da Guardiagrele; behind the high altar the urn of the Holy Child, arrived from Jerusalem in 1760, celebrated on the third Sunday of September. Since 2015 it has been dedicated to the Child Jesus.

Chiesa e convento di Maria Santissima della Misericordia

Places of worship · Abbeys and monasteries

In the upper part of the village, also known as the convent of Sant'Antonio: founded in 1327 by Roberto da Salle, follower of Celestino V, and remodelled between 1530 and 1600. It contains manuscripts of Saint Alphonsus Maria de' Liguori, a letter from Saint Joseph Calasanzio, an Abruzzo copper cross of the fourteenth century and a thirteenth-century statue of Our Lady of Mercy.

Centro storico: Fontecannella e palazzi

History and architecture · Squares and old town · Free entry

At the entrance of the village the Fontecannella, a sixteenth-century white stone fountain of Majella; in the center the sixteenth-century Palazzo dei Baroni Tabassi and the nineteenth-century Palazzo Verlegia.
