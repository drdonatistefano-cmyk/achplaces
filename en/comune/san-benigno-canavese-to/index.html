What to see in San Benigno Canavese

The municipality of Canavese was founded around the Abbey of Fruttuaria, founded in 1003 by Guglielmo da Volpiano and rebuilt in the eighteenth century: you can visit the abbey church with the excavations of the first abbey and the mosaic of the griffons, the surviving Romanesque bell tower and the fifteenth-century shelter.

Abbazia di Fruttuaria

Places of worship · Abbeys and monasteries

The first stone was laid on 23 February 1003 by Guglielmo da Volpiano, in the presence of the Marquis Arduino; at its apogee, between XII and XIII century, the abbey had 85 churches in Italy and housed up to 1,200 monks. In 1770 the abbot Carlo Vittorio Amedeo delle Lanze had the church and Romanesque monastery demolished, saving only the bell tower, and the new church (1766) was designed by Vittone and Quarini between Baroque and Neoclassical. Under the floor the excavations of 1979 brought to light the foundations of the Romanesque church and a mosaic of 1066 with two griffins, which can be visited from 2004 in an underground path (fresh, cover). Closed in summer; being parish, it is advisable to telephone (338 4128795).

Torre campanaria dell'abbazia

History and architecture · Towers · Free entry

Only Romanesque part survived the eighteenth-century reconstruction of the abbey, stands for six floors in stone masonry marked by bands of hanging arches in terracotta, with narrow slits on the lower floors, a monofora in the fourth and pairs of double-fores in the fifth and sixth; inside a stone staircase rises up to the bell cell. You can see it from the outside.

  • Where Piazza Cardinale delle Lanze 1, San Benigno Canavese
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes
  • Tourist portal page turismotorino.org

Sources: comune.sanbenignocanavese.to.it · turismotorino.org

Ricetto e centro storico

History and architecture · Squares and old town · Free entry

One of the three doors, one of the corner towers and traces of other towers, remains of the medieval recipe dating back to the fifteenth century; on some facades there are still parts of double-fores and terracotta decorations. The old town includes the shelter and a larger area, bordered by the chapels of the four main villages.

  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes
