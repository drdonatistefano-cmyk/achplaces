What to see in San Potito Ultra

Small Irpino centre at 517 meters in the Salzola valley, 8 km from Avellino, already a farmhouse of Candida and autonomous municipality since 1860: in the underground of the eighteenth-century palace of the Barons Amatucci is located the Museum of Labor, next to the mother church of Sant'Antonio Abate.

Museo del Lavoro

Museums · Themed

Born from the collection donated between 2001 and 2004 to the Province of Avellino by the architects Eirene Sbrizilo and Enzo De Felice and then entrusted to the Municipality, collects in about 250 square meters tools and tools of work of more centuries, Neapolitan nativity scenes with the figures of the crafts (scalpellino, typographer, carpenter, tinker, barber, blacksmith) and scenes of rural life. It also houses the archive of the Amatucci barons, which documents the relationships between owners and settlers. It is located in the basement of the 18th century baronial palace, bought by the Municipality in the 1980s and today municipal seat.

Chiesa madre di Sant'Antonio Abate

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches · Free entry

Romanesque Romanesque church with a Latin cross with a hut façade, portal surmounted by two monofores and rose window, and an imposing quadrangular bell tower in front of the monument to the fallen; tradition wants it founded by the Gerosolimitani. Damaged at war and by the earthquakes of 1962 and 1980, it was returned to worship on December 7, 2003. Inside, the altar of the Annunciation with an eighteenth-century painting and a panel of 1701 on the burials of the Amatucci.

Chiesa di Sant'Antonio di Padova

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches · Free entry

An eighteenth-century church with a single nave with three altars, the largest inlaid marble, built next to an older bell tower (a stone at the base has the date 1607) that dominates the village; on the left of the entrance there is a stone fountain of 1701 coming from the sacristy. It was damaged by the earthquake of November 23, 1980, and reopened to worship on December 20, 1997; it traditionally houses a relic of the saint.
