What to see in Ischia

The main town of the island, between the port, born in 1854 from the opening of the ancient Lake of Bagni, and Ischia Ponte, dominated by the Aragonese Castle; around, the beaches of Lido, the bay of Cartaromana with the thermal springs in the sea and the Tower of Guevara.

Castello Aragonese

History and architecture · Castles and fortresses · Viewpoint

It rises on an islet of trachyte 113 meters high, joined to Ischia Ponte by a masonry bridge of about 220 meters. The first fortifications date back to 474 B.C. with Gerone I of Syracuse; in 1441 Alfonso V of Aragon made it a fortress with walls, towers and ramparts against pirates and dug into the rock a gallery of 400 meters. Inside churches, courtyards, convents and gardens, including the church of the Immaculate Church with the large dome and the Cathedral of the Assumption with frescoes of the sixteenth century; an elevator rises to 60 meters.

Museo diocesano di Ischia

Museums · Old masters

Founded on 5 March 1997 by Bishop Antonio Pagano, the section of sacred art is divided into five nuclei (marbles, sculptures, paintings, silvers, various objects) and preserves a cover of early Christian sarcophagus from the IV-V century. The archaeological section, of about 1,000 m2 with the furnaces and necropolis of Pithecusae, is located instead at the complex of Santa Restituta in Lacco Ameno.

Torre di Guevara

History and architecture · Towers · Viewpoint

Also known as Torre di Michelangelo and indicated by the Municipality as a symbol of Ischia, it rises in front of the Aragonese Castle on a large green lawn, a few meters from the Scogli di Sant'Anna.

  • Where Via Nuova Cartaromana, Ischia
  • Entry To be checked
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes
  • Tourist portal page www.italia.it

Sources: comune.ischia.na.it · italia.it

Baia di Cartaromana

Nature and landscape · Sea and beaches · Free entry · Viewpoint

To the south of the Aragonese Castle, among the Scogli of Sant'Anna and beyond the Tower of Guevara, it has black volcanic rocks shaped by the sea and natural thermal springs that allow hot baths even in winter; rich in posidonia, it is part of the archaeological area and at 5-7 meters deep preserves the remains of a foundry of lead and tin. You can reach it by a trail of about 700 meters from Via Cartaromana or by taxi boat.

  • Where Via Cartaromana, Ischia
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 2 hours or more
  • Tourist portal page www.italia.it

Sources: comune.ischia.na.it · italia.it

Spiaggia dei Pescatori e Lido di Ischia

Nature and landscape · Sea and beaches · Free entry · Viewpoint

The Lido is the long strip of beaches and coves that connects the port to Ischia Ponte, starting from the beach of San Pietro, where the boats used to dry up; the Beach of the Fishermen, of fine sand, arrives a few hundred meters from the jetty of the Castle, with facilities and recreational activities.

  • Where Lungomare tra Ischia Porto e Ischia Ponte
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 2 hours or more
  • Tourist portal page www.italia.it

Sources: comune.ischia.na.it · italia.it

Spiaggia degli Inglesi

Nature and landscape · Sea and beaches · Free entry · Viewpoint

In the area of Sant'Alessandro, about 800 meters from the port: little more than 200 meters of pebbles and sand, partly free and partly equipped, sheltered and with seabed that gradually descends. You can walk from the hill of Sant'Alessandro, from the Olympic Bank or by taxi boat.

  • Where Sant'Alessandro, Ischia
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 1-1.5 hours
  • Tourist portal page www.italia.it

Sources: comune.ischia.na.it · italia.it

Ischia Porto

History and architecture · Squares and old town · Free entry

The main port of the island was originally a lake, Lake of the Baths, open to the sea at the beach of Ferdinand II in 1854; today it is the most lively center, with restaurants, clubs and shops. In the third week of September, the Port Festival recalls the arrival of Frederick II and his dolphin.

  • Where Via Porto, Ischia
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 1-1.5 hours
  • Tourist portal page www.italia.it

Sources: italia.it
