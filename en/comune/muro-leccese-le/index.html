What to see in Muro Leccese

The centre of Salento was built on an ancient Messapian city, of which the megalithic walls (IV-III century BC) and numerous menhirs remain; in the village the Prince's Palace with the Museum of Borgo Terra, the Byzantine church of Santa Marina with the oldest cycle of St. Nicholas and the Baroque churches of the Annunziata and the Immaculate.

Palazzo del Principe

History and architecture · Palaces and villas · Free entry

Built in the second half of the fifteenth century on the remains of a medieval structure, already fortified castle to defend the ancient village; on the north side a moat dug into the rock about four meters deep, on the façade the coat of arms of the Protonobilissimo with the dragon. In the courtyard the Granarie pits and the pavement that follows the medieval walls emerged from the excavations; in the underground large monolithic tanks for oil and a room-prison with the graffiti of the prisoners; on the noble floor the apartments of the princes. Today it is the seat of the municipality and cultural space; free or paid entry depending on the event.

Museo Diffuso di Borgo Terra

Museums · City history

Inaugurated in 2004 in the Prince's Palace, where many finds had emerged during the restoration of 1999, followed by excavations at the University of Lecce. It exhibits objects, ceramics and instruments from the Messapian age to the Middle Ages and the Renaissance, with in the center of the hall a plastic of the village; it was the first example in Salento of valorization of an ancient village through its archaeological evidences. Open every day 9-13 and 16-20, closed on Tuesdays; access ramp, Braille guides, audioguides and tactile path.

Mura messapiche

History and architecture · Archaeology · Free entry

The belt, almost four kilometers long, contained 107 hectares, a second extension only to that of Ugento. The excavations of 1986-1992 identified three concentric walls, the first before 300 BC; the best preserved stretch is the north-eastern one, about 840 meters long, with the north gate. Open-air archaeological area accessible to all.

Menhir di Muro Leccese

History and architecture · Archaeology · Free entry

In the territory there are five menhirs: the Trice (430 cm), in the square of the same name near Santa Marina; the Cross of Sant'Antonio (420 cm), on the border with Sanarica; the Miggiano (about 200 cm), near the Byzantine church of Santa Maria di Miggiano; the Crucifix (140 cm), with two engraved crosses, near the church of the Crucifix; and the Yellows (130 cm), covered with lichens. Area diffused in the territory, accessible to all.

Chiesa di Santa Marina

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches

The oldest church in Muro is one of the oldest in the Otranto Land, dating back to the IX-XI centuries, partly built with the blocks of the Messapian walls. A single nave with semicircular apse, it contains frescoes from the XI-XV centuries, including a cycle on the life of St. Nicholas before 1087, dated from the Touring around 1040 and considered the oldest dedicated to the saint.

Chiesa dell'Annunziata (chiesa madre)

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches

Built between 1680 and 1693 on a more ancient sacred building, as shown by a frescoed crypt; Baroque façade in Lecce stone with statues of Our Lady and Angel Gabriel. The interior with three aisles is a real picture gallery: eighteenth-century canvases by Liborio Riccio (the expulsion of merchants from the Temple , , , The sacrifice of Abraham , and Serafino Elmo (including the stories of Saint Oronzo), altar major of the early seventeenth-century coming from the convent of the Dominicans, pulpit, choir and organ of the eighteenth-century.

Chiesa dell'Immacolata

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches

Built between 1778 and 1787, it is considered one of the wonders of the Lecce baroque: the large rococo façade, scenographic, is lengthened by two lateral arches. Inside, with a single nave with vault decorated with rococo stuccos, canvases on the life of the Virgin and the Immaculate altar; it is home to the brotherhood of the same name, active since the seventeenth century.

Chiesa del Crocifisso

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches

Begin in 1573 and completed in 1613 by the will of Cornelia Delli Monti, widow of the feudal lord Giovan Battista I Protonobilisssimo, on the ruins of a Byzantine church on the site of the medieval farmhouse of Brongo. The austere façade has two baroque portals finely carved; inside, with a Greek cross, three altars of the late seventeenth century and, on the main altar, a sculptural group of Placido Buffelli of Alessano.
