What to see in Roccamorice

A small village on the fishing side of Majella, linked to Pietro da Morrone, the future Celestine V: in its territory there are the Celestinian hermitages of Santo Spirito in Majella, the largest and most famous of the mountains, and San Bartolomeo in Legio, dug in the rock of the Vallone di Santo Spirito.

Eremo di Santo Spirito a Majella

Places of worship · Abbeys and monasteries · Viewpoint

The greatest and best known of the Majella hermitages: in 1053 the monk Desiderio, then Pope Victor III, who built a small church there; Pietro da Morrone found it destroyed in 1246 and started the restoration by adding an oratory and a cell. Today it includes church, sacristy, guesthouse and monastic complex, with wooden choir, paintings of the Madonna and the Descent of the Holy Spirit; under the church the cave of the first hermits, the room of the Crucifix and the cell where Celestine V prayed, and the Santa Scala rises to the oratory of the Maddalena, overlooking the valley. A community of young friars lives there; from August 29th to the first Sunday of October every Sunday forgiveness is celebrated with plenary indulgence.

Eremo di San Bartolomeo in Legio

Places of worship · Abbeys and monasteries · Viewpoint

At about 610 meters below a large rock roof in the Vallone di Santo Spirito, documented from 1275 and rebuilt by Fr.Pietro da Morrone, inhabited still in 1320: the oratory is almost all carved into the rock, with the Holy Staircase on the terrace, two frescoed squares of the thirteenth century on the façade (Madonna with Child and Christ blessing) and, on the sixteenth century altar, the wooden statue of St. Bartholomew; under a stone flows the water considered miraculous.
