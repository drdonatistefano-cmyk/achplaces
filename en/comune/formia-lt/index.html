What to see in Formia

Seaside town on the Gulf of Gaeta, the ancient Formiae: in the medieval village of Castellone the Roman Cisternone of the 1st century BC, the octagonal tower and the church of Sant'Erasmo; on the port of Mola the Angevin tower of 1289, on the Appian Way the Tomb of Cicero, the National Archaeological Museum with the statues of the forum and the park of Gianola with the villa of Mamurra.

Cisternone Romano

History and architecture · Archaeology

Built in the first century BC to supply Formiae, the fish ponds and villas of Cicero and Mamurra, more than 10 meters below the village of Castellone: about 1,200 m2 and 7,000 m3 of capacity, four naves divided by 60 pillars about 6.5 meters high with pseudo-cruise vaults, from which the nickname of underground Basilica was identified with precision in 1860 by Pasquale Mattej, it was probably model for the Mirable Pool of Bacoli.

  • Where Largo Sant'Anna, Formia
  • Entry To be checked
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes
  • Tourist portal page www.visitlazio.com

Sources: italia.it · visitlazio.com

Tomba di Cicerone

History and architecture · Archaeology · Viewpoint

Mausoleum of Augustian age on the Appian Way in Acervara, attributed by tradition to Marco Tullio Cicero, killed in Formia on December 7th 43 BC by the assassins of Marco Antonio, but without inscriptions that confirm it: tower about 24 meters high with square base and circular drum, two burial chambers superimposed and fenced in cross-linked work around the funeral garden. Managed by Sinus Formianus.

  • Where SS7 Via Appia, località Acervara, Formia
  • Entry To be checked
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes
  • Tourist portal page www.visitlazio.com

Sources: italia.it · parchilazio.it · visitlazio.com

Museo archeologico nazionale di Formia

Museums · Archaeological · Paid entry

Born in 1997 from the expansion of the Antiquarium, in a wing of the eighteenth century municipal palace called "Stalloni dei Borboni": male and female honorary statues of the forum, mostly between the 1st century BC and I AD, with Gaius Caesar nephew of Augustus, and the sculptures returned from the National Archaeological Museum of Naples; also architectural elements, funerary objects and mythological subjects from the maritime villas.

Open only Tuesday, Friday, Saturday and Sunday 8.30-19.30 (VisitLatium, from 6 November 2023)

  • Where Via Vitruvio 184, Formia
  • Entry €5, reduced €2 (18-25 years)
  • Suggested time 1-1.5 hours
  • Tourist portal page www.visitlazio.com

Sources: visitlazio.com

Castellone, Torre ottagonale e Teatro romano

History and architecture · Squares and old town · Free entry · Viewpoint

The medieval village was built in the early Middle Ages as a refuge from the Saracen incursions on the Roman Arce: the octagonal tower 25 meters high, commissioned in 1377 by Onorato I Caetani, is the only integral one of the twelve of the belt, destroyed by the bombings of 1943. A little below, the cavea of the Roman theatre of Augusta age rests on the hill facing the sea: today it is incorporated in over 70 houses, among which you can see columns, vaults and walls in cross-linked work.

  • Where Piazza Sant'Erasmo, Formia
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 1-1.5 hours
  • Tourist portal page www.visitlazio.com

Sources: italia.it · visitlazio.com

Chiesa di Sant'Erasmo

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches

It rises on the burial site of St. Erasmus, bishop martyr between the third and fourth centuries, and is mentioned by Gregory the Great as early as 590; bishop's seat until the Saracen incursions of the ninth century, then Benedictine monastery since 919, of Montecassino and Olivetani since 1491. Three aisles with Gothic pillars, the main baroque altar in polychrome marble, the Pendlebury organ of 1912 arrived from Yorkshire in 1988; under the floor the excavations of 1970-1976 revealed a Roman necropolis, the tomb of the martyr of the fourth century and a caroling crypt.

  • Where Via Olivetani 23, Formia
  • Entry To be checked
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes
  • Tourist portal page www.visitlazio.com

Sources: italia.it · visitlazio.com

Torre di Mola

History and architecture · Castles and fortresses · Viewpoint

A cylindrical tower up to 27 meters high built in 1289 by Charles II of Anjou as an outpost to defend Gaeta, with belt and two doors to land and to the sea; it rises on the remains of Roman baths, visible from a glass floor with calidarium and frigidarium. It was long of the Caetans, damaged in war and then restored with a Renaissance portal brought by Gaeta.

  • Where Via Abate Tosti 35, Formia
  • Entry To be checked
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes
  • Tourist portal page www.visitlazio.com

Sources: italia.it · visitlazio.com

Parco di Gianola e Monte di Scauri e Villa di Mamurra

Nature and landscape · Parks and nature reserves · Free entry · Viewpoint

One of the last stretches of unbuilt coast of the Gulf of Gaeta, between the Aurunci Mountains and the sea, with hills up to 123 meters of Monte di Scauri, broom and cyst spot, Aleppo pines on the cliffs and a cork grove; the butterfly Charaxes jasius flies there. Along the promontory, the remains of the villa of Mamurra, a formian knight and engineer of Caesar, with cisterns and caves are covered for hundreds of metres; part of the coast is a protected marine area.

  • Where Via del Porticciolo Romano 10a, Formia
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 2 hours or more
  • Tourist portal page www.visitlazio.com

Sources: italia.it · parchilazio.it · visitlazio.com

Nature and landscape · Sea and beaches · Free entry · Viewpoint
