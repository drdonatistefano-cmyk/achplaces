What to see in Marianopoli

An eighteenth-century country with an orthogonal plan founded, according to tradition, in 1726 with a colony of Albanian Greeks: in Piazza Garibaldi the church of San Prospero and the Regional Archaeological Museum with the findings of Monte Castellazzo, the ancient Mytistran, and Balate-Valle Oscura; there is also a Museum of Rural Civilization.

Museo Archeologico Regionale di Marianopoli

Museums · Archaeological · Free entry

Established in 1984 and part of the Archaeological Park of Gela, on two floors in Piazza Garibaldi tells the territory from prehistory to the Hellenistic age with the findings of the excavations 1977-1984: Neolithic ceramics and the bronze age, archaic indigenous ceramics of Greek forms often decorated with birds with fantails, two stele with Greek inscriptions by Balate, the accessories of the necropolis of Valle Oscura (VI century BC) and the Hellenistic one of Castellazzo with ceramic siceliotic red figures. Accessible with elevator.

  • Where Piazza Garibaldi, Marianopoli
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 1-1.5 hours
  • Tourist portal page www.comune.marianopoli.cl.it

Monte Castellazzo (antica Mytistraton)

History and architecture · Archaeology · Viewpoint

Hill of 831 meters east of the village, on the valleys of the Belici and the Salìto, inhabited by the Neolithic at the Iron Age; in the sixth century BC there arose an indigenous center then Hellenized, probably Mytistran, which resisted the Romans in the First Punic War. There remains a necropolis of the Copper Age, Greek tombs of the 4th century BC, the city walls, the Hellenistic acropolis and traces of the archaic town. It is reached from the crossroads Chibbò.

Piazza Garibaldi e Chiesa di San Prospero

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches · Free entry

The village has an orthogonal system with rectangular blocks; the eighteenth-century church of San Prospero, mother church, closes on the west side Piazza Garibaldi, center of the village.
