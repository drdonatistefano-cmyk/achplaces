What to see in Ischia di Castro

Borgo on a tuff cliff at the confluence of two streams, linked to the Farnese: the Farnese Rocca renovated by Antonio da Sangallo il Giovane, the archaeological museum "Pietro e Turiddo Lotti" and, in the wood, the ruins of Castro, capital of the Farnesian Duchy razed to the ground in 1649.

Museo civico archeologico «Pietro e Turiddo Lotti»

Museums · Archaeological · Paid entry

Born in 1958 as Antiquarium and re-arranged in 2005, chronological route from Prehistory to the Renaissance: silci and furniture art paleolithic of the cave of Settecannelle, Etruscan sculptures in nenfro of fantastic animals of the 6th century BC from the necropolis of Castro, a rare wooden case, Longobard furnishings of the Selvicciola, medieval sculptures of the cathedral of San Savino di Castro, six thirteenth-century frescoes of the rock hermitage of Poggio Conte and capitals from the buildings of the Sangallo of Piazza Maggiore di Castro. Touring hours: Tuesday 9-13, Thursday 9-13 and 16-20, Wednesday, Friday and Saturday 16-20; closed Monday and Sunday.

  • Where Piazza Cavalieri di Vittorio Veneto, Ischia di Castro
  • Entry paid
  • Suggested time 1-1.5 hours
  • Tourist portal page www.visitlazio.com

Rovine dell'antica città di Castro

History and architecture · Abandoned villages

With Paul III Farnese, in the sixteenth century, Castro became the capital of a duchy and was embellished with churches, palaces and walls also by Antonio da Sangallo the Younger; in 1649 the troops of Innocent X razed it to the ground. Today the ruins are covered by the forest: the excavations have brought to light the main square with the Palazzo della Zecca, the cathedral, the convent of San Francesco, the fortifications and Porta Lamberta; you arrive with a path marked by the road Ponte San Pietro-Farnese.

Rocca Farnese

History and architecture · Castles and fortresses

The oldest preserved Farnesian residence of the 13th century: the Renaissance renovation of Antonio da Sangallo the Younger remained unfinished; recently a Renaissance loggia was reopened walled for centuries.

Torre dell'Orologio e borgo medievale

History and architecture · Squares and old town · Free entry

The Clock Tower is the gateway to the medieval part of the village, with preserved sections of walls and houses placed on each other on the tuff cliff.

Eremo di Poggio Conte

Places of worship · Abbeys and monasteries

The rocks excavated in the tuff and reachable by a path; from here come six frescoes of the 13th century today exhibited in the archaeological civic museum.
