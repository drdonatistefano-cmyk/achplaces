What to see in Bussoleno

Borgo della Valle di Susa with the parish church of Santa Maria Assunta and its 11th-century Romanesque bell tower, the medieval Casa Amprimo and the Mulino Varesio; in the village Foresto, on the Via Francigena, the chapel frescoed by Antoine de Lonhy and the Orrido with the Lazaretto.

Cappella della Madonna delle Grazie (Foresto)

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches

At the edge of Foresto, in the area of the Posta della Posta della Posta, where horses and carriages were changed along the Via Francigena. The interior and exterior frescoes, from the last quarter of the 15th century, are attributed to the Tolosano Antoine de Lonhy: outside an Annunciation on the triumphal arch re-emerged with the restorations, inside a Pietà in the apse, the Stories of Anna and Gioacchino on the vault and a series of saints. Visits by reservation (0122 622640).

  • Entry To be checked
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes

Parrocchiale di Santa Maria Assunta

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches

Renovated in Baroque style in the 18th century by the Lorraine architect Francesco de Willencourt, who reversed the original orientation (facing west, apse east). The 11th century Romanesque bell tower is the oldest medieval artifact that has come to us; in the interior there is a single nave, on the right of the main altar, a wooden crucifix from the 15th century.

  • Where Piazza Cavour 4, Bussoleno
  • Entry To be checked
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes
  • Tourist portal page www.comune.bussoleno.to.it

Sources: turismotorino.org · comune.bussoleno.to.it

Orrido di Foresto e Lazzaretto

Nature and landscape · Landscapes · Free entry · Viewpoint

Gorge of the river Rocciamelone close to the village Foresto, starting point for excursions to the Rocciamelone, with rock gyms, a via ferrata and the Ginepri Path (about an hour). At the entrance of the Orrido, leaning on the right wall, the Lazaretto: stone construction that according to tradition in the seventeenth century housed the sick with plague. Open all year round; you leave the car downstream and walk about 500 m along Via Gran Borgata.

  • Where Regione Orrido di Foresto 50, Bussoleno
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 1-1.5 hours
  • Tourist portal page www.comune.bussoleno.to.it

Sources: comune.bussoleno.to.it

Castello Borello

History and architecture · Castles and fortresses · Free entry

Documented from the 14th century, but perhaps from the 12th century and attributed by tradition to the Templars; it belonged to the Borellos and served as a refuge for the population at the time of the Marquis Adelaide, then passed to various families until the doctor Fiocchetto, distinguished in the plague of 1630. Quadrangular plant in plastered stone and roof in lose. Private property, visible only from the outside.

  • Where Frazione Baroni, Bussoleno
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes
  • Tourist portal page www.comune.bussoleno.to.it

Sources: turismotorino.org · comune.bussoleno.to.it

Casa Amprimo

History and architecture · Palaces and villas · Free entry

Typical medieval building with porch on the ground floor on masonry columns and wooden architraves, cross-windows framed in terracotta; inside there are late-Gothic frescoes with the lilies of France and the English lion. Private home, visible only from the outside.

  • Where Via Carlo Trattenero, Bussoleno
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes
  • Tourist portal page www.comune.bussoleno.to.it

Sources: comune.bussoleno.to.it

Mulino Varesio

Local life and traditions · Crafts

Ancient mill with a wooden castle on which lay the two millstones for flour. Open at local events or on request for schools; accessible on one floor.
