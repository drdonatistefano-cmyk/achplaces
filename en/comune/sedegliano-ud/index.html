What to see in Sedegliano

Municipality of the Friulian plain near the Tagliamento: in Coderno stands the birthplace of the poet friar David Maria Turoldo, example of rural construction in the pebbles of the river; the capital preserves a castle of the Bronze Age and Gradisca its ancient fortified "centa" with the church of Santo Stefano and the portal of Pilacorte.

Casa natale di padre David Maria Turoldo

Museums · House museums

The headquarters of the David Maria Turoldo Study Centre, founded in 2019 by an association founded in 1992, which takes care of its restoration and guided tours; it is an example of a Friulian rural house with courtyard, porch, kitchen on the ground floor, two bedrooms on the first floor and attic with conference room and small library, with walls in cobbles of the Tagliamento and fragments of terracotta. The two rooms of the Turoldo family retain the original structure. Open Saturday and Sunday 9-18, only by reservation by email.

Castelliere di Sedegliano

History and architecture · Archaeology · Viewpoint

Fortified village of the culture of the Friulian castelliers, founded at the beginning of the Ancient Bronze, renovated between Middle and Recent Bronze and frequented again by the second Iron Age: a rectangular terrapieno with bevelled corners, oriented on the cardinal points, 3,5-4 meters high and wide up to about 22, encloses about 2 hectares and is surrounded by a moat at least 13 meters wide. The interior now houses municipal sports facilities; you enter from a single entrance to the northern corner.

Centa di Gradisca e chiesa di Santo Stefano

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches

Oval relief surrounded by ditches still about five meters deep, the village's "cortina" conquered and sacked in 1309 by Enrico di Prampero di Cuccagna; there is the church of Santo Stefano, whose portal of 1515 has a bas-relief of Giovanni Antonio Pilacorte in the bezel. Several times struck by lightning in the seventeenth century, it was consecrated in 1700 with the new bell tower; a fire in 1971 destroyed the frescoes of the presbytery of Aurelio Mariani.
