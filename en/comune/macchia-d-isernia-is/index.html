What to see in Macchia d'Isernia

Medieval village of the province of Isernia, gathered around the hill of San Nicola di Bari and the Baronale Castle, built around 1100 and rebuilt in Renaissance style in 1480; from the historic center start trails towards the valleys of the torrents Cavaliere and Vandra.

Castello Baronale

History and architecture · Castles and fortresses

Built around 1100 by Clementina, daughter of Norman king Ruggero II, it was restored in Renaissance style in 1480 as a patrician residence and passed in 1748 to the Rotondi, then to the d'Alena, lords of the feud until 1806. The façade dominates the square with a Renaissance loggia with round arches; in the courtyard a Renaissance staircase with colonnade rises to the noble floors, where there was a private chapel. In 1799 and 1860 it was the scene of clashes, and between 1943 and 1944 it hosted the German Staff, then Italian commands and American officers.

The card of the Municipality indicates the access as "Closed": it is seen only from the outside

Centro storico e chiesa di San Nicola di Bari

History and architecture · Squares and old town · Free entry · Viewpoint

The medieval nucleus is collected in semi-wrapping form around the hill of San Nicola di Bari, with a second village dedicated to San Biase, between alleys and squares. In the upper part the bell tower of the church of San Nicola di Bari and the tower of the castle are facing, so much so that the first seems to touch the second.

Sentieri dei torrenti Cavaliere e Vandra

Nature and landscape · Hills and trails · Free entry · Viewpoint

From the old town, trails start on foot, by bike or on horseback, reaching the naturalistic areas along the Knight and Vandra streams.
