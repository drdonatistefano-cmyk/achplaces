What to see in Villarosa

Villarosa, at 523 meters of altitude, was founded in 1762 by Placido Notarbartolo-Zati as San Giacomo di Villarosa, with a regular plant centre along the Garibaldi course and Corso Regina Margherita; its economy was linked to agriculture and then, between the end of the nineteenth and mid twentieth centuries, to the sulfur mines. The main monument is the Mother Church of St. James Major, 1763.

Chiesa Madre di San Giacomo Maggiore

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches · Free entry

Erected in 1763 by the will of the Dukes Notarbartolo, founders of the village, it has a monumental eighteenth-century façade on a large staircase, brought back to the original stone with the restoration of 1985. The interior with a single nave, with eighteenth-century stuccoes, preserves canvases from the eighteenth century, an eighteenth-century organ and, at the high altar, the Assumption of the Virgin by Raimondo Bufera di San Cataldo (1842).

Centro storico, Piazza Vittorio Emanuele e Piazza San Giacomo

History and architecture · Squares and old town · Free entry

The eighteenth-century village (1762) has a chessboard system crossed by Corso Garibaldi and Corso Regina Margherita, which meet in Piazza Vittorio Emanuele, heart of Villarosa. A little south, St. James' Square opens next to the Mother Church, with free access and barrier-free.

  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes
