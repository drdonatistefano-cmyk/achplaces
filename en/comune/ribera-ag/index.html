What to see in Ribera

The village is the birthplace of Francesco Crispi, founded in 1635 by inhabitants of Caltabellotta and crossed by the rivers Verdura, Magazzolo and Platani; above the valley of the Verdura there are the ruins of the Castle of Poggiodiana, whose tower is in the municipal coat of arms, near the Wolf Gorges, while the village of Seccagrande overlooks the sea.

Castello di Poggiodiana

History and architecture · Castles and fortresses · Free entry · Viewpoint

The castle was built in the Arab age on a hill on the left of the Verdura with the name of Misilcassin, fortified by the Normans and named in the sixteenth century to Diana Moncada, he married in 1511 Count Gian Vincenzo Luna; the earthquakes of 1578 seriously damaged him. The irregular plan of about 3,000 m2 in sandstone remains part of the walls, an angular bastion and a cylindrical tower 25 meters high with cruising vault ribbed on octagonal plan, typical of the Swabian age.

dirt and steep path, unprotected cliffs, wells hidden from vegetation and unstable terrain: hiking shoes, avoid rainy days

Gole del Lupo

Nature and landscape · Landscapes · Viewpoint

Gorges excavated from the river Verdura among citrus groves and orchards, about 2.5 km from the city and close to the Castle of Poggidiana; from the area you can see the sea of the stretch of coast of Seccagrande.

Villa comunale

Nature and landscape · City parks · Free entry

The town's flagship is defined by the municipality: park with rare plants and centuries-old trees, driveways among flowers, three water tanks with the swans' house and a children's playground; inside there is the ethno-anthropological museum.

  • Where Villa comunale, Ribera
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes
  • Tourist portal page www.italia.it

Sources: comune.ribera.ag.it · italia.it

Museo etno-antropologico

Museums · Themed

Permanent Museum was founded in 1989 on the initiative of the association "Ribera Verde" (Ribera Verde), which has collected over 4,000 finds of rural civilization, pastoral and handicraft of the territory; tools of ancient peasant art and agricultural machinery have been recovered, catalogued, restored and made accessible to the public.
