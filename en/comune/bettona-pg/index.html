What to see in Bettona

A hilly village above the Umbrian valley, closed by medieval walls that incorporate Etruscan features of the 6th century BC: in Piazza Cavour the Palazzo del Podestà (1371) with the City Museum and the works of Perugino, the churches of Santa Maria Maggiore, San Crispolto and the Oratory of Sant'Andrea; in the plain the eighteenth-century Villa del Boccaglione.

Museo della Città

Museums · Old masters

Collection formed from 1904 with Roman and Roman works of art: the archaeological section on the ground floor of Palazzo Biancalana (nineteenth century, neoclassical; here the entrance) exhibits pre-Roman and Roman materials of the territory, including a marble head of Aphrodite; the gallery on the first floor of the Palazzo del Podestà has the Sant'Antonio da Padova and the Madonna della Misericordia del Perugino, works by Dono Doni, the pupil, Tiberio d'Assisi and Giuseppe Ribera, a terracotta glazed of Robbiana school and a bust of Canova signed and dated 1812. Some of the works, stolen in 1987, were found in Jamaica.

Piazza Cavour e Piazza Garibaldi

History and architecture · Squares and old town · Free entry

In Piazza Cavour the Palazzo del Podestà, completed in 1371, with external staircase, and Palazzo Baglioni, where the leader Malatesta Baglioni IV died on Christmas Eve of 1531 (the family ruled Bettona from the XV to the XVII century). In Piazza Garibaldi the Town Hall of the second half of the fourteenth century, with a sixteenth-century wooden choir in the council hall, and the octagonal fountain of 1890 by Garibaldo Mancini, instead of a fifteenth-century well.

  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes
  • Tourist portal page www.italia.it

Collegiata di Santa Maria Maggiore

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches

Ampliated and reconsecrated in 1225 and restored in neoclassical forms between 1803 and 1816: of the Romanesque-Gothic system only the chapel of Santa Rita remains. The main altar with ciborium with domed temple; the apse was frescoed in 1939 by Gerardo Dottori.

Chiesa di San Crispolto

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches

Built by the Benedictines at the beginning of the thirteenth century to guard the body of Saint Crispolto, the first bishop and martyr of Umbria; Latin cross plan and nineteenth-century facade of Antonio Stefanucci, pupil of Vanvitelli. Of the Romanesque structure only the cusp bell tower remains.

Oratorio di Sant'Andrea

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches

Of thirteenth-century origin, several times rebuilt (in the Baroque age): it contains a wooden ceiling with coffers dating back to the sixteenth century by Benedetto da Montepulciano, an eighteenth-century altar and frescoes of Giottesca school dated 1394 with the Stories of the Passion.

Mura etrusche e medievali e Balcone dell'Umbria

History and architecture · Monuments and memorials · Free entry · Viewpoint

The historic centre is entirely enclosed by medieval walls that incorporate sections of Etruscan walls of the 6th century BC into large sandstone blocks. From Porta Primo Maggio (Porta San Giacomo) you can reach the "Balcone dell'Umbria" (Balcony of Umbria), a viewpoint over the Umbrian valley.

Villa del Boccaglione

History and architecture · Palaces and villas

Country residence built in the eighteenth century by the Crispolti in the plain of Bettona, of neoclassical appearance from the second half of the century; the project is attributed to Giuseppe Piermarini, but the Superintendence indicates that the attribution is not certain (other hypothesis: Antonio Stefanucci). Interiors with stuccoes, mirrors, lanterns and helical stairs, stairway, secret garden with water lilies, exedra, obelisks and English grove, straight avenue, courtyard of honor and the church of the Santissima Annunziata. It was the seat of the German command in the Second World War; today it is of the State, which began its restoration.
