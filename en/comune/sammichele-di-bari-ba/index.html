What to see in Sammichele di Bari

Borgo della Murgia Barese grew up around the Caracciolo Castle, home of the Museo della Civiltà Contadina (Museo della Civiltà Contadina) Dino Bianco, with the Baroque church of Maddalena, the neoclassical matrix church, the clock tower, the apotropaic masks on the portals and the 'vignali' of the historic center.

Castello Caracciolo e Museo della Civiltà Contadina «Dino Bianco»

Museums · Themed · Free entry

Probably of Norman origin as a defensive outpost; an inscription of 1504 reminds the owner Heronimo Centurione, then passed to Michele Vaaz, founder of the village (1609), to Antonio De Ponte (1677), who made it a noble residence, and to Caracciolo (1779); in 1860 the façade was rebuilt in neo-Gothic style and between 1991 and 2002 was restored. The museum, founded in 1968 by Professor Dino Bianco, exhibits over 5,000 agricultural work objects, crafts, domestic life, traditions and games between the nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, in nine rooms on three floors. The Municipality indicates opening from June to September Friday, Saturday and Sunday 18-22, free admission.

  • Where Piazza Caracciolo 7B, Sammichele di Bari
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 1-1.5 hours
  • Official website comune.sammicheledibari.ba.it

Sources: comune.sammicheledibari.ba.it · borghipiubelliditalia.it · touringclub.it

Chiesa della Maddalena

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches

A previous chapel is remembered by an inscription of 1504 by Geronimo Centurione; the current Baroque church was built between 1615 and 1632, with late presbytery (between the late seventeenth and early eighteenth centuries), and houses a 17th century wooden statue of the Archangel Michael.

Chiesa Matrice di Santa Maria del Carmine

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches

Neoclassical, begun in 1845 and completed in 1862 without the two bell towers planned: inside the altar of the Madonna of Pompeii by Angeloronzo Rossi (1909), the Via Crucis in thirteen paintings and the apse fresco by Umberto Colonna, paintings by Mario Colonna and Margherita Deramo; in the bezel of the portal the Madonna of Carmine by Vincenzo Crispo (1954), bronze portals of 1983.

  • Entry To be checked
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes

Centro storico, vignali e maschere apotropaiche

History and architecture · Squares and old town · Free entry

Stone houses with vines bred above the doors, the "vignali" (wineyards), tradition that would date back about 400 years ago; to see the area of Casamicciola and Vico Spezzato. On the arch keys of the portals are carved apotropaic masks against the evil eye, grouped in four periods, from animal and mythical faces to serene human faces. At the entrance of the ancient village, in front of the Mother Church, the civic clock, built between 1876 and 1878 on a project by Vincenzo Ventrella.

  • Where Centro storico, Sammichele di Bari
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 1-1.5 hours
  • Tourist portal page borghipiubelliditalia.it

Sources: borghipiubelliditalia.it

Cimitero di San Francesco

Places of worship · Monumental cemeteries

It was built between 1840 and 1848 under the guidance of Pietrantonio Schettini, as a graveyard that bypassed the Bourbon ban to bury in churches; it was used until 1910.

  • Entry To be checked
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes

Palazzo Pinto e Monumento ai Caduti

History and architecture · Palaces and villas · Free entry

Palazzo Pinto, designed in 1897 for Francesco Pinto on Piazza Leonardo Netti and purchased by the Municipality in 1958, is now Palazzo della Cultura with Library and Historical Archives. The Monument to the Fallen, the work of sculptor Antonio Bassi, was inaugurated on May 24, 1937 in the square in front of Palazzo Gentile. Casa Larocca, municipal seat since 1860, now houses the tourist infopoint.

  • Where Piazza Leonardo Netti, Sammichele di Bari
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes
  • Tourist portal page borghipiubelliditalia.it

Sources: borghipiubelliditalia.it

Menhir della via Tarentina

History and architecture · Archaeology · Free entry

Two thick stones: one at the intersection between the provincial road and the Vetus via Tarenti, about two meters high, the other a few hundred meters further on the same road towards Monte Sannace, just over a metre high.
