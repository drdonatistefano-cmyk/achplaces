What to see in Casola in Lunigiana

Porta del Parco delle Alpi Apuane, Casola rises on a rocky spur above the Aulella, on the border between Lunigiana and Garfagnana, and was long of the Malaspina di Fosdinovo. The village grew up in the fifteenth century around the castle houses in the old town hall the Museum of the Territory of Alta Valle Aulella; in the territory, the Romanesque parishes of Codiponte and Offiano.

Borgo fortificato di Casola

History and architecture · Villages · Free entry · Viewpoint

The village, on a rocky spur above the river Aulella near the river Tassonaro, developed in the fifteenth century around a castle of which remain a cylindrical tower and few sections of walls. The name derives from the Roman term "Casuli" (Casuli), which was linked to Fivizzano from the fifteenth century to the nineteenth century, passed to the Duchy of Modena in 1841 and to the province of Massa and Carrara in 1859.

Museo del Territorio dell'Alta Valle Aulella

Museums · Archaeological

It is located in the old town hall of the village and preserves evidence of human settlements from the lower Paleolithic. There is also the baptismal font of 1452 coming from the Pieve di San Pietro di Offiano.

Pieve dei Santi Cornelio e Cipriano a Codiponte

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches

Cited from the end of the 8th century and already a garrison of the diocese of Luni, it is considered a symbol of the Romanesque in Lunigiana: in sandstone, with protruding façade, carved reliefs and capitals storied with symbols of medieval beasts. It rises on the right bank of the Aulella near a medieval bridge, beyond which there are the ruins of an 11th century castle, along the Via del Volto Santo. Inside a late medieval triptych with the Virgin enthroned between the Saints Cornelius and Cyprian, with the predella depicting the transport of the Holy Face from Lucca.

Pieve di San Pietro di Offiano

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches · Free entry

Founded probably between the 11th and 12th centuries on a variant of the Via Francigena towards the Passo del Tea, between pastures, fields and vineyards on the border with Garfagnana, owes the current Baroque aspect to a renewal of mid-eighteenth century. A portal bears the Malaspinian symbol of the dry plum, inside there is a canvas with the Last Supper and on the façade is walled the fragment of a pilgrim figure, the only marble element dating back to the Middle Ages.

open only during religious services
