What to see in Villafranca in Lunigiana

Medieval village at the crossroads between the Bagnone valley and the Magra valley, ancient free-of-duty villa and crossed by the Via Francigena, with the ruins of Malnido dei Malaspina castle and the Ethnographic Museum of Lunigiana in a mill of the sixteenth century; in the territory the villages of Filetto, with its forest of chestnut trees, Virgoletta and Malgrate.

Museo etnografico della Lunigiana

Museums · Themed

In a mill of the community of the sixteenth century, remained in operation until the 1930s, on the outskirts of the village along the Via Francigena; the original water wheels, moved by the nearby stream Bagnone, are still in place. Founded in 1977 by the Cultural Association Manfredo Giuliani, it exhibits three millstones of different stones (a sandstone for chestnuts, blue stone for corn, basalt for wheat), objects of chestnut processing, breeding, cheese, fishing and handicrafts, amulets of fertility, the "Raganelle" of the Holy Week, cradles in beech including the famous cradle of Bratto, a rebuilt Lunigiana cuisine and a large frame.

Ruderi del castello di Malnido e borgo

History and architecture · Castles and fortresses · Free entry · Viewpoint

The remains of the 12th century castle stand on a rocky spur overlooking the road and the ford on the Magra; it was the seat of the Malaspina of Spino Secco, which in the division of assets of 1221 held Villafranca for its commercial and financial advantages. Next are the remains of the church of San Nicolò and the square dedicated to Dante, guest of the Malaspina; from the area of the castle the medieval road leads to the parish church of San Giovanni.

Filetto

History and architecture · Villages · Free entry

The name derives from the Greek . ...Fulacterion , fortified village: born as a Byzantine fortification between the 5th and 6th centuries, it preserves the quadrangular plan of the late antique castra with perpendicular streets and central squares, still closed by the walls of the 17th century. In 1351 it was a market village on the Via Francigena, in the feud of Malgrate; in the seventeenth century it passed to the Ariberti of Cremona, which extended it with a wide central street, a square and a palace (the Borgo degli Ariberti) next to the church of Saints Jacopo and Antonio and the convent of the Fatebenefratelli, connected by suspended bridges and arches above the doors. In the ancient core narrow alleys, the Jewish Village and a large market square.

Selva di Filetto e oratorio di San Genesio

Nature and landscape · Landscapes · Free entry

Just outside the walls of Filetto, a centuries-old chestnut grove surrounds the small oratory of San Genesio, still a goal of devotion; Carlo Caselli called him "Selva of suicides." Here were found eleven stele statues, today at the Museum of the Stone Statues of Pontremoli, and two menhirs are still standing. Around 25 August, from the 17th century, there is the fair of San Genesio, once a cattle market and today a festival with music and products from Lunigiana under chestnut trees.

Virgoletta

History and architecture · Villages · Free entry · Viewpoint

Village of road lying on the ridge of a hill, with walls more than ten meters high, fountains, numerous speaking doors, the Malaspina castle and the church of Santi Gervasio and Protasio, whose bell tower dominates the village.

Malgrate, castello e torre

History and architecture · Castles and fortresses · Viewpoint

Ancient manor whose castle with the high tower still controls the access to the valley of Bagnone; the look extends over the valley of the Magra and the territory of Villafranca. In 1351 the young Bernabò Malaspina, a lord also of Filetto, ruled there.

Via Francigena lungo la Magra

Nature and landscape · Hills and trails · Free entry

The Via Francigena crosses the village and runs through paths, mule tracks and woods along the river Magra; stage 23, from Pontremoli to Aulla, to Filetto, Virgoletta and Villafranca. The route of Francigena in Lunigiana measures 71 km in three stages.
