What to see in Irsina

Walled village called Montepeloso until 1895, Irsina houses in the Cathedral the statue of St. Euphemia attributed to Andrea Mantegna, the church of St. Francis with the frescoed crypt, the Janora Museum, the underground booties and a series of historical palaces.

Cattedrale di Santa Maria Assunta e statua di Sant'Eufemia

Places of worship · Cathedrals

Built in the 13th century and renovated in 1777, it has a Baroque façade, a Gothic bell tower with double-fores and crypt, and preserves a relic of St. Euphemia's arm. The most famous work is the polychrome statue of the stone saint of Vicenza, 172 cm high, dated around 1450 and brought here in 1454 by Roberto De Mabilia from the Padua workshop of Mantegna: the saint holds one hand in the mouths of a lion and with the other holds the three-top mountain with the castle, symbol of Irsina. Attribution to Mantegna is now discussed by scholars.

Chiesa di San Francesco e cripta

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches

Church of the Franciscan monastery, of the twelfth century, known for the fresco of the Pantocratore; the crypt, obtained in a Norman tower, contains fourteenth-century frescoes of the Giotto school commissioned by the Del Balzo family.

  • Entry To be checked
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes
  • Tourist portal page www.italia.it

Sources: italia.it · borghipiubelliditalia.it

Museo civico "Michele Janora"

Museums · Archaeological · Paid entry

In the left wing of the monastery of San Francesco, six rooms exhibit about 300 of the more than 1,600 finds collected by the historical and archaeologist of Irsinian Michele Janora, mainly from necropolis of the Eastern Basilicata and north-western Puglia, from the Neolithic to the Hellenistic age. Open from Tuesday to Saturday 10-20, Sunday 10.30-20, closed on Monday.

  • Where Piazza San Francesco, Irsina
  • Entry €2.50, reduced €1
  • Suggested time 1-1.5 hours
  • Tourist portal page www.italia.it

Sources: cultura.gov.it · basilicataturistica.it · italia.it

Palazzi storici

History and architecture · Palaces and villas · Free entry

The main palaces date back to the Cinque-Seventh century: the Bishop's Palace next to the Cathedral, with the chapel of San Basilio; Palazzo Nugent, built on the medieval castle and enclosing Porta Maggiore or Sant'Eufemia, residence of the Riario Sforza in the eighteenth century; Palazzo D'Amato Cantorio (XV century) and Palazzo Janora, home of the historian Michele Janora, both bugnati in via Roma; Palazzo Arsia in via Sant'Angelo, with the motto Palazzo Rizzi in Via Antica Tribuna.

  • Where Via Roma, Irsina
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 1-1.5 hours
  • Tourist portal page www.italia.it

Sources: comune.irsina.mt.it · italia.it

Centro storico, mura e insediamento rupestre

History and architecture · Villages · Free entry · Viewpoint

The village, called Montepeloso until 1895, is surrounded by turrite walls and is rich in noble palaces, portals and sacred buildings; at the entrance of the village there is an important rock settlement. It was an important centre in Roman times and one of the most developed of the Basilicata under Longobards and Byzantines; in 1123 Callisto II made it a bishop's seat.

  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes
