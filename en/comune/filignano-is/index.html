What to see in Filignano

Municipality of the province of Isernia in the National Park of Abruzzo, Lazio and Molise, made up of 12 villages distributed over three valleys from the basin of Filignano to the first buttresses of Monte Marrone, with paths between dry walls, votive newsstands in majolica and ancient stone straw; in Cerasuolo the church of San Pasquale Baylon, rebuilt after the Second World War.

Borghi, sentieri e pagliare delle Mainarde

Nature and landscape · Hills and trails · Free entry · Viewpoint

The municipal territory, in the National Park of Abruzzo, Lazio and Molise, includes 12 villages on three valleys, from the basin of Filignano to the first buttresses of Monte Marrone, and the provincial road offers a panorama of the Mainarde. The paths in the woods are lined with dry stone walls, which also serve to terrace and protect the slopes, and votive newsstands decorated with majolica of Neapolitan school; in the woods remain the straws, ancient pastoral shelters in dry stone that recall the Greek tholos. From the village of Cerasuolo passes the Via Francigena del Sud coming from San Biagio Saracinisco.

  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 2 hours or more

Chiesa di San Pasquale Baylon a Cerasuolo

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches

Parish church wanted by Duke Pasquale Marotta, the last feudal man of Cerasuolo (dead in 1884), started at the end of the nineteenth century and completed at the beginning of the twentieth century. Destroyed during the Second World War, it was rebuilt thanks to the abbot of Montecassino Gregorio Diamare; damaged by the earthquake of 1984, it had cover and facade restored in 2005. It has three aisles and a stone facade, raised on the road and reached by an external staircase, marked by parasts with doric capitals.

  • Where Piazza San Pasquale, Cerasuolo, Filignano
