What to see in Montegrosso Pian Latte

Village of stone houses at 721 meters, on a terrace on the slopes of Mount Monega in the Upper Arroscia Valley, in the Regional Natural Park of the Ligurian Alps: surrounded by chestnut trees, it houses the museum . The chestnut and the Bosco Addomesticato , the Baroque parish of San Biagio and, in the surroundings, the alpine pastures of Pian del Latte and Case Fascei.

Museo «La castagna e il Bosco Addomesticato»

Museums · Themed

One of the locations of the Museo Territorio (Territoir Museum) .The Faces of the Ubagu della Valle Arroscia. The museum in the village, in six sections with panels, photos, maps and reconstructions, tells the history and traditions of chestnut in the communities of the Ligurian hinterland: tree botany, ethnographic uses, techniques of processing and conservation; it closes with the real section of a millennial trunk and a video. The open-air path continues with the restored stone dryer ('canissu', used in Valle Arroscia from the eighteenth century to the mid-Twentieth century), two rebuilt charcoals (one whole, one sectioned) with the coal barn, and ends in a chestnut grove.

the Park indicates that the visits are booked at the Municipality (tel. 0183 328731)

Borgo, parrocchiale di San Biagio e Oratorio della Santissima Annunziata

History and architecture · Villages · Free entry

The settlement is documented as "Montegrossum" from 1207, whose name (Plalum Lactis in medieval sources) recalls pastures and breeding. The old town preserves buildings supported by archivolts typical of an ancient mountain village. The parish church of San Biagio has baroque presbytery and preserves the portal of the oldest church, of the late fifteenth century; next to it there is the Oratory of the Santissima Annunziata, seat of the fraternity; in the village and surroundings there are the chapels of San Bernardo, San Sebastiano, San Lorenzo, Santa Elisabetta and Madonna dei Monti.

Case Fascei

Local life and traditions · Hidden corners · Free entry · Viewpoint

Once inhabited only by the shepherds, today it is a group of restored houses with stone walls and roofs in "ciappe" (the local stone slabs); the Park indicates it, with the alpine pastures of Pian del Latte, among the destinations around the village.
