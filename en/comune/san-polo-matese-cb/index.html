What to see in San Polo Matese

Country at the foot of the Matese Molisano, where Palazzo Rogati hosts the permanent artistic nativity scene created in 1961 by the Barcelonaese Juan Marì Oliva and a paleontological exhibition with fossils of the Cretaceous, photos and local memories and a section dedicated to zampogna; on the hill of the medieval village stands the parish church of San Pietro in Vincoli with the circular bell tower.

Presepe Rogati e Museo di Paleontologia

Museums · Themed

In Palazzo Rogati, the artistic nativity scene created in 1961 by the patron Luigi Rogati and the work of Juan Marì Oliva from Barcelona with Angelo Stefanucci from Rome: Palestinian and Eastern setting with camels, dancers, shepherds and stage scenes, and four figures with the faces of the Rogati family. In the other halls a collection of fossils of the Cretaceous (between 135 and 63 million years ago), with the rudist, mollusk with a coral-like appearance up to almost a metre long, photographs and local memories, an anthropological section and a wing on the zampogne.

Chiesa di San Pietro in Vincoli

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches

Parish church on the ridge of the hill of the medieval village, on the site of a Norman fortified garrison: built in Renaissance forms in the sixteenth century after the earthquake of 1456, with the eastern tower transformed into a circular bell tower with spiral staircase. It was collapsed in the earthquake of July 26, 1805, which killed 136 people, and was rebuilt in 1806-1808 with the help of the emigrants; the façade, collapsed for the war, was rebuilt in 1945-1949. It preserves a baptismal font of 1552, early seventeenth-century saintshop and a wooden statue of Our Lady with the Child of Paolo Antonio di Zinno (1755).
