What to see in Sassoferrato

Village on the Marena river, one of the most beautiful villages in Italy, home to the painter Giovan Battista Salvi, known as the Sassoferrato: the Rocca di Albornoz in 1365, the Palazzo dei Priori with the Archaeological Museum, the remains of the Roman Sentinum and the Romanesque Abbey of Santa Croce dei Conti.

Area archeologica di Sentinum

History and architecture · Archaeology

Remains of the Roman city with paved streets, cardoon and decuman, a thermal building, a foundry and a second thermal plant in Santa Lucia, outside the walls; near, in 295 BC, Rome defeated Sanniti and Galli in the Battle of the Nations.

Palazzo dei Priori e Museo civico archeologico

Museums · Archaeological

The palace, begun in 1335 and finished in the early sixteenth century and joined to the town hall, houses the museum with ruins and mosaics of Sentinum and the daily life of the Roman city; on the ground floor the Sala Perottiana with Byzantine and Flemish reliquaries, including the icon of San Demetrio, Byzantine mosaic on late fourteenth century table.

Palazzo Oliva: Civica raccolta d'arte e Raccolta incisori marchigiani

Museums · Old masters

The fifteenth-century palace on Piazza Matteotti: on the first floor the collection of Marche engravers from 1550 to today, on the second the Civic Art Collection, about thirty works including three tables by Pietro Paolo Agabiti and two paintings by Giovan Battista Salvi called the Sassoferrato (1609-1685).

Abbazia di Santa Croce dei Conti

Places of worship · Abbeys and monasteries

Built at the end of the 12th century by the Counts Atti for the Camaldolese monks with stones of Sentinum, it is one of the most important Romanesque buildings of the Marche: an inscribed Greek cross plan, four pillars with semi-colons of granite, capitals of Lombard taste (one with the Crucifixion), narthex with carved portal; it contains frescoes of the Fabrian school, the altarpiece of San Benedetto di Agabiti (1524) and a 17th century carved and gilded palotto.
