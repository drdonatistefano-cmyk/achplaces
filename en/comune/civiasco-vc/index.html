What to see in Civiasco

Sunny village along the Way of San Carlo between Orta and Varallo, on the watershed between Valsesia and the valley of Lake Orta; almost destroyed by a fire in 1779, it was rebuilt in Hispano-Moresco style brought by the emigrants who had worked in the Iberian peninsula. To see the Civic Museum Ercole Durio, the parish church of San Gottardo, the oratory of San Rocco and the Falconera ferrata.

Museo civico Ercole Durio

Museums · City history

In the center of the village, it is named after its benefactor, 19th century collector. It exhibits artifacts, weapons, furnishings and an ancient traditional dress; nearby there is the municipal library with more than 1,200 volumes.

Borgo, chiesa di San Gottardo e oratorio di San Rocco

History and architecture · Villages · Free entry

After the 1779 fire that destroyed it almost completely, the village was rebuilt in Hispano-Moorish style, influenced by the eighteenth-century migrants returned from the Iberian peninsula. The parish church of San Gottardo dates back to the 17th century; at the gates of the village the oratory of San Rocco houses an altar in marble of the eighteenth century. The village is a stop on the Way of San Carlo between Orta and Varallo for the Passo della Colma.

  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes
  • Tourist portal page visitvalsesiavercelli.it

Ferrata Falconera

Nature and landscape · Mountains · Viewpoint

Made by the Alpine Rescue of Varallo with the Alpine Guides of Alagna, it has two routes: the Partusacc, ring to Mount Tovo, and the most challenging Falconera, signposted by Civiasco with path 636. At the opening of the night lighting in 2020, it was presented as the first European ferrata equipped with a lighting system.
