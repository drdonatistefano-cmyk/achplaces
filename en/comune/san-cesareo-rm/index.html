What to see in San Cesareo

Centre between the Roman Castles and the Prenestini Mountains, about 38 km from Rome, with the remains of the Torraccia, the two-tiered nymph of the residence of the Emperor Maxentius (beginning of the fourth century), and the Railway-Museum of the Colonna Station, which preserves the last section of tracks of the old Rome-Fiuggi line.

Ferrovia-Museo della Stazione di Colonna

Museums · Themed

Born to preserve the last section, about 500 meters, of tracks of the line Rome-Fiuggi-Frosinone (designed from 1907, built from 1913 and opened in full in 1917, then abandoned in the post-war period) together with the buildings of the centenary station of Colonna, today in the territory of San Cesareo. After more than twenty years of abandonment, a group of individuals restored the tracks, which house a collection of historical rotabili; in the traveler building a faithful reconstruction of the office of the station manager, instruments, objects, documents and vintage photographs. Open Friday, Saturday and Sunday 9-13 and 14.30-18.30, with reservation required.

La Torraccia (Rotonda di San Cesareo)

History and architecture · Archaeology

Remains of a large Roman building from the beginning of the fourth century, in the residence of the Emperor Maxentius: a two-level nymph, square outside and circular inside, with a circular exedra in each of the four lower corners, vaulted with octagonal coffers and walls once covered with polychrome marbles.

Chiesa di San Giuseppe

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches · Free entry

In the chapel of the SS. Sacramento holds, in a walled case, the relic of St. Cesareus, patron saint celebrated on August 27 after the dispute between the confraternities of St. Anthony of Padua and St. Joseph for the choice of the saint.

Lane De.Co., vino e Sagra dell'Uva

Local life and traditions · Food and wine

Lana is a fresh pasta born at the end of the nineteenth century, when peasants from neighbouring countries built a village of huts on the Colle dei Marcelli: soft wheat flour ground with stone, eggs, water and salt, spread, dried on wooden sticks and cut into triangles; since 2019 it is produced De.Co. and served with cod. The Castelli Romani DOC wine and the polenta pizzas with herbs are also produced; at the end of September the Festival of Grapes and the Typical Products carries allegorical floats decorated with grapes.
