Mefite della Valle d'Ansanto

Nature and landscape · Caves and geosites · Free entry · Viewpoint

In the valley of Ansanto a small lake boils with sulphurous vapors that make the air irrespirable: dry in summer, bubbling in winter. Sacred to Osci and Sanniti and dedicated to Mephyte, Italian goddess of water and fertility, was identified as access to the underworld; Virgilio in the seventh book of the Eneide calls it "umbilicus Italiae" and writes Cicero, Pliny and Varrone. On the north-western slopes of the hill of Santa Felicita, the remains of the sanctuary (before the first half of the first century BC, in use until the fourth century A.D.) were excavated; the findings of the votive crest are exhibited in room V of the Irpino Museum in Avellino.

  • Where Valle d'Ansanto, Rocca San Felice
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 1-1.5 hours
  • Tourist portal page cultura.gov.it

Castello e Donjon

History and architecture · Castles and fortresses · Viewpoint

A fortified area that occupies the entire plateau at the top of the fortress, with walls still visible: after the first entrance a space housed the houses of artisans and soldiers, a second fortified entrance leads to the courtyard from which you access the Donjon. The entrance door is at the entrance of the Palazzo De Antonellis-Villani.

  • Where Piazza San Felice, Rocca San Felice
  • Entry To be checked
  • Suggested time 1-1.5 hours
  • Tourist portal page www.italia.it

Sources: comune.roccasanfelice.av.it

Santuario di Santa Felicita

Places of worship · Sanctuaries · Free entry

He rose to replace the pagan worship of the goddess Mefite with Christian worship: the first small church of the fourth century was destroyed with earthquakes of 1688 and 1694, was rebuilt at the end of the seventeenth century by the priest Giovanbattista Santoli and enriched over a century after a portal by the archpriest Vincenzo Maria Santoli. Rebuilt after World War I, it has been in its present form since 1928.

  • Where Colle di Santa Felicita, Rocca San Felice
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes
  • Tourist portal page cultura.gov.it

Sources: cultura.gov.it

Chiesa Madre di Santa Maria Maggiore

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches · Free entry

Probably born with the settlement around the 11th century, it was destroyed with the bell tower by the earthquake of 1980 and rebuilt in local stone preserving the original external appearance, reopened to worship on 4 August 1991. Inside there are wooden statues of San Felice, Maria SS. of Constantinople, San Giuseppe, Sant'Antonio da Padova, San Vito, San Vincenzo and San Francesco di Paola.

  • Where Rocca San Felice
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes
  • Tourist portal page www.italia.it

Sources: comune.roccasanfelice.av.it

Cappella di Maria SS. di Costantinopoli

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches · Free entry

Built in 1625 after a particularly grievous pestilence for the country. It preserves a painting from the early seventeenth century with the Madonna of Constantinople feeding the Child over a burning city, an Immaculate with Saints Nicola and Rocco kneeling and a work under the ceiling that recalls the Archangel Gabriel.

  • Where Rocca San Felice
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes
  • Tourist portal page www.italia.it

Sources: comune.roccasanfelice.av.it

Museo civico «Don Nicola Gambino»

Museums · Archaeological

In the middle of the medieval village, with the remains of the Donjon in the background, exhibits archaeological finds: metal objects (knifes, nails, horseshoes, tools), glass, worked bone, fusaiole, pipe, a base of limestone fireplace with anthropomorphic figure, stone bullets, coins and about 150 ceramics, including glazed XII-XIII and protomaiolic of the XIII-XIV.
