What to see in Dolcè

In Valdadige, on the banks of the Adige, Dolcè preserves Volargne Villa del Bene, with the portal attributed to Sanmicheli and the 16th century frescoes of Brusasorzi, and the secular cypresses of the public park; in Ceraino the remains of the Chiusa, an Austrian fortress on the river gorge.

Villa del Bene

History and architecture · Palaces and villas

The fifteenth-century complex was completed by 1560: Giovanni del Bene bought it from Antonio Malfatti in 1538-1539, renewed it and frescoed its interiors, and the villa hosted prelates of the Council of Trento. The monumental portal, attributed to Michele Sanmicheli, dates back to 1551; the first courtyard has the oldest building with porch and loggia, the second a crenellated dolombara tower. The frescoes, recently restored, are attributed to Domenico Brusasorzi, Nicola Crollanza and Bernardino India: pergolas with grape petti on the staircase, landscapes of the Adige and the Chiusa and Roman stories in the loggia, eight sibillas and four scenes of the Apocalypse in the salon. It is State-owned and is open on Sunday afternoons (April-September 14-19, October-March 14-18) and by reservation on other days.

Chiusa di Ceraino

History and architecture · Castles and fortresses · Viewpoint

Remains of an Austrian fortress on the Adige, where the valley is narrowed over the river, used for the control of goods and the payment of duties; near stands the Fort Ceraino, part of the network of Austrian and Italian forts that controlled the valley.

  • Where Via Statale, Ceraino, Dolcè
  • Entry To be checked
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes
  • Tourist portal page www.visitverona.it

Sources: visitverona.it

Cipressi secolari di Volargne

Nature and landscape · City parks · Free entry

Three estimated 350-450-year-old cypress trees, near the banks of the Adige, in a public park; the largest is about 22 meters tall with 4.80 meters in circumference.
