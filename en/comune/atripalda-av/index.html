What to see in Atripalda

An Irpina town on the river Saturday, born next to the Roman colony of Abellinum, whose remains on the plateau of the Civita form the archaeological park; in Piazza Umberto I the Palazzo della Dogana dei Grani houses the archaeological museum, while the church of Sant'Ippolisto houses the Specus Martyrum.

Parco archeologico dell'antica Abellinum

History and architecture · Archaeology · Viewpoint

Remains of the Roman colony of Abellinum, founded at the end of the II century BC on a Sannite settlement, on the plateau of the Civita along the left bank of Saturday: a stretch of wall in cross-linked work, a public area with spas and hole and a domus of Hellenistic-Pumpian type of about 2,500 m2, perhaps of a freedom of Agrippa. The colony fell after the earthquake of 346 AD and was abandoned during the Greek-Gothic War.

  • Suggested time 1-1.5 hours
  • Official website www.italia.it

Palazzo della Dogana dei Grani e museo archeologico

Museums · Archaeological

Palace in the present form of 1883, center of trade of the grains in the Bourbon age; since 1993 the ground floor houses the archaeological museum with the finds of the necropolis of Capo La Torre and Abellinum. The permanent exhibition "La Dogana nella Dogana" is also held, with statues that decorated the façade of the Dogana di Avellino. Open Monday to Friday.

Chiesa di Sant'Ippolisto Martire e Specus Martyrum

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches

Church of late early Christian origins, known for the crypt called Specus Martyrum, a catacomb with the remains of Christians killed during the persecutions; there are traditionally preserved the graves of Romulus and Bishop Sabino (VI century).
