What to see in Giovinazzo

Seaside town north of Bari, with the ancient centre on a promontory surrounded by walls of the fifteenth century, dominated by the Romanesque Cathedral of Santa Maria Assunta, and the large square Vittorio Emanuele II with the Triton Fountain. In the hinterland, about 6 km, the Dolmen of San Silvestro testifies to the human presence since the bronze age.

Cattedrale di Santa Maria Assunta

Places of worship · Cathedrals

12th century cathedral in Piazza Duomo, in the area of the old port, which can be reached by passing under the arches of Via Cathedral. Only the apse retains the original Romanesque system: the rest was largely renovated in Baroque style in the eighteenth century. The best preserved part is the crypt, remained as it was, supported by 10 columns with unusual Roman capitals and 12 pillars that protrude from the perimeter walls; the church preserves the Byzantine icon of the Madonna of Corsignano.

Centro antico, Arco di Traiano e palazzi

History and architecture · Squares and old town · Free entry

The ancient centre is located on a promontory closed by walls of the fifteenth century and preserves Roman, medieval and Baroque remains; the Arch of Traiano is the entrance. Along the way the Palazzo del Marquis di Rende, the sixteenth-century Palazzo Saraceno, seat of the Town Hall, and the imposing Palazzo Ducale seventeenth-century courtyard, with the facade on the sea close to the remains of the Roman walls. Quiet during the day, in the evening you live; walking along the ramparts of Via Cathedral you arrive at the Duomo.

  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 1-1.5 hours

Piazza Vittorio Emanuele II e Fontana dei Tritoni

History and architecture · Squares and old town · Free entry

The main square, with one of the centers of the town life, is striking for the breadth and richness of the palaces that surround it; its trapezoidal shape is best grasped from the center, where is located the Triton Fountain.

Dolmen di San Silvestro

History and architecture · Archaeology

Among the olive groves of the coastal Murgia, about 6 km from the center, was discovered in 1961 demolishing a pile of stones. Originally at mound, with a diameter of 40 meters, it is built dry and crossed by a long and narrow corridor; on the original deposition plane were found the remains of 13 individuals with fragments of the set, testifying to the human presence in the area since the Bronze Age. Tel. 080 2254581.
