What to see in Mortara

City of Lomellina along the Via Francigena, traditionally linked to the battle of 773 between the Franks of Charlemagne and the Longobards of King Desiderio; the city symbol is the basilica of San Lorenzo with the Gothic façade and the paintings of Cerano, Lanino and Procaccini, while at the gates of the city stands the abbey of Sant'Albino. It is known for the Salame d'Oca di Mortara IGP and the Palio of late September.

Basilica di San Lorenzo e Museo della Collegiata

Places of worship · Basilicas

Symbol of the city, is a Romanesque-Gothic building of the fourteenth century in brick, with an elaborate Gothic façade linked to Bartolino da Novara, which painted it at the end of the fourteenth century; the interior with three naves is described by Italia.it as a sort of small museum of sacred art, with paintings especially of the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries. In the chapel of the right aisle there is the large table of Cerano with the Crucifixion with the Magdalene and the Saints Ambrose and Lorenzo; there are then the fifteenth-century Polyptych of St. Joseph of Lorenzo of Mortara, wooden crib with 80 bas-relief figures, the Madonna of the Rosary by Gerolamo Giovenone and works by Bernardino Lanino and Julius Cesare Procaccini.

  • Suggested time 1-1.5 hours

Abbazia di Sant'Albino

Places of worship · Abbeys and monasteries

At the gates of Mortara, along the Via Francigena, the abbey was born according to tradition around the battlefield of 773, in which the Franks of Charlemagne defeated King Desire, and at the burials of the fallen Frank soldiers; the origins are located between the V and the VIII century and over the centuries it was a stop of pilgrims headed for Rome. The church remains with frescoes from the fifteenth century, inscriptions left by pilgrims and an elegant Renaissance portal, the apse and the Romanesque-Gothic bell tower, the remains of the cloister with stone loggia and wooden architraves.

Santuario della Madonna del Campo

Places of worship · Sanctuaries

Church already existing in 1100, preserves pictorial evidence of different eras, including a Mercy, and two statues of San Domenico and San Giovanni Battista attributed to Giovan Battista Crespi called Cerano, a painter from Lombardy of the seventeenth century.

  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes
