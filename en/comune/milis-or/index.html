What to see in Milis

Country of citrus groves at the foot of Montiferru, with the Romanesque Saint Paul, the parish church of San Sebastiano and the neoclassical Palazzo Boyl, seat of the Jewel Museum and Sardinian costume.

Palazzo Boyl e Museo del gioiello e del costume sardo

Museums · Themed

Built at the beginning of the nineteenth century by the Marquis Boyl on the site of a noble house of the fourteenth century, it is a neoclassical building with Pompeian red facade and entrance gallery; Carlo Felice, Carlo Alberto, Grazia Deledda, D'Annunzio and Balzac stayed there (1838). It houses a cultural center with a small art gallery and the museum of costume and Sardinian jewelry: fabrics, clothes and jewels that show the evolution of the costume in the last two centuries.

Chiesa di San Paolo

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches

Romanesque, begun around 1140 probably by the Camaldolese monks and completed in the first quarter of the thirteenth century: the upper part of the façade alternates rows of golden limestone and dark basalt, with three blind arches and diamond motifs as in Santa Giusta. Inside there is a polychrome baroque wooden altar with statues of Saints John, Peter and Paul and three sixteenth-century paintings of Catalan school (Saint Paul, Crucifixion, Madonna and Child). It rises in the cemetery, where Italian and German soldiers are resting dead in the bombing of 1943.

Parrocchiale di San Sebastiano

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches

Church in Gothic-Aragonese style in the center of the village, in front of Palazzo Boyl; it holds the clothes with which, according to legend, was dried in 1675 the statue of St. Paul that would exude blood.

Chiesa di Santa Vittoria

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches

It preserves a large wooden altar of the seventeenth century; there were found tombs of the sixth-sixth century. The Boyls donated the litter used for the statue of Jesus in the ritual of the Holy Week of S'Icravamentu.

Agrumeti di Milis

Nature and landscape · Landscapes · Free entry

The oranges, perhaps planted by the Camaldolese monks already in the twelfth century, are the heart of the agricultural identity of the country, greened by the abundance of water at the foot of Montiferru; a short distance from São Paulo are the Pernis-Vacca and La Vega gardens.
