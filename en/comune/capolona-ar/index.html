What to see in Capolona

A common hill between the southern slopes of Pratomagno and the right bank of the Arno, at the gates of Casentino, established in 1808. The most important monument is the Pieve di Santa Maria a Sietina with its frescoes of the fourteenth and fifteenth centuries; around, medieval parishes such as San Giovanni and San Martino Sopr'Arno and villages such as Bibbiano, Santa Margherita and Castelluccio.

Pieve di Santa Maria a Sietina

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches

Near the shore of the Arno, along an ancient Roman road, it is the most important sacred building of Capolona; the name would call Satres, the Etruscan Saturn, to which a temple was dedicated here. After an early Christian church, the present Romanesque parish church with three aisles and three apses dates back to the 13th century. It preserves two cycles of frescoes: one of about 1370-1380 attributed to the Master of Pieve in Sietina and one of the nineties of the fifteenth century, of followers of Lorentino d'Andrea influenced by Piero della Francesca, commissioned by the family of merchants Bacci Arezzo.

Pieve di San Giovanni (Santi Giovanni Battista e Martino)

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches · Viewpoint

Documented from the 10th century, in the center of the village of San Giovanni, owes its current appearance especially to the changes of the eighteenth century and preserves a crucifix of the fourteenth century. From the village the view ranges from the Valley of the Beaches and from the peaks of the Alpe di Catenaia to the hills of Chianti.

  • Entry To be checked
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes

Pieve di San Martino Sopr'Arno

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches

Cited in 1017 among the goods of the canons of the Arezzo Church, it was transformed in the nineteenth century; it remains the semicircular apse and some original masonry.

Bibbiano

Castelluccio

History and architecture · Villages · Free entry

It takes its name from a small castle that controlled this section of the Arno, of which there are few medieval traces like the Portaccia; it was the seat of the municipality until the twentieth century. The church of San Michele Arcangelo, documented in the fourteenth century as Sant'Angelo in Fabriciano, was rebuilt in the second half of the nineteenth century with a clock bell tower instead of the small sailing bell tower.

  • Where Castelluccio, Capolona
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes
  • Tourist portal page www.visittuscany.com

Sources: discoverarezzo.com · visittuscany.com
