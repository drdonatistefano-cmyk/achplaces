What to see in Lauro

The centre of the Vallo di Lauro, dominated by the Lancellotti Castle, of Lombard-Norman origin and rebuilt at the end of the nineteenth century; the birthplace of Umberto Nobile, to which the museum in Palazzo Pignatelli is dedicated, with the remains of a Roman villa at the convent of San Giovanni del Palco.

Castello Lancellotti

History and architecture · Castles and fortresses · Viewpoint

Of Lombard-Norman origin, it is mentioned for the first time in a document of 976 and belonged to Sanseverino, Orsini and Pignatelli before the purchase by the Lancellotti in 1632. Fired by French troops in 1799, it was rebuilt in the late nineteenth century by Prince Filippo Lancellotti in an eclectic style: the crenellated walls contain two courtyards with fountains, apartments, representative rooms, stables, chapel and a tower with loggia overlooking the Vallo di Lauro.

Museo Umberto Nobile

Museums · Themed

In Palazzo Pignatelli (XVII century), which also houses a museum of naïf art, the civic museum dedicated to the builder of dirigibles and polar explorer born in Lauro in 1885, which in 1926 overwhelmed the North Pole with Norge and in 1928 shipwrecked on the pack with Italy. Inaugurated in 1988 and re-arranged between 1994 and 1996, it exhibits instruments and garments of the two polar expeditions, drawings and projects, correspondence on Nobile's flights and honours.

Villa romana di San Giovanni in Palco

History and architecture · Archaeology

Outside the village, at the convent of San Giovanni del Palco on the border with Taurano: the excavations of the Soprintendenza of 1981 have brought to light about 1330 m2 of a villa on three levels, dating from the late Republican age to the first century AD, rendered uninhabitable by a landslide. You can see the thermal sector with frigidary, tepidary, calidary and circular laconicum and a nymph with niches in marble tiles and vitreous pasta and a hunting mosaic with deer, dog and amorino.
