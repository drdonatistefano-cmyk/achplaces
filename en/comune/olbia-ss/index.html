What to see in Olbia

Porta della Sardegna nord-oriente and economic center of Gallura, overlooking the marine area of Tavolara: the Romanesque basilica of San Simplicio, the archaeological museum on the islet of Peddone with the Roman ships sunk by the Vandals, the remains of the Roman city, the castle of Pedres and a rich nuragic heritage, between beaches like Pittulongu and Porto Istana and villages like San Pantaleo and Porto Rotondo.

Museo archeologico di Olbia

Museums · Archaeological · Free entry

On the islet of Peddone in front of the old port, in a building reminiscent of a moored ship. On the ground floor wrecks of roman onerary ships, trees and rudders, the reconstruction of two ships set on fire in the vandal attack of the fifth century, a medieval wreck and the plastic of the port of the second century A.D.; on the first floor the history of the city from prehistory to the modern age, with Nuragic, Phoenician and Greek materials, a granite stele with the goddess Tanit, the heads of the emperors Domitian and Domitia, a Hercules and an Egyptian statue of Osiris. Tuesday-Saturday 8-13 and 16-19; audio guides and guided tours, groups over 30 people by reservation.

  • Where Isola Peddone, Porto Vecchio, Olbia
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 2 hours or more
  • Tourist portal page www.sardegnaturismo.it

Basilica di San Simplicio

Places of worship · Basilicas

Wanted by the judges of Gallura in three phases between the second half of the 11th and the middle of the 12th century, it is the most important religious monument of north-eastern Sardinia: three naves in local granite, about 33 for 13 meters and 12 high, on a hill once outside the walls. It is dedicated to Bishop Simplicius, martyr on May 15, 304; in 1614 under the high altar were found the relics of the Saints Simplicius, Rosula, Diocletian and Fiorenzio. In the area a Roman necropolis of about 450 tombs; in mid-May the feast of the patron with costume procession and Palio della Stella.

Museum Civitatense - Basilica di San Simplicio

Museums · City history

Museum path that tells the hidden history of the basilica, built on the tomb of the Olbian martyrs and became the cathedral of the ancient diocese of Civita; underground there emerged a temple dedicated to the cult of Ceres and numerous burials. Monday-Saturday 10-17, guided tours; entrance fee.

Tracce della città punica e romana

History and architecture · Archaeology · Free entry

Olbia became Roman with the conquest of Sardinia and in the imperial age it was a commercial center and naval base. The forum was located in the area of the current town hall, near the port: a stretch of the original pavement is visible at the intersection between via Dante and corso Umberto I. The Carthaginian walls are partly visible in Via Torino; in the ancient port, in Via D'Annunzio, the remains of the Roman port and the wrecks of 24 boats were found.

  • Where Via Dante angolo corso Umberto I, Olbia
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes
  • Tourist portal page www.sardegnaturismo.it

Sources: sardegnaturismo.it

Acquedotto romano di sa Rughittula

History and architecture · Archaeology

Considered the most intact aqueduct of Sardinia, dated between the 1st and 3rd century AD and restored or rebuilt between III and IV: it brought water from the sources of Cabu Abbas to the thermal baths for about 3.5 km. You can see a section of about 100 meters with a rectangular cistern; other remains in Via Canova and Via Nanni.

Castello di Pedres

History and architecture · Castles and fortresses

On a granite rock about 140 meters above sea level, about 5 km south of the center: probably built in the 13th century under the Pisan Visconti, in the mid-300s passed to the Hospitallers of St. John of Jerusalem and then to the Aragonese, and was abandoned from the following century. A recent staircase rises to the top, where the quadrangular mast is about 10 meters high (two of the four original floors) and an underground cistern; halfway up the remains of a small watchtower.

  • Where Località Pedres, circa 5 km a sud di Olbia
  • Entry To be checked
  • Suggested time 1-1.5 hours
  • Tourist portal page www.sardegnaturismo.it

Tomba di Giganti su Mont'e s'Abe

History and architecture · Archaeology

About 300 meters from the castle of Pedres, a funerary collective monument of more than 4,000 years ago, among the largest of the kind in Sardinia: born as allée couverte in the culture of Bonnanaro, around 1600 BC was transformed into a tomb of Giants with exedra and stele. Approximately 28 meters long and 6 meters wide, it has an inner chamber of about 10 meters; in the center of the exedra stood a stele of granite about 4 meters high.

  • Where Località Pedres, Olbia
  • Entry To be checked
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes
  • Tourist portal page www.sardegnaturismo.it

Pozzo sacro di sa Testa

History and architecture · Archaeology

A well temple dating back to the XV-XIII century B.C., discovered in the 1930s, looking for a source of water: a little less than 18 meters long, granite, trachyte and shale, with a lock plant, circular courtyard, vestibule and a staircase of 17 steps that descends to the room covered in tholos, almost 7 meters high. You can reach the road of the Industrial Port towards Pittulongu and the signs for Cala Saccaia.

  • Entry To be checked
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes

Nuraghe Riu Mulinu

History and architecture · Archaeology · Viewpoint

On a rocky spur at the top of Cabu Abbas, almost 250 meters above the Gulf of Olbia, with views up to Tavolara: circular tower of about 8 meters of diameter of the XIV-XIII century BC, surrounded by a wall of about 220 meters that encloses the rocks. Under the ladder a sacrificial pit with burning bones; in the excavations of 1936 a female bronze with amphora appeared on the head.

Porto Istana

Nature and landscape · Sea and beaches · Free entry · Viewpoint

Four small white sand coves separated by rock stripes, at the foot of the capo Ceraso promontory in the hamlet of Murta Maria, the northern limit of the protected marine area of Capo Coda Cavallo, overlooking Tavolara. The floor is gently sloping, suitable for children; accessible to disabled people, connected by bus to the city, with ample parking and rental of equipment.

Isola di Tavolara

Nature and landscape · Parks and nature reserves · Free entry · Viewpoint

A limestone-granite relief, 560 metres high and about 4 km long, in the territory of Olbia, called the smallest kingdom in the world. According to the tradition, Carlo Alberto made its inhabitants Giuseppe Bertoleoni, whose descendants are still the only residents. It arrives at Spalmatore di terra, with marina and refreshment points; every July it hosts the film festival "One night in Italy." It gives the name to the marine protected area Tavolara-Punta Coda Cavallo, suitable for boat excursions, diving and snorkeling.

  • Entry Free

San Pantaleo

History and architecture · Villages · Free entry

Borgata di Olbia on the granite massif of Cugnana, near the Costa Smeralda, born in the late nineteenth century around a small country church and called "the village of the artists": houses in Gallura style in pink granite, square with atelier and artisan workshops. On Thursdays, in spring and summer, antiques and handicrafts markets were shot; here scenes of "The spy that loved me" were shot (1976).

  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 1-1.5 hours
  • Tourist portal page www.sardegnaturismo.it

Sources: sardegnaturismo.it

Porto Rotondo

History and architecture · Villages · Free entry

Frazione di Olbia tra i golfi di Cugnana e di Marinella, born in 1964 on the initiative of the Venetians Luigi and Nicolò Donà dalle Rose, with architecture inspired in Venice: the main square is Piazza San Marco. Port tour with 800 berths, walk along the seafront, Piazza Rudalza and the beach i Sassi; at the end of August the Big Game of offshore fishing.
