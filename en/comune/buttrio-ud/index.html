What to see in Buttrio

Country of the wine of the Eastern Hills: in the area of the eighteenth-century Villa of Toppo Florio, where since 1933 the Regional Wine Fair is held, the Museum of Wine Civilization exhibits about 2900 objects of peasant viticulture; on the hill stands the complex of the castle of Buttrio, and the Villa Bartolini contains frescoes of Chiarottini and Canal.

Museo della civiltà del vino

Museums · Themed

In the area of Villa di Toppo Florio, about 2900 objects document the peasant viticulture from the nineteenth century to the mid-twentieth century: sections on viticulture and wine making, the workshop of the carpenter workshop rebuilt, trade and consumption of wine, a Friulian inn of the thirties and a documentary collection. Open only by reservation (tel. 0432 63611).

Villa di Toppo Florio e parco

History and architecture · Palaces and villas · Viewpoint

Built in the first half of the eighteenth century on an existing building and modified in the nineteenth century, it passed to the Florio at the end of the century, which held it until 1947; then it was college, school and finally property of the Municipality. The tripartite façade has a central rustic body with balcony and tympanum; the landscape park of the second half of the nineteenth century, attributed to Giuseppe Rho and surrounded by a crenellated wall, descends on the hill with a boulevard of palm trees and exhibits Eagles finds collected by Francesco di Toppo. Here on May 7, 1933 the first regional wine fair opened, which the villa still hosts.

Castello di Buttrio (Villa de Portis Morpurgo)

History and architecture · Castles and fortresses · Viewpoint

The castle of Buttrio was destroyed in 1306 and rebuilt several times; in the seventeenth century the de Portis acquired the property and started the villa, then passed to Varmo, Morpurgo, Vidoni and Felluga, who restored it. The neoclassical dominical body with pentapartite façade flanks an older building with towers and the small church of Santi Gervasio and Protasio, between park and vineyards; today it is home, winery and restaurant.

Villa Bartolini Danieli

History and architecture · Palaces and villas

Built in the seventeenth century by the Bartolini family on a rural building, it is reached by a long avenue of cypresses: the central body on three floors is flanked by two square towers connected by a porch and loggia. The lounge has allegorical and landscape frescoes from the mid-eighteenth century by Francesco Chiarottini and Giambattista Canal, restored since 1987; today it is home and restaurant.
