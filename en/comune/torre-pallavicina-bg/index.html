What to see in Torre Pallavicina

Small town on the border between the provinces of Bergamo, Brescia and Cremona, along the Oglio, linked from 1070 to the Counts Barbò; the symbol is the complex of the Tower of Tristan (XV century) and Pallavicino, immersed in a park of plane trees and lime trees of 25,000 m2; in the hamlet Santa Maria stands the sixteenth-century church of Santa Maria Assunta.

Torre e Palazzo Pallavicina

History and architecture · Palaces and villas · Viewpoint

The tower, called Torre di Tristano, was built in the fifteenth century by Tristano Sforza for defensive purposes; next, in the sixteenth century, the Marquis Adalberto Pallavicino erected the palace that gave the name to the village, with the motto "SIBI ET AMICIS" engraved on the arcades of the facade. The complex combines a military fortress with a seventeenth-century residence, in a park of plane trees and lindens of 25,000 m2. According to popular tradition an underground passage connected the tower to the castle of Soncino, but attempts to explore it failed and the entrance was walled.

Chiesa di Santa Maria Assunta

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches

Church of the hamlet Santa Maria, dating back to the XVI century and restored in the XX. Inside it preserves paintings from the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries and a Solaro's altarpiece depicting the Assumption. The municipality is included in the Park of Oglio Nord, established in 1988.
