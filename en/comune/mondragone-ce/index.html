What to see in Mondragone

Heir of the Roman colony of Sinuessa, founded in 296 BC along the Appian Way, Mondragone preserves the remains of the city submerged at the Vagnole, the Fortress Montis Dragonis on Mount Petrino and an archaeological civic museum with finds from the Paleolithic to the Roman age.

Museo civico archeologico «Biagio Greco»

Museums · Archaeological

It collects the findings of the excavation campaigns financed by the municipality. The oldest materials date back to Aurignatian (about 34,000-27,000 years ago) and include engraved pebbles and bone fragments; they follow protohistoric, archaic and Roman-age objects, including materials related to the Aurunci.

Sinuessa, area archeologica

History and architecture · Archaeology

Cologne founded by the Romans in 296 BC and then important stop of the Via Appia, famous for the thermal baths frequented by Roman matrons and patricians; in 375 it suffered enormous damage due to a catastrophic earthquake. The remains of the submerged city are located in the hamlet Le Vagnole. On the occasion of the Appia Day 2026 the Superintendence opened the Regina Viarum Archaeological Park in Mondragone with free guided tours.

Rocca Montis Dragonis

History and architecture · Castles and fortresses · Viewpoint

The fortress was built between the 8th and 9th centuries by the survivors of Sinuessa and abandoned between the 15th and 16th centuries; since 2001 the municipality finances an excavation campaign. It rises about 500 meters on Mount Petrino with wide view; the front is too steep, you climb from the road on the back and then walk on a path. During the feast of St. Michael, in early October, the castle was illuminated.

Chiesa di San Michele extra moenia

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches

13th century church in Romanesque-Gothic style in the Sant'Angelo district. It preserves a protorinascimental fresco of the Virgin with the Child and saints.

Santuario del Belvedere

Places of worship · Sanctuaries

The sanctuary dates back to about 1200; devastated by the Saracens between 1542 and 1550, it was ruled by the Carmelites from 1569 to 1624.
