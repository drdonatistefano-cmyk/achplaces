What to see in Camporgiano

Camporgiano, on the right bank of the Serchio between the Apuan Alps and the Apennines, was from 1446 until the unification of Italy dominion of the Este, which left the Fortress with the Civic collection of Renaissance ceramics. The municipality is crossed by the Via del Volto Santo and houses churches with wooden sculptures of Pisano, in Camporgiano and in the hamlet of Puglianella.

Rocca Estense

History and architecture · Castles and fortresses · Viewpoint

Built in the fifteenth century on a rocky spur overlooking the main square, it is an example of extensive military architecture with trapezoidal walls and truncated corner towers; on top there is a hanging garden with conifers. The earthquake of 1920 left little of its original structure; inside you can see the base of the bell tower of the Romanesque church of San Sisto and stretches of medieval walls emerged from the excavations, and at the feet there is the stone fountain called \"pilon" .

Civica raccolta di ceramiche rinascimentali

Museums · Archaeological

Set up on two floors in the extense castle, it exhibits ceramics from the beginning of the fifteenth century to the second half of the seventeenth century, largely recovered from the drains emptied during the restorations of the sixties: graffite, faenza majolica (sign of the extensive domination), mugs, bowls and engraved cans, with motifs related to the fertility cycle and animals. On the first floor everyday objects such as spoons and home furnishings.

  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes
  • Tourist portal page www.visittuscany.com

Chiesa di San Jacopo

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches

Medieval church in Romanesque style restored in 1838. Above the main altar there is a Holy Trinity of the Camaiorese Francesco Pellegrinetti and under the apse a Madonna with Child in wood carved by Andrea Pisano.

Via del Volto Santo, tappa Camporgiano-Castelnuovo

Nature and landscape · Hills and trails · Free entry · Viewpoint

The path of the Holy Face, branch of the Via Francigena, runs from Pontremoli to Lucca in about 150 km divided into ten stages and ends at the cathedral of San Martino, which preserves the wooden crucifix of the Holy Face. Camporgiano is on the seventh stage, between Piazza al Serchio and Castelnuovo di Garfagnana; the stretch from Camporgiano to Castelnuovo measures about 10 km.

  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 2 hours or more
  • Tourist portal page www.visittuscany.com
