What to see in Castell'Azzara

Amiata mining country with views of the Val di Paglia and Lazio, gathered around the Aldobrandesca fortress and the church of San Nicola; in the territory the Natural Reserve of Monte Penna, the Grotta del Sassocolato, the galleries of Cornacchini, the Rocca Silvana above Selvena and the Villa Sforzesca of Cardinal Alessandro Sforza.

6 places to visit in Castell'Azzara, in the province of Grosseto (Tuscany). Every entry is checked against official sources.

Centro storico, Rocca e chiesa di San Nicola

History and architecture · Squares and old town · Free entry · Viewpoint

On the highest point of the village the fortress built by the Aldobrandeschi in the 13th century, remained to them until 1439 when it passed to the Sforza with the county of Santa Fiora: today it is a two-storey building flanked by the clock tower. In front of the church of St. Nicholas, several times restored, with a painting from the seventeenth century attributed to Sienese painters; the church of Our Lady of the Rosary contains paintings from the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries. From the village, whose name according to legend comes from a game of dice between the Pope and the Emperor, the view spans the Val di Paglia and Lazio.

  • Where Castell'Azzara
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 1-1.5 hours
  • Tourist portal page www.visittuscany.com

Rocca Silvana

History and architecture · Castles and fortresses · Viewpoint

Aldobrandesco Castle on top of a mountain near Selvena, a few kilometers from the capital, strategic point for the control of the nearby mines of cinnabar; from the ruins the view embraces Selvena, abandoned mines and woods.

  • Where Presso Selvena, Castell'Azzara
  • Entry To be checked
  • Suggested time 1-1.5 hours
  • Tourist portal page www.visittuscany.com

Sources: visittuscany.com

Villa Sforzesca

History and architecture · Palaces and villas

Built in 1576 (1580 according to another card of the same portal) for Cardinal Alessandro Sforza, as a summer residence and base for the repression of the brigandage; after the death of the cardinal he fell for a long time. Recently restored, it houses gates, coats of arms, remains of fortifications and frescoes, and houses works by Carlo Alberto Torlai and architectural drawings by Fabio Rossi.

Visit only on request according to Visit Tuscany

  • Entry To be checked
  • Suggested time 1-1.5 hours
  • Tourist portal page www.visittuscany.com

Sources: visittuscany.com

Riserva naturale del Monte Penna

Nature and landscape · Parks and nature reserves · Free entry

Established in 1996 on the south-western slopes of Amiata, it protects about 1,000 hectares on a series of peaks, the highest Poggio della Vecchia (1,086 m) and Civitella (1,107 m). Woods of ash, beech and oak, with the "Bosco della Fonte" (Bosco della Fonte) of great naturalistic interest and monumental chestnut trees near Selvena; there live buzzards, allocches, common owls, foxes, singers, wild boars and roe deer.

  • Where Monte Penna, Castell'Azzara
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 2 hours or more
  • Tourist portal page www.visittuscany.com

Sources: visittuscany.com

Grotta del Sassocolato

Nature and landscape · Caves and geosites

Karst cavities in the limestone of the Poggio delle Forche, about one kilometer from the village following the signs; the name means "saxo" dripping, for the concretions on the walls. Once used as a barn, in the visitable stretches it has stairs and houses large colonies of bats, up to 2,000-2,500 specimens in summer; in the surrounding mining tunnels and Etruscan tombs.

Closed since 1995 to protect bats; visitable in summer with the Speleological Group L'Orso di Castell'Azzara

  • Where Grotta del Sassocolato, Poggio delle Forche, 58034 Castell'Azzara (GR)
  • Entry To be checked
  • Suggested time 1-1.5 hours
  • Tourist portal page www.visittuscany.com

Sentiero ed ex gallerie minerarie del Cornacchino

Nature and landscape · Caves and geosites

One of the oldest mines in Amiata, reachable with a short detour from the mine route that starts from Castell'Azzara: the narrow galleries, where women and children worked in the 19th century, can be visited safely today.
