What to see in Gragnano

On the Lattari Mountains, Gragnano is the city of pasta: the Valle dei Mulini, a thirteenth-century hydraulic complex that ground the wheat, the nineteenth-century square of the historic pasta factories, the large church of Corpus Domini with works by Luca Giordano and Domenico Morelli, and in the hamlet of Castello the church of Santa Maria Assunta, a national monument since 1927.

Valle dei Mulini

Nature and landscape · Landscapes

13th century hydraulic complex, important technological innovation: in the Middle Ages the mills grind the wheat for the population, exploiting the spring water of the Lattari Mountains which fell by gravity from one mill to another at different altitudes, with a domino effect. The valley is part of an ancient route that connected Castellammare di Stabia to Amalfi and in the Seven and nineteenth centuries was painted by the artists of the Grand Tour.

Chiesa del Corpus Domini

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches

The most important place of worship of Gragnano, founded in 1555 and completed in 1571, then covered with Baroque decorations between 1720 and 1750; after the earthquake of 1805 the façade was redesigned in 1836 by Camillo Ranieri. The ceiling houses a canvas by Francesco Maria Russo of about 400 square meters, among the largest in Europe; there are the Transfiguration of Marco Pino da Siena, the canvases of the apse and the Last Supper of Giacinto Diano, the Dinner in Emmaus by Domenico Morelli and the Madonna in Gloria by Luca Giordano. You climb up a staircase in lava stone; the carved wooden portal dates back to 1584 and next to it stands the Romanesque bell tower.

Chiesa della Madonna del Carmine

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches

Built in the first half of the sixteenth century and linked to the settlement of the Carmelites, then passed to the Franciscans after the suppressions of 1806; the cult of the Madonna del Carmine was tied to the pasta makers of the city. Piazza Marconi (ex Piazza San Leone)

History and architecture · Squares and old town · Free entry

Piazza born in the nineteenth century with the urban renewal linked to the trade of pasta: here were founded the historical pasta factories The nineteenth-century fountain, designed by the architect Giuseppe Fazzini, has a tub in stone and a grooved cup from which comes out a cone with water jet; it preserves two biceps masks in marble of the seventeenth century of the previous fountain, with shields with wheat ears, symbol of the city.

Museo della Biodiversità - CITES

Museums · Themed

Inside the Municipal Library, it exhibits in showcases about 150 specimens of animals or their parts protected by the Washington Convention (CITES), in force in Italy since 1980; the route is designed for children, families and schools. Hours from the MiC card: Monday and Wednesday 9-13 and 16-18, Tuesday, Thursday and Friday 9-13, Saturday and Sunday closed.
