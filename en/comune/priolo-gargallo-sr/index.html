What to see in Priolo Gargallo

A coastal municipality north of Syracuse, autonomous since 1979, rises around the octagonal square desired by Tommaso Gargallo in 1807; on the Magnisi Peninsula remain the remains of Thapsos, an emporium of the Bronze Age, next to the Riserva delle Saline di Priolo, where 216 species of birds have been recorded.

Riserva naturale orientata Saline di Priolo

Nature and landscape · Parks and nature reserves

The wetlands of the ancient salt pans, established in 2000 (54.5 hectares) and managed by LIPU, at the slopes of Mount Climati along the coast, in the middle of the industrial pole. There were 216 species of birds, about 40% of the Italian ones, including the major stern (symbol of the reserve), the courier of Leschenault and the firecracker fulvo; the sultan chicken, extinct in Sicily in the fifties, reintroduced by LIPU and INFS, has nested there spontaneously. Open every day; guided tours for groups and schools by reservation.

Thapsos (Penisola Magnisi)

History and architecture · Archaeology

On the Magnisi Peninsula, a site of extraordinary archaeological value, remains of ancient Thapsos, one of the great commercial empori of the Bronze Age (from the end of the 15th century BC), such as Lipari, Cannatello and Ustica. According to the history of the municipality, in the second millennium BC, King Ibleon had chosen his residence there.

Piazza «4 Canti» e chiesa dell'Angelo Custode

History and architecture · Squares and old town · Free entry

The octagonal square called "Canti" was built in 1807 by the will of Tommaso Gargallo, together with a small church and forty houses, the first nucleus of the village; the small church, named after the Guardian Angel, was elevated to a parish by the bishop of Syracuse in 1814 and the Marquis bought the clock for the bell tower.

  • Where Piazza 4 Canti, Priolo Gargallo
  • Entry Free
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes
  • Tourist portal page www.comune.priologargallo.sr.it
