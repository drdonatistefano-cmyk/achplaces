What to see in Giurdignano

Country of Salento near Otranto called "Giardino megalithico d'Italia" for the numerous menhirs and the seven dolmen still intact scattered between center and countryside, including the Dolmen Stabile and the Menhir of São Paulo. In the village the Crypt of St. Saviour of the eighth century, the Column of St. Rocco and the underground mill 16th century Trappitello del Duca.

Dolmen di Giurdignano e Dolmen Stabile

History and architecture · Archaeology · Viewpoint

Giurdignano is considered the largest and most diverse megalithic site in Italy, with seven dolmen still intact. The Dolmen Stable, along the road to Giuggianello between olive trees and dry stone walls on a plateau at 102 meters, dates back to the beginning of the II millennium BC: it has a rectangular plan with a covering plate resting on a series of blocks, and for many scholars it was part of a wider megalithic structure. Other imposing dolmens, perhaps used as altars, are Orfine, Chiancuse, Peschio Gravasce, Santa Barbara, Sferracavalli, Paolo Niuri and Grassi.

Percorso dei menhir: San Vincenzo, Menhir Gemelli e Vicinanze

History and architecture · Archaeology

In the town there are the menhirs of Vico Nuovo (the "Menhir Gemelli") Cross of Fausa, Santa Maria di Costantinopoli and San Vincenzo, the latter in a small square of Via Menhir, probably of the Bronze Age and starting point of the megalithic route. In the countryside, beyond the underground mill, the Menhir Vicinanze 1 door on the eastern face two crosses engraved after the edict of Thessalonica, and about 200 meters the Menhir Vicinanze 2 rises on a rocky spur; other menhirs are Mount Tongolo, Palanzano and Madonna del Rosario.

Frantoio ipogeo Trappitello del Duca

Local life and traditions · Food and wine

Built in 1518 under a farm outside the old town and entirely dug into the rocky bank, it is the only remaining of the four underground mills that the village had around 1870. A staircase with a barrel vault goes down to the large room with the base of the millstone pool; around the "slaves" for the olives, the deposits, the rest of the "trappetari" and the mule stable, with presses rebuilt to the Calabrian and the Genoese. He worked for over three and a half centuries until 1940; he was abandoned, he was bought by the municipality in 2000 and equipped with information panels.

Piazza Municipio: Chiesa Madre e Colonna di San Rocco

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches

In the square stands the votive column of San Rocco, erected in 1772 in white Lecce stone with the polychrome statue of the saint on top: according to tradition, it was built by a ship captain who escaped a plague that had killed much of the crew. In front of the Church Mother of the Transfiguration, originally dedicated to San Rocco, built in the second eighteenth century, with a sober façade and Baroque interior with stuccoes, furnishings and paintings by Giandomenico Catalano and Oronzo Tiso.
