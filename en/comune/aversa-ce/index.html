What to see in Aversa

Norman city between Naples and Caserta: the Cathedral of San Paolo started in 1053 with the walk-in and the House of Loreto, the Diocesan Museum, the monastery of San Francesco delle Nunche and a center rich in churches, monasteries and palaces at court, as well as the birthplaces of Cimarosa and Jommelli.

Duomo di San Paolo

Places of worship · Cathedrals

It was built on the remains of a small agricultural sanctuary, it was begun under Prince Riccardo Drengot in 1053 and completed by his son Giordano around 1090, as a reminder of an inscription on the portal of the left transept; of the Norman period remains the walkway with radial chapels. In 1630 the House of Loreto was built, a copy of that of the Marche basilica, and in the eighteenth century it was almost completely renovated according to the plan of the Roman Carlo Buratti: three naves on pillars, octagonal dome, bell tower and Baroque façade.

Museo diocesano

Museums · Old masters

Distributed between the walkway of the cathedral, the chapel of Loreto and the Old Treasure, it tells the evolution of the Duomo with frescoes, sculptures, tombstones and paintings: the portal of the Nymphios, the tombs of the bishops Giorgio Manzolo and Balduinis, the Triptych of San Michele of the school of Antoniazzo Romano, the Madonna of the Gonfalone by Francesco Solimena, the silver throne by Aniello Guariniello (1755), the Breviarium aversanum (1499) and seven fifteenth-century tables by Angiolillo Arcuccio.

open mon, mer, sat 10-12, Fri 10-18, sat 16.30-18; closed during liturgical celebrations

Chiesa di Santa Maria degli Angeli

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches

A small late Baroque church near the court of Naples North, entrusted to the Confraternity of the Gonfalone del Santo Sepolcro: Latin cross plan, dome of the first decades of the eighteenth century and valuable majolica floor; on the high altar stood the canvas of Francesco Solimena with San Bonaventura that receives from the Madonna the Gonfalone.

according to the FAI the church needs urgent restoration
