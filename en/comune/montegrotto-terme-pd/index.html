What to see in Montegrotto Terme

Spa resort at the foot of the Euganean Hills, known in Europe for its mud therapy and frequented already in Roman times, as shown by the excavations of the Augustan thermal complex; in the city the International Museum of Art Glass, the Duomo on the Montagnone Hill, the Oratory of the Madonna and Villa Draghi with its park of 30 hectares.

Area archeologica di viale Stazione / via degli Scavi

History and architecture · Archaeology

Remains of a monumental spa complex of Augustan age, discovered between 1781 and 1788 and excavated again in 1953 and between 1965 and 1970: three large pools with the water supply system (one fully visible), a small theatre or odeon, a building with two apses, thermal baths and a hydraulic network of over 200 meters. From April to October open on Sunday 11-13 and from 15 am to sunset, on other days by reservation; visible from outside.

closed from November to March (open April-October), visible from outside

Museo internazionale del vetro d'arte e delle terme

Museums · Themed

About 500 glass works, with pieces by Del Negro and Guarnieri, Giuman, Seguso, Scarpa, Toso and Bianconi, glass sculptures, design objects, the historic collection of Luxardo containers, Nancy glass and Russian glasses from Vladimir's region, as well as Bohemian, English and German works.

Villa Draghi e il suo parco

History and architecture · Palaces and villas · Viewpoint

Small neo-Gothic villa with square plan, rebuilt in 1848-1850 by Pietro Scapin on the site of the seventeenth-century holiday house of Alvise Lucadello, with swallow-tailed blackbirds inspired by Palazzo Ducale; it takes its name from the family that owned it from 1874 to 1965 and since 1972 it is owned by the Municipality of Abano Terme. The interiors are bare and host conferences and events; the park of about 30 hectares, halfway along the coast of Monte Alto, includes part of the forest with trails, including the route Villa Draghi and Monte Ceva, about 10 km in the Regional Park of the Euganean Hills.
