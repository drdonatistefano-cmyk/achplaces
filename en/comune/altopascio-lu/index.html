What to see in Altopascio

Altopascio, the city of bread between Lucca and Pistoia, is a historic stop on the Via Francigena, where the Knights of the Tau welcomed and cared for pilgrims in their Hospital for centuries. The center preserves the church of San Jacopo with the bell tower of 1280 and the bell tower "La Smarrita" , Piazza degli Ospitalieri and the Medici Loggia; just outside there is the reserve of Lake Sibolla.

Chiesa di San Jacopo Maggiore

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches

In 1988 the remains of a first church of the late XI century emerged; the medieval building, dedicated to the saints Iacopo, Egidio and Cristoforo patrons of the pilgrims, was restored in the fifteenth century and in the sixteenth century had a door to colored marbles on the south side. After the closure of the hospital desired by Pietro Leopoldo, in 1827-1830 the church was rebuilt with different orientation and the medieval part became the transept.

Campanile e campana «La Smarrita»

History and architecture · Towers

The bell tower in Romanesque-Lucchese style next to the church was built in 1280 and, with its wounds, also had defensive function: it still looks like a medieval fortress. It hosts the bell "La Smarrita" which every night at the night of the dark sounded to guide the pilgrims to the hospital.

Piazza degli Ospitalieri e complesso dello Spedale del Tau

History and architecture · Squares and old town · Free entry

It is the most evocative part of the old town, with an octagonal well, next to the complex of the ancient Hospital managed by the Hospital Order of the Friars of San Jacopo, known from the second half of the 11th century as Knights of the Tau. Here the welcome to the pilgrims of the Via Francigena became a real medical hospitality with doctors and lay surgeons. In the square there is also the Sala Peregrinatio, exhibition space for contemporary art exhibitions.

Raccolta storica nel Loggiato Mediceo

Museums · Archaeological

Housed in the Medici Loggia (1472-75), which dominates Piazza Garibaldi, ancient market square, and still bears the Medici coat of arms on a column, documents the history of Altopascio. It exhibits mainly Renaissance ceramics and stone fragments of medieval buildings from the excavations of the eighties and nineties, from the large fifteenth-century orcs for wheat to daily dishes; born in the municipal library, it is an exhibition ordered since 1990 and documentation center since 1991.

Riserva naturale del Lago di Sibolla

Nature and landscape · Parks and nature reserves

The reserve, of about 60 hectares, protects a small wetland considered one of the most important marshes of Tuscany from the floristic point of view. It makes one imagine how the Padule di Fucecchio and that of Bientina appeared before the nineteenth-century Bonifice.
