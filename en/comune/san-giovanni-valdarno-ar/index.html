What to see in San Giovanni Valdarno

New land founded by the Florentines in 1299 with plant attributed to Arnolfo di Cambio and birthplace of Masaccio: between Masaccio Square and Cavour Square overlook the Palazzo d'Arnolfo with the Museo delle Terre Nuove, the Basilica of Santa Maria delle Grazie with the Museum of the Annunciation of Blessed Angelico, San Lorenzo and the Pieve di San Giovanni Battista; on Corso Italia the Casa Masaccio, contemporary art center.

9 places to visit in San Giovanni Valdarno, in the province of Arezzo (Tuscany). Every entry is checked against official sources.

Piazza Masaccio, Piazza Cavour e Corso Italia

History and architecture · Squares and old town · Free entry · Viewpoint

The center of the new land founded in 1299, first called San Giovanni in Altura and then Castel San Giovanni: a large square divided into two, Piazza Masaccio and Piazza Cavour, around the Palazzo d'Arnolfo, and Corso Italia flanked by medieval and Renaissance buildings. In January, for Sant'Antonio Abate, in Piazza Masaccio, pets and courtyard animals are blessed. The walk proposed by Visit Tuscany takes about 2 hours with the museum.

Palazzo d'Arnolfo e Museo delle Terre Nuove

History and architecture · Palaces and villas

Built between two and fourteenth century between the two squares, it housed the representative of the Florentine municipality and the town council, with rooms for wheat in case of war or famine; the works of the first fifteenth century for the apartments of the vicar marked the role of St. John in the Valdarno. Portici, crenellated tower and about 250 coats of arms of the vicars on the façade, from the first fifteenth century to 1772; in the atrium frescoes and coats of arms of the fifteenth century and the Marzocco. National monument since 1909, the name "d'Arnolfo" dates back to the post-war period and the restoration of 1934. It houses the Museum of New Lands on the foundation of the new cities of the late Middle Ages.

Basilica di Santa Maria delle Grazie

Places of worship · Basilicas

Born from the miracle of Mona Tancia during the plague of 1478, when a grandmother fed her orphan grandson with prayer: the chapel of 1484 became three-nave church in 1523 and the neoclassical façade was built between 1840 and 1856. In the atrium there is the large polychrome terracotta by Giovanni della Robbia (1513), the Assunta that gives the belt to St. Thomas; inside the vaults frescoed at the end of the seventeenth century, the main altar rebuilt after 1596 on a project by Bernardo Buontalenti and the frescoes of the miracle, attributed by Vasari to a pupil of Perugino.

Museo della Basilica di Santa Maria delle Grazie

Museums · Old masters

Small museum of sacred art in the premises next to the basilica, with paintings, sculptures, furnishings and vestments from the late fourteenth to the eighteenth century coming from churches and convents of the territory. The masterpiece is the Annunciation of Blessed Angelico (1432-1440); there are works of Scheggia, brother of Masaccio, already in San Lorenzo, Mariotto di Cristofano, Jacopo del Sellaio, Domenico di Michelino, Giovanni da San Giovanni and the triptych of the Trinity between Saints of Mariotto of Nardo from the Pieve.

Casa Masaccio

Museums · Modern art

According to Ugo Procacci's archive research, Masaccio spent most of his childhood there (it is not the established birthplace). Purchased by the Municipality in the early sixties and renovated in the seventies, it is a contemporary art center with temporary exhibitions of Italian and European art, meetings, workshops, cinemas and concerts; the municipal collection, born from donations, includes works by Alighiero Boetti, Giovanni Anselmo, Alberto Garutti, Mauro Staccioli, Massimo Bartolini, Alberto Moretti, Venturino Venturi and Marco Airò.

  • Official website www.casamasaccio.it

Chiesa di San Lorenzo

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches

Gothic church of the fourteenth century on the side of Piazza Masaccio, which incorporated a smaller church as a right aisle; sandstone and brick facade. The restoration of 1903 brought to light the frescoes of Scheggia, the brother of Masaccio, including the Martyrdom of San Sebastiano; in the presbytery the triptych of the Coronation of the Virgin of Giovanni del Biondo (about 1374). In 1780 a mummified body was found in a pillar of the counterfaçade, still visible in a niche.

Pieve di San Giovanni Battista

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches

The baptismal church of the city, begun in 1312 next to the disappearance Porta Sant'Andrea, in front of the Palazzo d'Arnolfo: classic façade with pediment and three arched porch on Tuscan columns decorated with rounds of Robbiana school, inside with a single nave with wooden cover.

  • Where Piazza Cavour, San Giovanni Valdarno
  • Entry To be checked
  • Suggested time 30-45 minutes
  • Tourist portal page www.visittuscany.com

Sources: visittuscany.com

Chiesa della Santissima Annunziata

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches

Part of a complex that was a hospital of the community until the early sixteenth century and around 1528 became a monastery of Augustinian nuns; a small late Baroque church, renovated in the second half of the seventeenth century, with stuccoes, two canvases and the fresco of the ceiling of the Florentine Antonio Puglieschi, high altar of 1685 and a Madonna with Child attributed to the Master of the Nativity of Castello (about 1460).

Stufato alla sangiovannese

Local life and traditions · Food and wine

Plate mentioned in a document of the fifteenth century, of veal cooked slowly with a set of spices, celebrated every year by the Palio of the stew among the local cooks called "stofatari" (stofatari), other typical product is tarese, traditional salami recognized by Slow Food.
