What to see in Lacedonia

The center is an irpino with a Renaissance structure with medieval walls and four doors, linked to San Gerardo Maiella, who lived in the bishop's palace from 1741 to 1744: today the palace houses the diocesan Museum, next to the eighteenth-century Cathedral, the churches of Santa Maria della Consolazione and the Cancelata and the Castello Pappacota.

Museo diocesano San Gerardo

Museums · Themed

Opened on 12 August 1997 in the ancient bishop's palace, before the sixteenth century, where San Gerardo Maiella lived from 1741 to 1744 at the service of Bishop Albini. It has a lapidary section (stemmi, acquasantiere, architectural elements from the Middle Ages to the twentieth century) with the well of the miracle of Gerardiello, an archaeological section with Roman and Sanniti artifacts, including inscriptions in Osco and Latin, and an ethnographic section with a wine press of the fifteenth century.

Visitable only by appointment (sindaco@comune.lacedonia.av.it)

Cattedrale

Places of worship · Cathedrals

First stone laid on 28 September 1689 by Bishop Giambattista La Morea on the site of four chapels of confraternities; central nave completed in 1709 and consecration on 19 October 1766, when Bishop Nicola D'Amato raised it to basilica. Damaged by the earthquakes of 1930 and 1980, which modified its dome and facade, it was restored in 1988 on a project by architect Sergio Rosanova.

Chiesa di Santa Maria della Cancellata

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches

It rises, according to the source, on a pagan temple dedicated to Isis and before even to Hera and the Dioscuri, was Christianized around the second century and in the fourth century was the city's mother church; dedicated to the Assumption in 1486, it was abandoned after the construction of the new cathedral and restored after 1824.

Chiesa di Santa Maria della Consolazione

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches

Built in 1503 and enlarged in 1585 with a superior vault, it has a Romanesque portal and a early seventeenth-century holy waterworks; near the church remains a drinking trough of the time when cattle were brought there. In front is the Column of the Pedoca, coming from the excavations of the pagan temple, to which Bishop Marco Pedoca added the cross in 1587.

Castello Pappacota

History and architecture · Castles and fortresses

Built around 1500 to the west of the village by Ferdinando Pappacota, whose king Frederick of Aragon had granted the fief in 1496: it was a fortress with three towers, blackbirds, cannons and underground passages. Sold around 1700 to Andrea Doria Panfili, then passed to the Onorato family; remain the blackbirds of the south tower, wounds and an ancient well.

Centro storico e Via Tagliata

History and architecture · Squares and old town · Free entry · Viewpoint

Renaissance urban plant with medieval walls and four doors, stone portals of the Seven-nineteenth century, Palazzo Pandiscia (formerly Cappucci house, residence of San Gerardo), a Renaissance loggia and a Roman arch. Via Tagliata is the oldest street in the country: here in 212 BC the Roman proconsul Gneo Fulvio Centumalo was camped. In Piazza De Sanctis the bust of Francesco De Sanctis.
