Pieve di San Lorenzo

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches

Already documented as curtis in 877 and as parish church of the diocese of Reggio Emilia in 980, it passed to the Canossa (Boniface resided there in 1012); the Romanesque church in present brick, with three naves on cylindrical pillars with triabsidated transept and crypt, dates back to the end of the 11th century and was run by a community of canons. Tradition binds her to Matilde, but no document prior to the sixteenth century indicates her as a client. Abandoned from the second half of the seventeenth century, it was restored in 1932 (neoromanic façade and protyro with a Roman bas-relief are of that period); authentic the central nave, the heads of the transept and the apses with arches on semi-colons, while in the plumes of the apse two angels were emerged painted around 1100. Closed temporarily for restoration after the earthquake of May 2012.

Why go. One of the Romanesque parishes of the Matilda plain.
