Castello dei Cavalieri di Malta

History and architecture · Castles and fortresses

Born between 1150 and 1170 as the hospice of the Knights Hospitallers for the pilgrims going to Rome (documented from 1171), it was fortified in a square manor with corner towers and courtyard with three orders of loggias, in the final fifteenth-century form of the architect Fioravante Fioravanti from Bologna. In 1502 there was held the Diet of Magione, the conspiracy against Cesare Borgia described by Machiavelli in the Prince, re-enacted every summer; Benedict XIV (1746) and Pius VII (1805). In the Romanesque chapel of St. John the Baptist two frescoes by painters of the workshop of Pinturicchio (Nativity; Madonna and Child among Saints John the Baptist and James). Still owned by the Order of Malta, at the center of a farm.
