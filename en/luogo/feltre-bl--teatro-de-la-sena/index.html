Teatro de la Sena

History and architecture · Historic theatres · Paid entry

On the first floor of the sixteenth-century Palazzo della Ragione, from the Palladian porticos on Piazza Maggiore: the grand hall of the Major Council, completed in 1611, became the Social Theatre in 1684; here Carlo Goldoni, in Feltre in 1729-1730, discovered his passion for theatre. After lightning in 1769 and the long closing, in 1802 was redesigned by Giannantonio Selva, the architect of the Phoenix of Venice, with 24 stages; the decorations and curtain, the only surviving painting by Tranquillo Orsi, were restored. That's the little Phoenix. Open Friday, Saturday, Sunday and holidays 10-13 and 15-18 (October-March) or 15-19 (April-September); closed December 25 and January 1.

Why go. The small Phoenix where Goldoni discovered the theatre.
