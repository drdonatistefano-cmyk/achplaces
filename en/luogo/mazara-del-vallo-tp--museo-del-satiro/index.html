Museo del Satiro

Museums · Archaeological · Paid entry

In the sixteenth century deconsecrated church of Sant'Egidio is exposed the Dancing Satyr, bronze from the end of the fourth century BC attributed to the school of Praxitele: the left leg was fished in the spring of 1997, the body on March 4, 1998 by the fishing boat Capitan Ciccio, between Sicily and Capo Bon; it depicts a dancer in ecstasy with panther skin, restored by the Central Institute for Restoration. Also displayed is an elephant paw in punic-hellenistic bronze, a medieval cauldron, amphora and two cannons of Torretta Granitola.

Why go. The Greek bronze re-emerged from the sea.

  • Where Chiesa di Sant'Egidio, Piazza Plebiscito, Mazara del Vallo
  • Entry €8, reduced €4
  • Suggested time 1-1.5 hours
