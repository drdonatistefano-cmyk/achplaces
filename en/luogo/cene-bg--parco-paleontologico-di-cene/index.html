Parco paleontologico di Cene

Nature and landscape · Caves and geosites · Paid entry

After a landslide in 1965 the professor of science Don Antonio Canova found the first fossil, a fragment of fish; since 1970 the area has been protected and dug by the Civic Museum of Natural Sciences of Bergamo, which conserves thousands of fossils of the Triassic (about 220 million years ago): fish, crustaceans, molluscs, reptiles and plants. In 1973 there were found the remains of the Eudimorphodon ranzii, the oldest pterosaur in the world. Opened in 2002, it is a geosite of international importance: visitor center with multilingual panels, LIS and Braille supports, interactive stations and microscopes, naturalistic path to the fossiliferous site and simulated excavation area; free guided tours and workshops during the summer weekends (2026: 13 June-27 September, 14-19). Accessible to the disabled.

Why go. Where the oldest flying reptile in the world was found.

open only on weekends from June to September with obligatory booking; in the other months visits by reservation (ValSeriana)
