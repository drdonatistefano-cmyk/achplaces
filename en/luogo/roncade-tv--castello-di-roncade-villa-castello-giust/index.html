Castello di Roncade (Villa Castello Giustinian)

History and architecture · Castles and fortresses

The complex, donated around 900 by Otto II to the counts of Collalto and then destroyed by Cangrande della Scala, was rebuilt by the Venetian Girolamo Giustinian on a project attributed to Mauro Coducci: walls, towers and moat precede the villa by a few years, completed in 1508. It is the only Venetian villa still surrounded by crenellated walls, with loggias of Sansovino taste, external frescoes, an oratory dedicated to Sant'Anna with busts of Girolamo Giustinian and his wife Agnesina Badoer, and an entrance avenue with seventeenth-century statues of a company of footmen. From the early twentieth century it belongs to Ciani Bassetti, who produce wine there; in front of the castle stands the Monument to the fallen of Libero Andreotti (1924).

Why go. A Renaissance villa-fortress with vineyards and tastings.
