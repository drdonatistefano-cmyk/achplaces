Castello Piccolomini, Museo d'arte sacra della Marsica e Collezione Torlonia

History and architecture · Castles and fortresses · Paid entry · Viewpoint

Fortress built by Celano from 1392 at the behest of the counts, enlarged in 1451 by Leonello Acclozamora (noble floor and four corner towers) and completed as residence by Antonio Todeschini Piccolomini, grandson of Pius II, Count of Celano since 1463; it has a central court with well and loggia portico. Damaged by the earthquake of 1915 and rebuilt until 1960, since 1992 it houses the Museum of Sacred Art of Marsica, eight rooms of paintings, sculptures and goldsmiths from the 6th to the 18th century (two wooden doors carved from the 12th century, the triptych of Alba Fucens, the cross Orsini of 1334), and the Torlonia Collection with the findings emerged from the drying up of the Fucino, including a relief of the 2nd century A.D. with a city and its territory. Open from Tuesday to Sunday 9:30-18:30, closed on Mondays.

Why go. The castle passing between fortress and Renaissance palace, overlooking the Fucino.
