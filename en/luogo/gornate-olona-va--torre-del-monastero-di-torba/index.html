Torre del Monastero di Torba

History and architecture · Archaeology · UNESCO World Heritage · Paid entry

Rare example of late antique defensive architecture, born in the 5th century as a Roman military outpost downstream of Castelseprio castrum and then used by Goths, Byzantines and Longobards. In the 8th century the Benedictine nuns transformed it into oratory and mausoleum; on the second floor there are frescoes with Christ blessing and angels, the Virgin and Child, a group of saints and a theory of nuns in prayer, restored from 1978 to 2019 by Pinin Brambilla Barcilon. The excavations with the University of Padua found at the entrance a horse burial of the sixth century.

Why go. One of the few Roman towers left in northern Italy, frescoed by nuns and UNESCO heritage.
