Convitto Nazionale Giordano Bruno e chiesa di Sant'Antonio da Padova

History and architecture · Palaces and villas

A former Franciscan convent that the tradition ties to the passage of St. Francis in 1220; after the suppression of the convents decreed by Giuseppe Bonaparte in 1807 it became a national boarding house, attended from 1821 to 1827 by Luigi Settembrini, who later named it at Giordano Bruno. Portale in piperno of 1758; in the living room the ceiling houses the largest canvas in the world (700 sq m), painted in 1756 by Giovanni Funaro and workshop with the Immaculate Conception, St. Francis and St. Anthony within architectural squares. In the adjacent church of Sant'Antonio da Padova, on the sides of the marble altar, two angels, head of the altar by Giuseppe Sammartino (1761).

Why go. The 700 sq.m. painted ceiling and the angels of Sammartino.
