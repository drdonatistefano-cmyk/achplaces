Villa romana e Antiquarium

History and architecture · Archaeology · UNESCO World Heritage · Paid entry

Villa marittima d'otium of the Roman aristocracy, among the best preserved of the Tyrrhenian area: built in the early years of the first century A.D. (Julio-Claudia age) and in use until the 7th century, extends to the sea along the river Reginna Minor, on two or more floors around the triclinium-nympheo turned into a barrel, with mosaics of a hunting scene and a marine tiase. There are rooms decorated with stuccoes and frescoes and part of the viridarium surrounded by arcades and a large bathtub; discovered by chance in 1932, it was completely freed after the flood of 1954. On the terrace the Antiquarium displays frescoes, amphoras and finds of other villas in the area and excavations of Vietri and Tramonti.

Why go. A Roman villa on the sea with mosaics and frescoes.

Closed to the public since August 25, 2025 for restoration of decorated surfaces and mosaics and for accessibility; reopening to be communicated on official channels
