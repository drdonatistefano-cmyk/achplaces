Castello dei Principi d'Acaja

History and architecture · Castles and fortresses · Viewpoint

It was built by Filippo I d'Acaja between 1324 and 1332 as a fortress and was then transformed by the Savoys into a residence: Bona di Savoia and Cristina di Francia stayed there and in November 1562 Emanuele Filiberto signed the Treaty of Fossano; later it was the prison of the Waldenses and barracks. Four corner towers joined by crenellated walls, courtyard with loggia with fifteenth-century columns, the Room of the Grotesques frescoed by the Flemish Giovanni Caracca and a panoramic tower with views of the Monviso; in the sixteenth-century loggias the civic library (over 150,000 volumes). Guided tours only, from Wednesday to Sunday at 11 am and at 3 pm (reservation recommended); the route has about one hundred steep steps to spiral without handrail.

Why go. The symbol of the city, with views from the towers of the Alps.
