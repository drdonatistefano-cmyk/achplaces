Forte di Fortezza

History and architecture · Castles and fortresses

Built between 1833 and 1838 at the behest of Emperor Francis I of Austria and inaugurated by Ferdinand I, it was the largest construction site in Europe at the time, with 3,500-5,000 workers, about 20 million bricks and 250,000 cubic metres of granite. Never besieged, soon lost strategic importance and was a warehouse of weapons and barracks until 2003; opened to the public since 2005, it hosted the biennial Manifesta 7 and the provincial exhibition of 2009. Over 65,000 square meters it is divided into lower and upper forts, connected by an intermediate level and an underground staircase of 452 steps; inside a neo-Gothic church of the garrison and a provincial museum with the history of the fort, including the gold treasure of the Italian national bank hidden here in the Second World War.

Why go. The largest historical site in South Tyrol, among galleries and art exhibitions.
