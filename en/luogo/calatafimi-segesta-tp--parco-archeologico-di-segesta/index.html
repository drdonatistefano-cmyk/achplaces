Parco archeologico di Segesta

History and architecture · Archaeology · Viewpoint

A site stratified with helimal, Hellenistic-Roman remains of Muslim, Norman-Svevian and medieval era: Segesta, the main city of the Elimi and rival of Selinunte, was destroyed by Agatocle of Syracuse in 307 BC and rebuilt as a Roman city. Outside the walls stands the Doric temple of the fifth century BC, with 36 columns and remarkably intact; on the top of Mount Barbaro the Hellenistic theater (mid III century BC), with the cavea partly dug into the rock, which in summer hosts classical shows, music and poetry, even at dawn. You can also visit the two acropolis, the Vanella Cave linked to the shrine of Contrada Mango, the quarries of the temples and the remains of a Hellenistic necropolis. Integrated ticket available online or at the Park ticket office.

Why go. The Doric temple intact in a wild landscape.
