Cave di Cusa

History and architecture · Archaeology · Paid entry

Cava of limestoneite that provided the column rocks for the temples of Selinunte, at 11-12 km; the work suddenly interrupted in 409 BC with the Carthaginian siege and the blocks remained where they were: over sixty cylindrical boulders and a rough capital. Visited by Jean-Pierre Houel between 1776 and 1779, in 1999 he received the international award "Carlo Scarpa" (Carlo Scarpa). Open from 9 am to 1 pm; in the afternoon only by reservation for groups of at least 20 people.

Why go. An ancient construction site stopped for 2,400 years.
