Area archeologica e Antiquarium di Veleia

History and architecture · Archaeology · Paid entry · Viewpoint

Roman town hall built after the conquest of the 2nd century BC on the town of Ligurian Veleia, 469 meters on a terrace on the valley of Chero, still frequented in the 5th century under the name of Augusta. It was discovered by chance in 1747 with the discovery of the Tabula alimentaria traianea; Duke Philip of Bourbon started its excavations in 1760. You can see the hole paved in sandstone with portico on three sides and the basilica on the fourth, where there were twelve marble statues of the Julian-Claudia family today in Parma, the baths, the residential district and the cistern long believed an amphitheater; the Antiquarium, in the building wanted by Maria Luigia, exhibits pre-Roman finds, the so-called Ligurian Jupiter and the casts of Tabula and Lex rubria. Closed on Mondays; from April to October Tuesday-Thursday 9-16.30, Friday-Sunday and holidays 11-18.30.

Why go. The Roman city of Tabula alimentaria.

In winter (1 November-30 March) hole and spas are covered and not visible: call the Antiquarium to know if the covers have been removed
