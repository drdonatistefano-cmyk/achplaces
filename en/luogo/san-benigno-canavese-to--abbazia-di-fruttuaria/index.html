Abbazia di Fruttuaria

Places of worship · Abbeys and monasteries

The first stone was laid on 23 February 1003 by Guglielmo da Volpiano, in the presence of the Marquis Arduino; at its apogee, between XII and XIII century, the abbey had 85 churches in Italy and housed up to 1,200 monks. In 1770 the abbot Carlo Vittorio Amedeo delle Lanze had the church and Romanesque monastery demolished, saving only the bell tower, and the new church (1766) was designed by Vittone and Quarini between Baroque and Neoclassical. Under the floor the excavations of 1979 brought to light the foundations of the Romanesque church and a mosaic of 1066 with two griffins, which can be visited from 2004 in an underground path (fresh, cover). Closed in summer; being parish, it is advisable to telephone (338 4128795).

Why go. Two abbeys on top of each other, with the mosaic of the griffins.
