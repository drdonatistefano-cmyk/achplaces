Villaggio rupestre di Rivolta e gravina

History and architecture · Archaeology · Viewpoint

The horseshoe ravine, about 3 km of green that was the natural defense of the country during the invasions, hosts on the right side of the descent the village of Rivolta: 66 caves on five levels, considered one of the most important rock villages in Italy, with cisterns, ovens, fireplaces, gardens and palms for oil and wine, of a very high ingenuity. The first settlement was dug into the tuff and the parietal decorations are still visible. The ravine is set to "The Gospel according to Matthew of Pasolini (1964) and to "Who has seen me" (1964). Better go in spring or early autumn, morning or evening.

Why go. One of the most spectacular rock villages in Italy, with five floors of caves.
