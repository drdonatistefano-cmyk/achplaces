Colle di Santu Antine: fortezza punica e pozzo sacro

History and architecture · Archaeology · Viewpoint

Volcanic hill of about 600 meters detached from the Jar for erosion: on top the remains of a Punic fortress of the fourth century BC with six towers and a building that tradition considers a temple of Juno, from which would derive the name of the village. There is also a 39-metre deep nuragic sacred well, indicated as the deepest in Sardinia and then re-used in the Christian age, where a rare Roman-era winch was found; next to the remains of the Romanesque church of St. Helena and St. Constantine the Great, celebrated in early August with a procession of one hundred knights.

Why go. Nuragic and Punic archaeology with views from the Jar to the sea.
