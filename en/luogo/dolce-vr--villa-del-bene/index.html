Villa del Bene

History and architecture · Palaces and villas

The fifteenth-century complex was completed by 1560: Giovanni del Bene bought it from Antonio Malfatti in 1538-1539, renewed it and frescoed its interiors, and the villa hosted prelates of the Council of Trento. The monumental portal, attributed to Michele Sanmicheli, dates back to 1551; the first courtyard has the oldest building with porch and loggia, the second a crenellated dolombara tower. The frescoes, recently restored, are attributed to Domenico Brusasorzi, Nicola Crollanza and Bernardino India: pergolas with grape petti on the staircase, landscapes of the Adige and the Chiusa and Roman stories in the loggia, eight sibillas and four scenes of the Apocalypse in the salon. It is State-owned and is open on Sunday afternoons (April-September 14-19, October-March 14-18) and by reservation on other days.

Why go. A Renaissance villa frescoed on the Adige, a few steps from Verona.
