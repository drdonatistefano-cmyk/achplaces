Oratorio di San Giacomo

Places of worship · Churches and parish churches

Small Romanesque building with a rectangular plan along the ascent to the castle, almost hidden by two tall pine trees; for some scholars it dates back to the 10th century and is already mentioned at the end of the 13th century in the Liber Notitiae Sanctorum Mediolani di Goffredo da Bussero. The dedication to St. James Major suggests a pilgrimage to Santiago. The restorations of the fifties and sixties of the twentieth century brought to light frescoes from different eras: in the apse paintings byzantine with apostles, a fragment of the martyrdom of St. James, a St. John the Baptist and a Madonna with Child who blesses a devotee, probably a Visconti, of 15th century painters.

Why go. Romanesque and fifteenth century frescoes on the ascent to the castle.
