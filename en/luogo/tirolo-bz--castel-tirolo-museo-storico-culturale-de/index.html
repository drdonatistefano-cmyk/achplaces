Castel Tirolo - Museo storico-culturale della Provincia

History and architecture · Castles and fortresses · Paid entry · Viewpoint

The first nucleus dates back to about 1100; the mother house of the counts of Tyrol, which gave the name to the region, passed to the Habsburgs in 1363 and fell from the fifteenth century, when the government moved to Innsbruck; plundered and put to auction by the Bavarian occupants around 1800, it was bought by Merano and donated to the emperor Francesco I, then restored. It contains Romanesque sculptures, marble portals, protogothic frescoes, a copy of the Gothic altar (the original is in Innsbruck) and the oldest Tyrolean windows; it houses the provincial museum of history and culture, with the permanent exhibition in a renewed wing. You can only reach it on foot, 25-30 minutes from the village; closed on Mondays; not suitable for wheelchairs.
