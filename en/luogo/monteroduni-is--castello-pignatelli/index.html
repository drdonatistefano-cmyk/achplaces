Castello Pignatelli

History and architecture · Castles and fortresses · Paid entry · Viewpoint

Of probable Longobard origin, under control of the Volturno valley against Saracen incursions, it had a walls in Norman times; from 1503 to 1668 it was the D'Afvolta, then the Pignatelli, who transformed it into residence. A door with a Renaissance balcony and a staircase lead to the loggia; on the first floor the hall of representation has a large marble fireplace, a terracotta floor with the coat of arms of the Pignatelli (three 'pignate's) and a wooden ceiling of the eighteenth century painted in tempera with chivalry motifs, while a tower preserves a room covered with majolica. On the second floor it runs the way of rounds with slits and cadio; on the ground floor kitchens, ovens and cellars with large barrels. Visits on Saturday and Sunday (10:30-12:30 and 17:00-18:30) only by reservation at least 2 days before, on phone 0865 491586.

Why go. Nobile rooms and walk of patrol over the Volturno plain.
