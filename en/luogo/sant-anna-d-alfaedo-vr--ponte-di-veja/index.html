Ponte di Veja

Nature and landscape · Caves and geosites · Viewpoint

Natural arc with a light of about 40 meters, minimum thickness of 9 meters and width of 20 meters, indicated as the largest in Europe: according to one hypothesis it is what remains of an ancient karst cave (covolo) eroded by water, according to another is linked to the earthquake of 1223. Under the collapsed boulders at the base and in two caves were found remains of the Paleolithic, exposed in the nearby museums. Andrea Mantegna depicted it in the frescoes of the Chamber of the Sposi in Mantua and the tradition has it that Dante, exile to Verona guest of Cangrande della Scala, was inspired by the Malebolge. Walking paths and stairs with stone signs descend under the arch; accessible 24 hours a day, with parking at the Trattoria Ponte di Veja.

Why go. To pass under the stone arch that inspired Mantegna and, it is said, Dante.
