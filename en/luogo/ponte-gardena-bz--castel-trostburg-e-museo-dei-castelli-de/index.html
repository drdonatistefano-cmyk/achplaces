Castel Trostburg e Museo dei castelli dell'Alto Adige

History and architecture · Castles and fortresses · Paid entry · Viewpoint

Documented at least from the 12th century, it passed from the lords of Velturno to the counts of Tyrol and then to the Wolkensteins, who held it for over six centuries: among them the poet and traveller Oswald (1377-1445). The large halls of representation added between the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries give it the present form: Gothic hall with three spans, loggia with frescoes of coats of arms, painted chapel and the New Hall of the Knights (1618 circa) with wooden ceiling inlaid coffered; decisive restorations of the Südtiroler Burgeninstitut at the end of the twentieth century. The Museum of Castles exhibits about eighty scale models. Guided tour only (German or Italian, 40-50 minutes plus 15 minutes for the exhibition), from Holy Thursday to October 31, closed on Monday; Invalid Museumcard.

Why go. The Wolkenstein castle and all the castles of South Tyrol in miniature.
